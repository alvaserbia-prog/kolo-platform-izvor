This data protection impact assessment (hereinafter: DPIA) is prepared in accordance with Art. 54 of the Law on Personal Data Protection ("Official Gazette of the RS", no. 87/2018, hereinafter: LPDP). The DPIA is based on the provisions of Chapter IX of the Rulebook on the KOLO System (Art. 60–67), the Privacy Policy of the KOLO Platform, and the Records of Processing Activities of Personal Data.

The KOLO system by its nature processes personal data — the verification graph, the contribution ledger, donation data, and, in the context of certain modules, special categories of data. At the same time, the system rests on the principle of data minimization as one of its four structural principles. This DPIA covers all active processing activities in Phase 1 and identifies additional risks arising from the activation of Module 3 (Social Programs), Module 4 (Children), and the Cooperatives Module.

## 2. Systematic Description of Processing

## 2.1. System Description

The KOLO system is a platform for recording contributions to the common good, based on the principles of pseudonymity, data minimization, transparency of the ledger, and non-refundability of donations. The system operates through the KOLO Protocol — a technical means of processing that automatically records users' contributions in recording units (POEN).

The KOLO Foundation is the data controller within the meaning of the LPDP — it determines the purposes and means of processing (Art. 2 para. 1 item 8 LPDP). The Foundation is the controller even when it does not physically store users' data in its own databases — the legally relevant criterion is the determination of the purpose and means of processing, not the physical storage of data. If the Foundation engages third parties for infrastructure maintenance, those parties are data processors within the meaning of the LPDP (Art. 45), on the basis of a processing agreement.

## 2.2. Design Decisions for Data Protection

Data protection in the system is based on data protection by design and by default (Art. 50 LPDP) and rests on three design decisions:

First design decision — pseudonymity of the ledger. Records in the contribution ledger are tied to pseudonyms, not to users' personal names. The user's first and last name is kept only in the identity register (activity no. 21), separately from the contribution ledger, and is entered only in five cases: upon registering a farm and joining a farm, upon a public donation, upon acquiring the capacity of a cooperative member, upon establishment of receipt of a sponsorship — the name of the representative who submitted the application — and upon entering the call list, when it is entered by the user themselves (Art. 31a of the Rulebook on the KOLO System). It is not displayed to other users, except to a Kolo member who calls a user from the call list, by a click and only while the call is open (activity no. 24), and is not included in POEN records. Pseudonymity is not anonymity — pseudonymized data remain personal data within the meaning of the LPDP because they can, with additional information, be linked to an identified person.

Second design decision — data separation. The Foundation does not store personal data of users in its own databases — all users' data are stored on the Protocol's infrastructure. The Foundation directly stores only the banking documentation of donations and the record of the link between the legal entity sponsor and the user in whose record the contribution is entered.

Third design decision — data minimization. The Platform collects only the data necessary for the functioning of the system. Data minimization is a structural principle of the KOLO system that cannot be abolished by any governance decision.

## 2.4. Data Flow

Data in the KOLO system follow this flow:

The user registers on the Platform and enters a pseudonym, email address, and password. The password is hashed before storage. The user may undergo the proof-of-reality procedure (verification based on direct personal acquaintance of the verifier and the verified person, or on a conversation following a call), whereby the verification graph is recorded in the system in pseudonymous form, together with the datum of whether the verification is a confirmation or an introduction into the Kolo. A user who knows no Kolo member comes onto the call list by a conversation request, by a public donation of at least 5,000 dinars made in a single payment, by monetary sponsorship, or by registering a farm entered in the Register of Agricultural Holdings; once they consent to the call and leave a telephone number, the Kolo member who opens the call sees their first and last name from the identity register and the number, and the Foundation records who opened the data. The user may voluntarily enter additional data (name and surname, telephone number) for easier use of the platform.

User activity — dinar donations, reaching thresholds, operational contribution, verification of other users — is automatically recorded in the Protocol through the issuance of POENs. The ledger is pseudonymous and public for participants (members without confirmation, Kolo members and Kolo keepers). The accounting coefficient is calculated automatically, by a deterministic formula.

The Foundation directly stores the banking documentation of natural persons' donations and the sponsorship record of legal entities. These data are stored separately from platform data.

Technical data (IP address, device data, access log) are collected automatically for platform security purposes.

A user's listing contains data about the good or service offered (title, description, category, price, place, photographs) and, at the user's choice, a telephone number. The listing is publicly visible; the telephone number is accessible only to participants.

A cooperative participating in the system submits to the Foundation the first and last name and the pseudonym of cooperative members, for persons who have consented to this, and the Foundation links the account to the cooperative. The first and last name are not displayed to other users; the records of a cooperative are visible to participants under the name of the cooperative, and cooperative members under their pseudonyms.

A cross-border transfer actually takes place for images (Cloudflare R2), the email sent by the Platform (Resend), alerts to the Foundation (Telegram), and traffic measurement (Google), and is governed in accordance with Art. 65–69 LPDP and Art. 9 of the Privacy Policy.

## 3. Assessment of Necessity and Proportionality

For each processing activity it is assessed whether the processing is necessary to achieve the purpose and whether a less invasive alternative exists.

## 3.1. Processing Activities Based on the Execution of a Contractual Relationship (no. 1, 2, 4, 20, 21, 22, 23)

Registration (pseudonym, email, password) is the minimal set of data necessary for the functioning of the system. Without a pseudonym there is no ledger; without an email address there is no communication and account recovery. The password is stored exclusively in hashed form. Proof of reality (the verification graph, the type of verification, and the datum of whether the user is in the Kolo and whether they are confirmed) is necessary for ensuring the principle of one person, one account. The datum of whether a verification is a confirmation or an introduction into the Kolo, and the record of who confirmed a member without confirmation and when, are necessary in order to apply the restrictions of a member without confirmation, and so that that confirmation, like a verification, can be subject to supervision and responsibility for it can be borne as for a verification. Activity recording is the essence of the Protocol's functioning — without it the system cannot exist. Pseudonymization reduces the risk, and an alternative (anonymization) would make functioning impossible. Conclusion: the processing activities are necessary and proportionate.

A farm (no. 20) is displayed on the public map because the offer of local food, visible by place, is the purpose for which the farm joins the system; the display is an integral part of participation and a condition of the farm contribution. Only what is necessary for the display and for establishing the conditions is processed: the address is written out on the basis of the applicant's statement, the BPG is stated only when the farm is entered in the register and is not public, and members are displayed under their pseudonyms. Conclusion: the processing is necessary for the purpose and proportionate with the measures under point 5.17.

The identity register (no. 21) serves the principle of one person, one account, which the chain of confirmations cannot verify by itself: a verifier does not know how many accounts a person has. The first and last name is therefore entered where the system, by the nature of things, already obtains it or where it can be verified — in a public donation (the bank has identified the payer), for a cooperative member (the cooperative knows them), for a farm (the name is entered by the member themselves, together with the declaration of accuracy of data), for a sponsorship (the bank has identified the paying legal entity, and the representative's name is compared against the public Register of Business Entities) and for the call list (the name is entered by the user themselves, and it is compared with the names kept alongside other accounts, together with the place, in order to recognise the same person with two accounts; a match blocks nothing, no. 24). Only the first and last name is processed; the unique citizen identification number, data from identity documents, and copies of identity documents are not processed, and the entry is not a condition for proof of reality or for other functions, and never confirms reality. Outside the administrator team, the name is shown only to a Kolo member who calls a user from the call list (no. 24), so that they can check that they are speaking with the same person. A less invasive alternative — not keeping the name with the account — does not achieve the purpose, because the same person with two accounts could then not be recognized even in these cases. Conclusion: the processing is necessary for the purpose and proportionate with the measures under point 5.18.

Vouchers from the allocation (no. 22) process what is necessary for the allocation: the declaration of non-connection with the giver, the consent to the display of the record of the annulment, and the taking and redemption of the voucher. The record of the annulment, with the description of the good or service and the name of the giver, is displayed alongside the pseudonym in the ledger accessible to participants, and the giver sees the holder's pseudonym when checking. The basis for that display is the consent given when taking the voucher, or explicit consent where the description reveals data concerning health; taking a voucher is voluntary, so a user who does not want the display does not take the voucher.

Recommendations (no. 23) process only who recommended whom and when — without text, rating or reasoning. That pair is necessary so that the same Kolo member can recommend the same user only once and can withdraw the recommendation. Other users are shown only the number, so a less invasive form than the one the purpose requires does not exist.

## 3.2. Processing Activities Based on Consent (no. 3, 9, 19, 24)

Voluntarily entered data (name, surname, telephone) are optional — the user enters them only if they wish. Posting a listing constitutes consent, given with a warning that the listing is publicly visible. Consent is voluntary and may be withdrawn at any time by removing the listing, without consequences for status. The data of cooperative members (no. 19) are submitted only with the consent of the person concerned and are reduced to a minimum — first and last name, pseudonym, name of the cooperative, and the moments of linking and termination; without the first and last name the number of cooperative members cannot be reliably established, because a pseudonym does not prove membership in a cooperative. Upon withdrawal of consent the status of cooperative member terminates, while the POEN in the person's record remains theirs; the first and last name entered in the identity register (no. 21) is kept according to the rules of that activity. Conclusion: the processing activities are based on free consent and are proportionate to the purpose.

The call list (no. 24) processes the telephone number only with the explicit consent of the user and only until they enter the Kolo, and the first and last name only when it is already entered in the identity register. A less invasive form than the one the purpose requires is not available: a person who knows no one in the network can be met only if someone contacts them, and a telephone conversation is the smallest step by which acquaintance is achieved. The caller needs the name in order to check that they are speaking with the person who made the donation or submitted the sponsorship or farm application, and not with someone posing as that person. The name and number are not displayed alongside the list, but only upon opening, to one Kolo member at a time and only while the call is open. The record of who opened the data is based on legitimate interest (Art. 12 para. 1 item 6 LPDP): without it, misuse could not be established from the system; the record contains only pseudonyms and time, is seen only by the administrator team, and the Kolo member is informed of it in advance, so the interest prevails.

## 3.3. Processing Activities Based on a Statutory Obligation (no. 5)

Natural persons' donations — the Foundation is required by the Law on Accounting and tax regulations to retain records of donations for 10 years. The identity of the donor is provided through the banking system (verified bank accounts), which is an already existing infrastructure. A less invasive alternative does not exist because the law is mandatory. Conclusion: the processing is necessary and legally obligatory.

## 3.4. Processing Activities Based on Legitimate Interest (no. 6, 7, 12)

Legal entity sponsorship — the processing is necessary for the recording of sponsorship and lawful financial reporting. Proportionality test: the Foundation's interests prevail because the data are limited to the minimum necessary for recording and for a valid donation contract — the sponsor's name, registered office, registration number and PIB, and the name and function of the representative who signs the contract — and the applicant is informed in advance. Only the sponsor's name is published on the ranking list, because the registered office of an entrepreneur would often be their home address. Technical data and logs — the processing is necessary for platform security, prevention of misuse, and detection of unauthorized access. The retention period is 12 months, which is proportionate to the purpose. Conclusion: the legitimate interest is justified and proportionate in both cases.

Integrity monitoring of the verification system (no. 12) — the processing is necessary to prevent misuse of the proof-of-reality system (false and "farmed" verifications, account multiplication), which would devalue the contribution ledger and governance. No new data are collected — the processing operates on existing pseudonymous data (verification graph, ledger metadata, activity indicators). The system does not make automated decisions within the meaning of Art. 38 LPDP — it only flags accounts or groups for human review, and any measure is taken by an authorised person. Proportionality test: the interest of the Foundation and honest users in the integrity of the system prevails over the minimal intrusion, because no new data are introduced, the processing is pseudonymous, no automated decisions are made, and it is subject to human review and the right to object. Conclusion: the legitimate interest is justified and proportionate.

## 3.5. Automated Decision-Making (no. 8)

POEN issuance and calculation of the accounting coefficient are automated processing activities that may have a legal or significant effect on a person within the meaning of Art. 38 LPDP. The annulment of a parent's confirmation of reality due to inactivity in the procedure for confirming the existence of a child, which took effect upon expiry of the period and without a decision of an organ of the Foundation, has been abolished by this assessment together with that period: if confirmation of the child's existence does not arrive, nothing is taken away from the parent. There is thus no longer any automated processing that takes away a person's status. The automatic confirmation of the chain (Art. 5a of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality) only grants status, and does so under a rule published in advance; every such confirmation goes to supervision, and a chain longer than three steps is separately reported to the Foundation. The Foundation provides: a deterministically defined public formula for the accounting coefficient, the user's right to an explanation of the logic, the right to human review, and the right to object. The public nature of the formula and the deterministic approach reduce the risk of arbitrariness. Conclusion: the processing is necessary for the functioning of the system, with adequate guarantees.

## 5. Risk Mitigation Measures

## 5.1. Technical Measures

Pseudonymization of the ledger — records are tied to pseudonyms; the first and last name is kept only in the identity register, separately from the contribution ledger, subject to the measures in point 5.18; the unique citizen identification number is not processed. Data separation — identification data (pseudonym, email) are maintained in a separate record from accounting data (contribution ledger, POEN balances). Encryption of data in transit — TLS encryption minimum version 1.2 for all communications. Encryption of data at rest — encryption at the hosting infrastructure level. Ledger integrity — zero-sum invariant (sum of all balances equal to zero) with automatic verification, atomic recording and timestamping of records; deviations are visible and subject to review. Password hashing — passwords are stored exclusively in hashed form. Audit trail — administrative actions and the initiation of conversations regarding a listing are logged in the audit trail.

## 5.2. Organizational Measures

Access control based on the principle of minimum necessity — every user, administrator, and process has access only to the necessary data (Art. 51 para. 2 LPDP). Multi-factor authentication for administrative access to the infrastructure. Confidentiality obligation for all persons with access to data. Regular training of employees and associates on data protection. Regular security checks and penetration testing of the system. Defined procedure for managing security incidents with notification of the Commissioner within 72 hours (Art. 52 LPDP).

## 5.3. Measures Specific to a User's Listing

Minimization by design — publication requires a content minimum about the GOOD or SERVICE (photograph, description, category, place), not about the person; a photograph of the face, name, and surname are neither requested nor required. The telephone number is not public — it is accessible exclusively to participants. A visible mark that the advertiser is not in the Kolo — protects the other party to the exchange. The restriction to three active listings per user has been abolished by assessment v4.8.1. Removal of the listing at any time, which also withdraws consent, without a written request.

*Note on the amendment.* This version abolishes the former guarantee board and with it the processing of the "recognition card" (name, surname, year of birth, place, nickname, description of occupation, telephone number, and consent to being called) — the most sensitive processing of personal data the system had. All data collected through it have been deleted. The amendment removes processing; risk R5 thereby falls from 6 to 4.

## 5.4. Measures for Automated Decision-Making

Deterministically defined public formula for the accounting coefficient — every user can verify the calculation logic. The user's right to an explanation of the processing logic. Right to human review — the user may request that the decision be reconsidered by an authorized person. Right to object in accordance with Art. 38 LPDP.

## 5.5. Measures for Backup and Recovery

Data are regularly backed up to geographically separate locations. Backup includes the Protocol's ledger, identification data, and system configuration. Recovery procedures are regularly tested. Backup data are subject to the same protection measures as primary data — encryption, access control, access logging.

## 5.6. Measures for Social Programs (Module 3 — Verifier Confirmation)

Explicit consent — the application to the program and the request for confirmation from verifiers are initiated exclusively with the applicant's explicit consent. Consent is requested before confirmation is requested from anyone and expressly states: how many persons will be asked; that those persons learn which program is concerned and that this may reveal their special-category membership; that the recording of POEN under the program is displayed to participants with the pseudonym, the name of the program and the amount, and to persons who are not signed in and to users only as a daily total per program; what can be derived from the amount in support to the elderly and support to mothers; and that the ground on which the entitlement was established is not displayed to any user. Minimization toward verifiers — verifiers are not shown the content of the application (children's dates of birth, disability decision, age); they confirm exclusively on the basis of personal acquaintance with the applicant. Limited circle of recipients in the procedure — the request for confirmation is received only by the applicant's own verifier network (persons who already know them personally), not the broader community. Special categories only with the application processor — the entered data are accessible exclusively to the person at the Foundation processing the application, who alone is authorized to decide on it. No external channels — the notification of a request for confirmation is displayed exclusively within the platform; the message the platform sends by electronic mail or to the user's device contains neither the name of the program nor the applicant's pseudonym. Hard block and responsibility — the application is not approved until the fulfilment of the conditions is confirmed under full responsibility by a Kolo member, or founder, who knows the applicant personally — their verifier, if they confirmed them, or another Kolo member who knows them personally — or by the Foundation (where there are more verifiers, confirmation by one is sufficient); a Kolo member who merely introduced the applicant into the Kolo after a telephone conversation, and a member without confirmation, do not confirm the fulfilment of the conditions; a refusal requires a reason. Withdrawal of consent — implemented as an action available to the applicant alongside the program itself, at any time and without stating reasons; withdrawal terminates the procedure, ends the recording, and deletes the entered data. Deletion of entered data upon termination of the application — the data entered in the application are also deleted when the application is refused, suspended at the review of status, or when the user's status ceases; a new application requires re-entry and fresh confirmation by the verifier. Closing the procedure — once the procedure has ended, the confirmation requests and the notifications about them are removed from the verifiers' accounts. Proof of disability status is a decision of the competent authority — medical documentation and diagnosis are not collected (minimization of special categories).

Graduated display of the individual record and omission of the ground — the individual recording of a contribution under a social programme is displayed to participants, together with the pseudonym, the name of the program, and the amount. Persons who are not signed in and users are shown a daily total per program (the number of support recipients and the total amount), and a day on which, within one program, a contribution was recorded for only one support recipient is not published, because the total would then equal the individual amount. The load-bearing measure is no longer the omission of the record but **the omission of the ground**: the ground on which the entitlement within the program was established is not displayed to any user, so the published name of the Special Support programme — which covers both reduced capacity and loss of home — does not state which ground is concerned. In the case of support to the elderly and support to mothers the amount is derived from the data on the basis of which the entitlement was established (the support recipient's year of birth, or the number and ages of the children) and discloses them; the amount is nevertheless displayed, because the verifiability of the ledger towards the community has been chosen as the prevailing interest, and upon application the applicant expressly consents also to what may be derived from the amount. The description of the record still does not name the program — the name is derived from the application, not from the description, which is permanent and forms part of the data export. A breakdown by ground is available to the support recipient themselves, alongside the program, and to the person processing the application within the Foundation.

Parental consent for data concerning a child — under support to mothers, the date of birth of a child who is not themselves a user of the Platform is processed. The legal basis is the consent of the parent or legal guardian (Art. 16 LPDP), given by the applicant themselves; only the date of birth is processed, without the child's name and without any other data concerning them, and it is erased when the application ceases to be valid.

Accepted consequence — membership in a program remains known to the applicant's own verifiers even after the application ends. This cannot be removed without abolishing the confirmation mechanism itself, which protects the integrity of the programs against untrue applications. The mitigation lies in the fact that consent is given after that consequence has been expressly communicated, together with the only alternative — that the user does not apply for the program.

## 5.7. Measures for Integrity Monitoring of Verifications

No collection of new data — monitoring operates exclusively on data that are already lawfully processed (verification graph, POEN ledger metadata, activity indicators), in pseudonymous form. No automated decision-making — the system does not take any action on an account; it exclusively flags accounts or groups and a numerical risk score for review, and every measure is taken by an authorised person (superadministrator). Emphasis on hollowness, not density — the rules prioritise the absence of genuine activity, so that a close-knit real-world community is not wrongly treated as misuse. Ability to dismiss — the reviewer may mark a finding as unfounded, whereupon the account is not re-flagged for a defined period. Right to object — the user may contest a measure in accordance with Art. 37 LPDP. Audit trail — all actions taken on findings are recorded in the audit trail. Restricted access — findings are accessible exclusively to superadministrators; the alert channel contains only aggregate numbers, without personal data. Retention period — resolved findings are retained for at most 12 months, then deleted. Closed group check — the recognition of members without confirmation who have introduced one another into the Kolo and exchange only among themselves, without exchange with a Kolo member — operates on the same data and under the same measures: it produces no effect towards a user, but only displays the group to the Foundation, which may invite the members of the group to a conversation; the conversation is not a measure, and a measure, if needed, is taken under the rules that apply to every finding.

## 5.9. Measures for the Supervision Case

No new data from users — a case arises from the supervisor's assessment of a record that is already lawfully processed. Closed list of reasons — the reason is selected from a predetermined list, and free text is possible only with the reason "other", which prevents arbitrary observations about a person from being entered. No conflict of interest — supervision may not be performed by anyone who took part in the verification, on either side, nor may the same supervisor act twice on the same record. No decision-making — the case is a record, not a body; a false verification is established solely by the Management Board, so there is no automated decision-making within the meaning of Art. 38 LPDP. Narrow circle of recipients — the case is seen only by a superadministrator; the verifier and the verified person do not see it. Deletion by time limit — a suspicion that was not confirmed is deleted 90 days after the case is closed, automatically and without a request from the data subject. Audit log — the opening and every resolution of a case are recorded. Right to object — Art. 37 LPDP and objection to a decision under the Terms of Use.

## 5.10. Measures for the Exchange Contribution Path

Ceased by assessment v4.8.0, together with activity no. 15 and risk R15.

## 5.11. Measures for the Children's Space (Module 4 — Children)

**Minimization in the period before consent** — until the account is taken over by a parent, the pseudonym, the data necessary for access to the account, the parent's email address, and the records of friendships formed in that period are processed; the date of birth is not requested from the child but entered by the parent, and is not changed thereafter. **Functions closed in that period** — the Chat Room, listings, communication with adults, and the recording of POEN are unavailable until the account is taken over, so the minor user is not exposed to adults in that period; a friendship is formed solely by scanning a code in person, with another minor user. **The minor user's own way out** — a minor user whose account is awaiting takeover may delete it themselves at any time, without the parent's involvement and without addressing the Foundation. **The period as a measure** — an account that nobody takes over within fourteen days is deleted without a request from the person. **No identifying data in the message to a third party** — the invitation to the parent states only the pseudonym, never the name, because the accuracy of the address is not verified. **Two exits without login** — the recipient of the message may declare that they are not the child's parent and may delete the account. **Indirect but real verification of the parent and of parenthood** — full access and the recording of contributions (Kolo child) open only once the parent is a Kolo member (and not a member without confirmation) and the child's existence has been confirmed by a Kolo member who knows the parent personally. **Three states of a child** — waiting child (the parent has not taken over the account), child with a parent (the parent has taken over the account but is not a Kolo member, being a user or a member without confirmation, or the child's existence has not been confirmed) and Kolo child; until reaching the state of a Kolo child, the child does not post listings, does not transfer POEN, does not write to those who are not their friends, does not enter the overview by school, and does not see the overview of the school's children. **Narrowed parental insight** — conversations between minor users are not shown to the parent; friendships with dates and conversations without content are shown. **Notification of the adult party** — in a conversation with a minor user, the adult user is shown that the conversation is read by a parent. **Filtering of the children's Chat Room by the friendship graph** — a minor user sees the messages of their friends, and replying by quoting another's message is not supported so that the filter cannot be circumvented. **Non-publicity of the graph** — friendships are not public and do not enter public aggregates. **No automated decision-making** — the recording of a contribution is an application of publicly published rules and does not affect the user's status (Art. 38 LPDP). **Closed profile of a minor user** — adult users are shown a notice with the parent's pseudonym instead of the profile; parental consent does not open the profile but governs communication and exchange. **Aggregate nature of the overview by school** — only numbers and shares are published publicly, with no personal data; the individual overview of a single school covers only Kolo children, is available only to a Kolo child of that school and gives no access to the profile. **No history of school choices** — only the current value and the moment of the last change are kept, with a thirty-day interval between two changes. **Confirmation of the minor user's own address** — an address stated by a minor user is recorded only after they confirm it by opening a link sent to that address; a typing error therefore does not hand access to the account to a third party. **Purpose narrower than the channel** — no notifications are sent to a minor user's address; it serves only for setting a new password. **A way out even without an address** — where a minor user has no address of their own, the parent sets the new password, with notice to the minor user, so a forgotten password does not mean the loss of the account. **Balancing test for processing on the basis of legitimate interest** — the processing of the parent's email address and of the minor user's data in the period before the account is taken over rests on legitimate interest (Art. 12 para. 1 item 6 LPDP). The interest is obtaining the consent required by law: without contact with the parent the consent cannot even be requested, so without this processing the minor cannot be given lawful access. The processing is necessary because no less intrusive means exists — the parent's address is the only datum the minor has, and without a record of the account itself the invitation could not be tied to anyone. The interests and rights of the minor prevailed where they could: the scope is reduced to what is necessary, the period is limited to fourteen days, the functions that would expose the minor user to adults are closed, the message to the parent contains only the pseudonym, the recipient of the message has two ways out without login, and the minor user has their own. The expectations of the data subject are respected in that the processing is initiated only by the minor, by entering their parent's address, and that its only outcome is a message to that parent. **Accepted consequence of deletion without login** — the account may be deleted without logging in, on the basis of the one-time authorization from the message to the parent, so whoever holds the message may delete the account even when they are not that child's parent. The consequence is accepted: the alternative is that the account continues to use the address of a person unconnected with the minor user, and the only person the system can ask at that moment is the one holding the message.


**Confirmation of the child's existence by a Kolo member who knows the parent personally** — the confirmation is requested by a parent who is a Kolo member, or a founder; it is given only by a Kolo member, or founder, who knows the parent personally, and not by the other parent or by a member without confirmation. The confirmation brings no POEN, and responsibility for a false confirmation is borne as for a false verification. **Minimal notice** — the request is displayed to all Kolo members only with the parent's pseudonym, without the child's pseudonym, age, gender and school. **No loss due to absence** — if the confirmation does not arrive, nothing is taken away from the parent: the sixty-day period for a declaration, the annulment of the parent's confirmation of reality due to inactivity with the write-off under it, and the procedure in which the existence of the child is confirmed by the Foundation have been abolished by this assessment. **Notice to the parent of a Kolo child** — when a Kolo child befriends a child who is not a Kolo child, the parent of the Kolo child receives a notice that the friendship has been formed; they do not read the content of conversations between the children. **Notice of account take-over** — the minor user receives a notice every time someone takes over their account, with the pseudonym of the person who took it over. **Report of false parenthood** — the minor user, the other parent or any other user may report that the person who opened or took over the account is not that child's parent; upon the report the Foundation immediately suspends the link temporarily, so that while the check lasts the person has neither insight nor the powers of a parent; the report is decided on by an authorised person of the Foundation, who terminates the link upon establishing that the person is not the parent, whereupon the place of the parent is freed; the person to whom the report relates is not told who submitted it; if after termination of the link the child remains without a parent, the account is not deleted but passes into the waiting child state and the fourteen-day period for take-over runs anew; a false declaration of parenthood mandatorily entails exclusion, and a complaint may be lodged against the decision (Art. 4e of the Rulebook on the Participation of Children; Art. 28 and 37a of the Terms of Use).

## 5.12. Measures for Collective Procurement

**Minimisation of the proposal** — a procurement proposal contains only the name of a good; quantity, price, reasoning, and any other data are not requested. **Aggregate publication** — the register of proposals is published without pseudonyms; an individual proposal is not published. **No proof of circumstances** — apart from verification, no eligibility criteria are set, so no documents or statements about a user's occupation, property, or circumstances are collected. **No new collection** — the number of POEN by which the order is established is read from activity No. 4. **The supplier receives no personal data** — solely a list of collection codes is provided to it. **Narrowed publication of the outcome** — the pseudonym and position in the order are published alongside the list of those who collected a share, but not the number of POEN in the record. **No automated decision-making** — establishing the order is the application of publicly published rules and produces no consequences for user status (Art. 38 of the LPDP), with an express right of objection decided upon by a human being. **Deletion** — a proposal is deleted by choosing another name, by removal, by the carrying out of a procurement for that name, and at the latest upon the expiry of twelve months.

## 5.13. Measures for Cross-Border Transfer

**Processing in the European Union** — the application is executed and the database is located in the Frankfurt region. The Protocol's ledger, the data from applications for support programmes, messages between users, and the data of minor users therefore remain within a circle with an adequate level of protection. **A narrow scope of what leaves** — only the images uploaded by users, the email sent by the Platform, the alerts sent to the Foundation, and traffic data go to a third country. **The alert channel is reduced to a minimum** — an alert to the Foundation contains the pseudonym and the type of event, and contains neither the data entered in an application for a support programme nor the content of users' messages. **An agreement as a condition of transfer** — data are transferred exclusively to a processor with which a processing agreement containing standard contractual clauses or other safeguards under Art. 65 LPDP has been concluded. **Annual verification** — the Foundation keeps a copy of each such agreement and verifies at least once a year whether it is in force and whether the list of sub-processors has changed. **Traffic measurement upon consent** — Google Analytics is not loaded until the visitor consents, and cookieless measurement sets no cookies and does not track the visitor across websites.

## 5.14. Measures for Termination of Status and the Right to Erasure

An accurately named procedure — upon termination of status the account is pseudonymized, not anonymized; the retained records remain personal data, and the limitation on erasure rests on Art. 30 para. 3 LPDP (statutory retention obligation and legal claim), not on a claim that the data have ceased to be personal. Deletion of content — the email address, voluntarily entered data, the content and photographs of listings (the listings are removed from the advertising space, the photographs from storage), and the free text entered alongside re-registrations of POEN are deleted. Deletion of messages — messages in a conversation are deleted when at least one party closes their account. An explicit list of what remains — the numerical records alongside a pseudonym that does not point to a name and the basis on which the POEN was recorded; the scope is enumerated in Art. 11 of the Policy instead of being left implied. The name alongside a public donation is removed from the list and deleted from the donation record, while the donation remains in the list with its amount and date. The right to rectification — a request to rectify inaccurate data is submitted within the Platform, as an appeal of the data-correction type. Verification of retention periods — the Foundation verifies at least once a year whether the retention period has expired for any category of data.

Withdrawal of the name alongside a public donation — consent to the publication of the name may be withdrawn at any time, separately for each donation, on the donations screen, without a written request. Until this assessment the name remained in the list even after the termination of status, on the reasoning that without the name the recording of POEN could not be attributed to any person. That reasoning holds for the moment of recording, when the basis of the recording is verified, and not afterwards: after withdrawal the donation remains attributed to the donor's account, and the Foundation keeps the payment details. A permanently public name was not necessary for the purpose, and consent that cannot be withdrawn is not free.

## 5.15. Measures for Proof of Consent and Proof that a Contractual Relationship Was Established

Record of consent — for every processing whose legal basis is consent, as well as for the acceptance of the Terms of Use by which the contractual relationship is established, the moment, the version of the act, and the text displayed to the individual are recorded, in the language of display. Until this assessment no such record existed: the confirmation boxes at registration were checked solely in the browser, so it could be demonstrated neither that consent had been given nor that the contractual relationship had been established, and that relationship is the legal basis of most of the processing set out in Section 3.

Verification on the controller's side — an account is not created without a recorded acceptance, and the check is performed on the server, not in the browser. The record is created in the same atomic operation as the account, so an account without a record cannot come into being even if the write is interrupted.

Separation of statements — acceptance of the Terms of Use and acceptance of the Privacy Policy are recorded as two separate statements, because they relate to different matters (Art. 15 paragraph 2 of the LPDP).

Immutability of the text to which the consent relates — a published version of an act is not changed; an amended act receives a new designation and is published as a separate document. The record of consent therefore permanently refers to the text in force at the moment it was given.

Explicit consent for special categories — alongside an application for a social programme, the text of the consent itself is recorded, not merely the fact that it was given. The text contains the number of persons who will be asked to confirm, which differs for each applicant, so without a snapshot it could not be established what the particular individual consented to.

Separation of the parent's consent from the request for confirmation of the child's existence — the consent to the processing of the child's data is recorded separately from the request by which the parent asserts that the child exists. The request is an assertion of fact confirmed by a Kolo member who knows the parent personally; the consent may be withdrawn at any time and without consequences for anyone else. While they stood in a single text, withdrawal of the consent could not be carried out without also overturning the assertion of the child's existence.

No new data for the purpose of proving — alongside the record of consent, no IP address, device data, or other identifiers are recorded. For consent to non-essential cookies, no identifier is established for a visitor who is not logged in; the decision is recorded in the cookie itself, together with the version of the notice under which it was made, and a record alongside an account is kept only for a logged-in user, for whom an account already exists.

Renewal of the decision upon amendment — a decision made under an earlier version of the cookie notice is not carried over to the new one; the individual is asked again.

Withdrawal and access — consent is withdrawn as easily as it was given, the record of the consent given is not deleted in the process but the moment of withdrawal is recorded in it, and the individual sees in their account and in the data export which consents they have given and by which text.

Confirmation of the email address — a message with a confirmation link is sent to the address entered at registration. Confirmation is not a condition for using the account; it establishes that the address belongs to the individual who created the account, whereby the record of consent ceases to attest to the account alone.

## 5.16. Measures for the Cooperatives Module

**Minimization** — only the first and last name, the pseudonym, the name of the cooperative, and the moments of linking and termination of the status are processed for a cooperative member; identity documents, address, personal identification number, and data on membership in the cooperative are not requested. **Consent** — the datum is submitted only for persons who have consented to this; upon withdrawal of consent the first and last name entered alongside membership in the cooperative are deleted (the entry in the identity register is kept according to point 5.18), and the status of cooperative member terminates, without consequences for the POEN in the person's record. **No display** — the first and last name are not displayed to other users and are not included in any public display or aggregate; the records of a cooperative are displayed to participants under the name of the cooperative, and cooperative members under their pseudonyms. **Restricted access** — the datum is accessible only to the administrator team; administrative access requires multi-factor authentication. **No minors** — the actions under the Rulebook on Cooperatives are not carried out with the account of a minor user. **No new role in the chain of confirmations** — a cooperative does not confirm the reality of a user, so the verification graph (activity no. 2) remains unchanged. **Retention periods** — the first and last name entered alongside membership in the cooperative are deleted upon termination of the status of cooperative member, upon withdrawal of consent, or upon closure of the account, while the entry in the identity register is kept according to point 5.18; the link between the account and the cooperative is retained under the pseudonym, for the purpose of verifying growth thresholds already recorded. **Access** — the datum is included in the user's data export.

## 5.17. Measures for Farms and the Map of Local Producers

**Minimization** — the name, settlement, exact location, description of production, role of the applicant, and pseudonyms of members are processed; the BPG is stated when the farm is entered in the register and is not checked in the register; the registration of a farm does not confirm the reality of the holder, but only brings the user onto the call list (point 5.21); the telephone number is taken only from the holder, or from the applicant where the holder has no account on the platform, for the conversation before approval of the farm; the first and last name of members is kept in the identity register (point 5.18), not alongside the farm. **No extract from the register** — the extract from the Register of Agricultural Holdings is not attached to the application (Art. 4b of the Rulebook on Farms), so the Foundation does not receive data from the extract that are not needed for the purpose. **No written-out address** — the farm is displayed as a point on the map, without the street and house number in the text. **Adults only** — the account of a minor user cannot be a member of a farm. **Review before display** — the registration and every subsequent joining are reviewed by the Foundation; a member is displayed with the farm only upon approval. **Deletion** — upon withdrawal and closure of the account a member ceases to be displayed; upon annulment of approval or termination of the farm the point is removed, and the name, description, location, and BPG are deleted. **Access** — the BPG and the declaration of accuracy are accessible only to the administrator team; administrator actions are recorded in the audit trail. **Address on the basis of the applicant's statement** — the address is a mandatory part of the application and is stored with it; it is written out alongside the point on the map on the basis of the applicant's statement given in the application, the display ceases upon deletion of the farm from the platform, and for farms approved earlier the address is not written out until the applicant gives the statement; it is deleted together with the other farm data; to find the point, the query goes from the applicant's browser to the OpenStreetMap address search service.

## 5.18. Measures for the Identity Register

**Closed set of cases** — the first and last name is entered only upon registering a farm and joining a farm, upon a public donation, upon acquiring the capacity of a cooperative member, upon establishment of receipt of a sponsorship and upon entering the call list (Art. 31a of the Rulebook on the KOLO System); any extension requires an amendment of the Rulebook and of this assessment. **Minimization** — only the first and last name, the pseudonym, the case of entry, and the time are processed; the unique citizen identification number, the identity card number, data from identity documents, and copies of identity documents are not processed; the comparison against the Register of Business Entities is recorded only as a fact, and an extract from the register of agricultural holdings is kept only until the decision on the farm application and does not enter the identity register (point 5.17). **Separation** — the name is not entered in the contribution ledger, in the description of a POEN record, in notifications, or in the audit trail. **No display** — the name does not appear in any display to other users, nor alongside a farm, a cooperative, or a listing; the only exception is a Kolo member who calls a user from the call list, to whom the name is opened by a click, only while the call is open and with recording (point 5.21); the safeguard is a test that reads the source code. **Entry does not confirm reality** — entry is not verification; reality is confirmed only in the chain of confirmations. **One name per account** — a different name obtained later does not overwrite the one entered; correction upon a complaint is carried out by the superadministrator. **Restricted access** — accessible only to the administrator team; the person proposed by a cooperative signs a statement of confidentiality; administrative access requires multi-factor authentication. **No minors** — the register is not kept for a minor user. **No retroactive entry** — existing names are not transferred. **Retention periods** — deleted upon closure of the account, that is, upon pseudonymization upon termination of status. **Access** — the datum is included in the user's data export.

## 5.19. Measures for Vouchers from the Allocation of Goods and Services

**Consent before taking** — before taking a voucher, the user gives consent to the display of the record with the description, with an express warning that the display may reveal data about them, including data concerning health; without consent the voucher is not taken, and taking is voluntary. **Pseudonym, not name** — the display is alongside the pseudonym, and the name from the identity register is never displayed. **Circle that sees the ledger** — the record is visible only to users to whom the ledger is accessible under the Rulebook. **Confirmations only by participants** — the giver's confirmations may be given only by participants designated by the giver. **Named and non-transferable voucher** — prevents trading in vouchers. **Photograph of the price list** — visible only to the administrator team. **Retention period** — ten years from the expiry of the voucher's validity period, together with the accounting documentation.

## 5.20. Measures for Recommendations

**Only the number** — the profile displays exclusively the number of recommendations received; who recommended whom is not displayed to anyone other than the Kolo member who gave the recommendation. **Only positive** — a recommendation cannot be given in negative form, so the system keeps no unfavourable judgement about anyone. **No consequences** — a recommendation produces no POEN record and does not affect status, access to functions, verification or the display of listings. **One per pair, with withdrawal** — the same Kolo member recommends the same user once and may withdraw the recommendation at any time. **No children** — a minor user neither gives nor receives recommendations. **No recommendation by a member without confirmation** — a member without confirmation does not give recommendations. **Deletion** — recommendations are deleted upon closure of the account of the person who gave them, or of the person who received them.

## 5.21. Measures for the Call List and the Extended Access of an User

**Name and number hidden until opened** — the list is displayed without the name and number; the first and last name from the identity register and the number are opened by a click, only to the Kolo member who opened the call and only while the call is open; a call is open with one Kolo member at a time, and one Kolo member holds at most one open call at a time. **Recording of who opened** — the Foundation records which Kolo member opened the data and when; the record contains only pseudonyms and time, is seen only by the administrator team, is kept as long as the account of the user whom the Kolo member called exists, and is deleted upon closure of that account, or upon pseudonymization; it is not exported through the Platform, and the user obtains it upon a request sent by email to privatnost@ekolo.rs. The period is tied to the account of the person called, because the record serves to establish misuse of their data while they participate in the system; a Kolo member who calls them and introduces them into the Kolo is at the same time their verifier, so in that case is known to them anyway, and the user learns upon request who opened the call without verification. Until this assessment such a record was not kept, by decision of the controller; it is introduced because a Kolo member now also sees the name alongside the number, and donors, sponsors and farm holders also come onto the list. **Narrower circle of recipients** — the list is seen only by Kolo members, whose reality has been confirmed and who are responsible for misuse under the Terms of Use (Art. 24); members without confirmation do not see it. **Advance notice and consent** — before coming onto the list, the user is informed that a Kolo member will call them and that this member will see their name and number; they leave the telephone number themselves, when consenting to the call, and may withdraw consent without consequences for the donation, sponsorship, farm and the contribution recorded on their basis. **No new collection of the name** — the name is taken from the identity register and is not requested from the user anew. **Request for confirmation** — the request of a member without confirmation for a conversation for confirmation is seen and opened only by Kolo keepers; nothing is collected with it except the data that are also collected for a conversation request and the datum that the applicant has inscribed ZRNO, which determines only the order on the list and the deadline for the call. **Short retention period** — the user is removed from the list, and the number deleted, upon verification, or upon confirmation of the member without confirmation, upon withdrawal of consent or of the request, and upon termination of status. **No transfer** — the name and number do not enter notifications, email or the audit trail. **Adults' space** — the children's regime remains available by a declaration of age and offers a child more than an adult's account; a conversation between an adult and a minor still requires parental consent; content in the common space is subject to moderation and reporting. Risk R25 has been accepted by decision of the controller as a consequence of opening exchange to users.

**Conversation with the farm holder** — the telephone number of the farm holder (activity no. 20) is seen only by the administrator team, is used only for the conversation before approval of the farm, and is deleted upon the decision on the application; the Foundation does not hold conversations with the other members of the farm on that basis and does not collect their number. The holder leaves the number for the call list separately, when consenting to the call.

## 8.2. Module 4 — Children

This assessment **activates** Module 4, together with the Rulebook on the Participation of Children. The system processes data of minors (Art. 16 LPDP) on the basis of the consent of a parent or legal guardian, with additional restrictions for persons under fifteen years of age.

**Two entry paths and a window before consent.** A minor user's account is opened by a parent from their own account, or by the minor themselves. In the latter case there is a window in which data are processed BEFORE parental consent has been obtained. The period is deliberately reduced to a minimum: the pseudonym, the data necessary for access to the account, the parent's email address, and the records of friendships formed in that period are processed; the Chat Room, listings, and communication with adults are closed, no contribution is recorded, the minor user may delete the account themselves, and an account that nobody takes over within fourteen days is deleted. Processing in that period rests on legitimate interest (Art. 12 para. 1 item 6 LPDP), with the balancing test in section 5.11. The fourteen-day period is a protective measure, not an operational convenience.

**The parent's address as third-party data.** The address is entered by the minor and its accuracy is not verified. It is processed on the basis of legitimate interest (Art. 12(1)(6) LPDP) for the purpose of obtaining the consent required by law. Proportionality test: the interest prevails because the processing is reduced to a single message, the message states only the pseudonym (never the name), the address is not recorded as the address of the minor user's account and opens no path to login, and the recipient of the message has two actions that stop the processing — a declaration that they are not the child's parent, and deletion of the account, both without login.

**Consent is stronger than the reasonable-effort standard.** A minor user's account gains full access and the recording of contributions (Kolo child) only once the parent is a Kolo member (and not a member without confirmation) and the child's existence has been confirmed by a Kolo member who knows the parent personally — that is, once both the parent and the existence of the child have been confirmed by third parties in the real world. Common practice is satisfied by pressing a button in a message; here that check is indirect but real.

**Parental insight is NARROWED compared with the previous version.** The parent does not read conversations between minor users, because such insight also touches another child whose parent has not granted it; they see the list of friendships and the list of conversations without content. The parent does read a conversation with an adult user, and this is expressly shown to the adult party. This is the only amendment in the history of this assessment that **removes** processing rather than adding it.

**A new recording channel.** A friendship between minor users records a contribution (Art. 15, item 9 of the Rulebook). The recording is an application of publicly published rules and produces no consequences for the user's status, so there is no automated decision-making within the meaning of Art. 38 LPDP.

**Confirmation of the child's existence (amended by this assessment).** The existence of a child is confirmed by a Kolo member who knows the parent personally, and the request is seen only by Kolo members and founders, with the parent's pseudonym; members without confirmation do not see the request. The sixty-day period, the annulment of the parent's confirmation of reality due to inactivity, and the procedure in which the existence of the child is confirmed by the Foundation have been abolished, and with them ceases the only automated processing that touched a person's status (point 3.5). The parent of a Kolo child receives a notice when the child befriends a child who is not a Kolo child. The measures are in point 5.11.

**Report of false parenthood (introduced by this assessment).** A child's account is opened or taken over by an adult user on the basis of their own declaration that they are the parent, so a person who is not the parent may gain insight into the child's profile and into the child's conversations with adults. A notice to the minor user of every take-over of the account, with the pseudonym of the person who took it over, is introduced, together with the report of false parenthood, upon which the link is immediately suspended pending a decision of an authorised person of the Foundation (Art. 4e of the Rulebook on the Participation of Children). Only the report, the suspension and the decision are processed, on the basis of legitimate interest (protection of the child). The decision is made by a human, so there is no automated decision-making within the meaning of Art. 38 LPDP. The measures are in point 5.11.

Additional risks: minors are a particularly vulnerable category; the friendship graph reveals the child's circle of acquaintances; the window before consent. The risk is described as **R16**, and the measures in point **5.11**. The next amendment of this assessment is required upon any amendment of the rules of the children's space.

## 8.9. Prevention of Multiple Accounts (assessment v4.8.4)

By assessment v4.8.4 the verifier no longer guarantees uniqueness: they confirm reality and continuity and declare that they are not aware that the verified person has another account. Instead, for persons who come onto the call list a comparison is introduced: the first and last name (identity register, the fifth case of entry under Art. 31a of the Rulebook on the KOLO System), place and telephone number become a condition for the list, are kept as long as the account exists and are compared with the data kept alongside other accounts (activities no. 21 and 24). The comparison blocks nothing; the Kolo member who opens the call sees only an indication of a match and the place of the other account, while both accounts are seen only by the administrator team.

Where the Foundation establishes that two accounts are accounts of the same person (Art. 4b of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality), the ceasing account is closed, and the net ledger updates from the ceasing account are written off from the account that remains — the eighth ground for a negative record (Art. 14 para. 3 item 8 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System). Together with the establishment, a single supervision case is opened concerning the recipients of ledger updates from the ceasing account, with the sole question of whether the recipient is the same person or whether the ledger updates were fictitious; the case has no effect on the recipients' accounts, the recipient is not notified until a measure is imposed on them, and it is not monitoring of ledger updates. The decision is taken by a human, with a statement of reasons and a complaint, so there is no automated decision-making within the meaning of Art. 38 of the LPDP.

The risk of wrongly flagging namesakes and members of a household has been assessed within risk R24; no new risk is introduced, and the number of active processing activities does not change (activity no. 24 has been extended, no new one arises). The statement on the origin of funds accompanying a donation is no longer collected (activity no. 5), which reduces the scope of processing.
