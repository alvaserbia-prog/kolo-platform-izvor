# Rulebook on the KOLO System

*KOLO is a mutual support system based on the idea that people can record what they contribute to each other and to the community. This Rulebook governs how that system works: who its actors are, what the common good they collectively build consists of, how contributions are recorded, and how decisions are made. The Rulebook is a binding act whose provisions govern the rights and obligations of users.*

## Chapter I — General Provisions

### Article 1

This Rulebook governs the fundamental concepts, structure, and operating rules of the system, including:

— the common good as the purpose of the system — its concept, content, licensing and rules of contribution — and the actors and instruments of the system: the KOLO Foundation, the KOLO Protocol, and the KOLO Community;

— POEN and the contribution ledger;

— ZRNO, the accounting coefficient, and the accounting framework of the system;

— user statuses and proof of reality;

— contribution to the common good — founding, financial, operational contribution, and sponsorship;

— development phases of the system and system governance;

— system modules;

— transparency and protection of personal data;

— the legal nature and structural limits of the KOLO system;

— liability and risks of users, and dispute resolution;

— amendments to this Rulebook and the system of derived acts.

This Rulebook governs the listed areas at the framework level. Detailed parameters, tables, procedures, and operational mechanics are established by special rulebooks that reference this act.

Rules on access to the platform, use of the goods and services exchange space, and other matters of technical platform use are governed by the Terms of Use.

### Article 2

Individual terms in this Rulebook have the following meanings:

**KOLO system** — a participatory common-good system; the functional whole comprising the common good, the actors and instruments of the system, and users, organized for the purpose of recording contributions and participation in the common good. The KOLO system is not a legal entity nor a special legal category.

**Common good** — the collective good of all users; the purpose of the KOLO system. It comprises the system software, system rules, the contribution and participation ledger, and content created in the system.

**KOLO Foundation (Foundation)** — a non-profit private legal entity established under the Law on Endowments and Foundations, headquartered in Sombor; the legal instrument of the system, which safeguards the common good but is not its owner.

**KOLO Protocol (Protocol)** — the technical mechanism of the common good that maintains the ledger, calculates the accounting coefficient, and applies the system rules; it has no legal personality.

**KOLO Community (Community)** — a descriptive term for the totality of system users; it is not a legal entity and does not make decisions.

**Core system** — the minimal set of elements of the KOLO system that functions from the outset; comprises the common good, the Protocol, the Foundation, the Community, users, POEN, ZRNO, the accounting coefficient, proof of reality, and the contribution recording channels.

**Module** — an extension that adds functionality to the core system without changing it; activated according to its own prerequisites.

**POEN** — the internal accounting unit of the system; a record in the Protocol's ledger by which contributions and other forms of participation in the common good are recorded.

**ZRNO** — the internal accounting unit of the system by which a user's position in the common good is recorded in the Protocol's ledger.

**Accounting coefficient** — the numerical ratio of the total number of recorded POENs to the number of ZRNOs available for inscription in the Protocol.

**Accounting period** — the time interval at the end of which the Protocol calculates the accounting coefficient and applies the rules for ZRNO inscription and write-off, and changes in ZRNO status.

**Zero-sum invariant** — the property of the system whereby the sum of all POEN records, including the Protocol's record, equals zero at all times.

**Proof of reality** — a model of user verification based on personal acquaintance or on a conversation following a call, which confirms the reality and continuity of a user. Uniqueness — one person, one account — is not confirmed by verification: it is a prohibition under the Terms of Use, and a multiple account is established by the Foundation under the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

**Chain of confirmations** — the mechanism of proof of reality in which Kolo members confirm the reality of users whom they know personally, or introduce into the Kolo users from the call list after a telephone conversation, while members without confirmation introduce into the Kolo users whom they know personally.

**Confirmation** — a verification by which a Kolo member, or a founder, confirms the reality of a user whom they know personally, in accordance with the Rulebook on Proof of Reality. The act by which a Kolo member, or a founder, confirms a member without confirmation whom they know personally is also called a confirmation, as is the act by which a Kolo keeper confirms them after a telephone conversation; it is not a new verification (Article 28a).

**Introduction into the Kolo** — a verification by which a Kolo member, after a telephone conversation, introduces into the Kolo a user from the call list, or by which a member without confirmation introduces into the Kolo a user whom they know personally. Introduction into the Kolo is a verification within the meaning of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality; responsibility for truthfulness and the recording of POEN are the same as for a confirmation.

**User** — any person with an account on the platform, regardless of status. This is also a service user within the meaning of the regulations. A user is a child, a participant or, where neither, simply a user: an adult user who is not in the Kolo has the status of user. The position of a child is governed by the Rulebook on the Participation of Children.

**Member without confirmation** — an adult user whom someone has introduced into the Kolo, but who has not yet been confirmed by a Kolo member who knows them personally. A member without confirmation belongs to the Kolo; belonging to the Kolo is not membership in the Foundation. They have the rights and obligations of a participant. Provisions of the Foundation's acts concerning a Kolo member, or a confirmed user, do not apply to them, unless an act expressly provides otherwise.

**Kolo member** — a user whose reality has been confirmed in the chain of confirmations by a confirmation, in accordance with the Rulebook on Proof of Reality. Only a Kolo member, including a Kolo keeper and a founder, is a confirmed user; confirmed status determines the right to confirm the reality of others and the other rights that the Foundation's acts attach to a Kolo member.

**Kolo keeper** — a Kolo member to whom ZRNO has been inscribed in the Protocol. A member without confirmation to whom ZRNO has been inscribed is not a Kolo keeper.

**Founders** — the initial Kolo keepers, designated under Article 14 of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality (Article 82). This term is distinct from the founder of the Foundation and from founders within the meaning of the Rulebook on Founding Contribution (Article 37).

**Kolo** — the circle of users for whom a valid verification has been recorded in the chain of confirmations: members without confirmation, Kolo members and Kolo keepers. The Kolo is not a legal entity, and belonging to the Kolo is not membership in the Foundation or in any other legal entity.

**Participant** — a user for whom a valid verification has been recorded in the chain of confirmations, whether by confirmation or by introduction into the Kolo: a member without confirmation, a Kolo member and a Kolo keeper, as well as a founder. Verification determines access to system functions. As of this amendment, the term "participant" denotes only a person who is in the Kolo and replaces the earlier term "participant in the Kolo", or "verified participant". Where an act adopted before this amendment refers to a participant regardless of status, a user is meant, and where it refers to a user as a status, a user who is not in the Kolo is meant.

**Pseudonym** — the account name under which the user is visible in the system and in the public ledger.

**Founding contribution** — work performed before the platform's launch whose contribution is retroactively recorded in POENs; one-time and time-limited.

**Financial contribution** — a dinar inflow to the Foundation on the basis of donations from natural persons and sponsorship by legal entities, entrepreneurs and holders of registered agricultural holdings.

**Operational contribution** — an activity outside the platform whose contribution is recorded in POENs after verification of completion.

**Sponsor** — a legal entity or entrepreneur that supports the Foundation's operations by donating money, goods, or services, as well as a holder of a registered agricultural holding that supports the Foundation's operations by donating money or through sponsorship in goods and services; it is not a system user and does not receive POEN or ZRNO.

**Automatic recording** — the recording of POENs as an automatic act of the Protocol executed pursuant to system rules, without the discretionary decision of any actor.

**Circle** — an organizational unit of the system based on shared interest or activity; it has no legal personality.

**Cooperative** — a local organizational unit of the system based on the territorial principle, registered under the Law on Cooperatives; it has full legal personality.

**Collective forms** — the collective term for organizational units of the system that connect users into more permanent structures: Circles and Cooperatives.

**Gornje Kolo (Upper Kolo)** — the body of the community composed of the Kolo keepers who have activated ZRNO; it establishes proposals on system rules, which the Foundation's Management Board adopts by its own act (Article 51). The Upper Kolo does not govern the Foundation.

**Management Board (UO)** — the body of the KOLO Foundation whose composition, mandate, and competencies are governed by the Statute.

**Protective veto** — the right of the Foundation to refuse the execution of an Upper Kolo decision that would jeopardize the operational and financial sustainability of the Foundation before it attains financial self-sufficiency.

**Quadratic voting** — the decision-making mechanism in the Upper Kolo in which voting power equals the integer value of the square root of the number of active ZRNOs.

**Whitepaper** — the conceptual and public document describing the KOLO system.

Terms not defined in this Article have the meaning established in the relevant chapters of this Rulebook or in special rulebooks.

### Article 3

This Rulebook is the overarching operational act of the KOLO system. Special rulebooks and other derived acts must be consistent with this Rulebook.

This Rulebook is adopted and amended by the Management Board of the KOLO Foundation, following the procedure specifically governed by this Rulebook.

The language of the system is Serbian.

## Chapter II — The Common Good, Actors, and Instruments

### Article 4

The KOLO system consists of three elements: the common good as that which users share and to which they contribute; the Foundation as the legal instrument that ensures the conditions for the functioning of the system; and the Protocol as the software that maintains the ledger and applies the rules. Users form the Community, but the Community is not a body nor a legal entity — participation in system governance is exercised through the Upper Kolo, in accordance with this Rulebook.

This chapter governs each of those elements: what the common good is and under what conditions it is used, what the Foundation does and what it may not do, what the Protocol can do and of what it is deprived.

### Article 5

The common good is the purpose of the KOLO system. It comprises the system software, system rules, the contribution and participation ledger, and content created in the system.

The common good is the collective good of all users. No user, nor the founder of the Foundation, nor the Foundation has ownership rights over the common good or the right to dispose of it.

The common good does not constitute collective ownership within the meaning of applicable property law categories. The user's relationship to the common good is participatory — a right to use and to contribute, not a right to dispose.

The collective character of the common good is ensured by the common-good licenses, the legal structure of the Foundation, which safeguards the common good but is not its owner, and the four principles of the system.

### Article 6

The infrastructure on which the system operates — servers, databases, network equipment, and other technical resources — is not a constituent part of the common good. The ledger as a record and the content belong to the common good; the database as a storage medium belongs to the infrastructure.

Infrastructure is the operational prerequisite for the functioning of the system. Its provision and maintenance is a service obligation of the Foundation.

The Foundation has the right and duty to select, change, and upgrade the infrastructure. No such change affects the status or integrity of the common good.

### Article 7

The system software is licensed under the GNU Affero General Public License, version 3.0 (AGPL-3.0).

Content created in the system is licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-SA 4.0).

The moral rights of authors of software and content remain intact and are not transferred.

The software and content of the common good may not be re-licensed under more restrictive terms; only replacement with a license of equivalent copyleft, or share-alike regime, is permitted.

The system brand — the name "KOLO", the logotype, the mark, and the domains — is not part of the common good and is under the exclusive control of the Foundation.

### Article 8

Contributions to the software are accepted under the terms of the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO), by which the contributor confirms the right to contribute the code under the common-good license.

Contributions to content are accepted upon acceptance of the common-good license.

Contributions belong to the common good.

### Article 9

The KOLO Foundation is the legal instrument of the system, which safeguards the common good but is not its owner. Nor is the Foundation the owner of the system.

The Foundation provides and maintains the infrastructure, receives dinar donations, finances the costs and programs of the system, and represents the system in legal transactions.

The Foundation does not acquire POEN or ZRNO and does not participate in the accounting framework of the system. The Foundation's relationship with the system is exclusively in dinar funds.

The internal organization, bodies, mandates, and decision-making procedures of the Foundation are governed by the Statute.

### Article 10

The KOLO Protocol is the technical mechanism of the common good. The Protocol maintains the ledger, calculates the accounting coefficient, and applies the system rules.

All Protocol operations are deterministic and algorithmic, without discretion. The Protocol cannot act outside the system rules.

The Protocol has no legal personality. The Protocol does not enter into contracts, does not hold accounts, does not enter into legal relationships with users, and is not a controller of personal data.

The rules pursuant to which the Protocol operates are established in accordance with this Rulebook.

### Article 11

The KOLO Community comprises all system users, who together care for the common good.

The Community is not a legal entity, has no bodies or representatives, and as a whole does not make decisions.

Participation in system governance is exercised through the Upper Kolo, in accordance with this Rulebook.

## Chapter III — POEN and the Contribution Ledger

### Article 12

POEN is the way the system records who has contributed how much and who has received how much. Every contribution, every donation, every exchange of goods or services between users — all of it is recorded in POENs. POEN is not money and cannot be converted into money; it is an internal record in the system.

This chapter governs how POEN is created, how the ledger functions, what it means for the system to be in equilibrium (zero-sum), and through which channels contributions are recorded.

### Article 13

POEN is the internal accounting unit of the system by which contributions and other forms of participation in the common good are recorded. POEN serves to let users know how much they have contributed and how much they have exchanged within the system; the closest analogy is a record in a registry — it records a fact, but is not in itself a means that can be spent outside the system.

New POEN records are inscribed exclusively by the Protocol through the contribution recording channels.

POEN has no bearer; it exists solely as a record in the Protocol's ledger. POEN amounts are expressed in whole numbers. POEN does not represent money, currency, electronic money, a means of payment, digital assets, a financial instrument, or a security. POEN cannot be converted into a means with value outside the system.

The provision of the preceding paragraph is not merely a label but a consequence of the properties of the record. POEN does not meet the elements by which digital assets, that is a virtual currency, are determined:

— it cannot be acquired by purchase: the Foundation does not sell it and does not issue it for consideration, and the recording of POEN is not a counter-performance for any giving whatsoever;

— it cannot be alienated for consideration: offering, buying, selling and brokering the trade of POEN for value outside the system are prohibited by the Terms of Use and entail the measures under those Terms;

— it is neither redeemed nor converted (Article 71 of this Rulebook), so there is no exit from it in money;

— the Foundation does not determine its price and does not offer it on a market: it publishes no ratio of POEN to the dinar or to any other currency, and applies no such ratio in any of its own procedures. The amount in a listing is set by the user themselves (Article 19 of the Terms of Use); if in doing so they start from a value expressed in money, that is their own estimate, which binds neither the Foundation nor the other party;

— it does not exist outside the Protocol's ledger and cannot be transferred to a record the Protocol does not keep;

— it does not serve the settlement of monetary obligations and cannot be offered in place of money.

No one is obliged to accept POEN. The Foundation does not accept POEN, expresses none of its own amounts in it, and publishes no price list in POENs. The amount in POENs in an exchange is set by the parties themselves and binds no other user. The number of POENs annulled in the distribution of goods in the Foundation's projects, including collective procurement and the distribution of vouchers from sponsorship in goods and services, is determined from the recorded contribution under the rules of that distribution; it is not the price of a good or service, does not express their value, and does not constitute a price list.

POEN cannot be acquired for the purpose of performing an exchange. A contribution on the basis of a donation is not recorded at the moment of payment: recording presupposes that the Foundation has first established the fact of receipt of the payment and the person with whose funds it was made (Article 15 and Article 39).

On account of POEN the Foundation provides no payment services and does not issue POEN as electronic money. The elements by which a payment service and electronic money are determined are missing:

— a POEN record is not a claim either against the Foundation or against any other person (Article 72);

— POEN is not issued in exchange for a payment of monetary funds: a contribution on the basis of a donation is recorded under the rules of the system, independently of the act of donation, and is not consideration for the payment (Article 73);

— in no ledger update does the Foundation receive, hold or transfer users' monetary funds, and the update concerns not monetary funds but a record in the ledger kept by the Protocol;

— the Foundation charges no fee for a ledger update between users under Article 16 of this Rulebook and receives no fee whatsoever for it;

— there is no payout, redemption or conversion from a POEN record (Article 71);

— a person who has no record in the Protocol's ledger does not receive POEN, and neither does the giver of a voucher from sponsorship in goods and services receive POEN: the giver performs its own agreement with the Foundation for the benefit of third parties.

The properties set out in the preceding paragraphs also bear on the notion of a user's income. By the recording of POEN the user receives neither money nor goods; nothing passes from the Foundation's assets into the user's assets, and the record so created cannot be turned into money, assigned or collected. The Foundation makes no payment on any basis by reason of the recording, does not determine or publish a value of POEN expressed in money and applies no such ratio, so no amount expressed in money is determined on that basis.

### Article 14

The sum of all POEN records in the system, including the Protocol's record, equals zero at all times. This property ensures that POENs cannot be created without a counter-entry — every recorded contribution has an exact counter-entry in the Protocol's ledger.

The Protocol maintains a record that is negative at all times and represents the counter-entry for all POENs recorded with users and organizational units.

No user or collective form may have a negative POEN record. There are eight exceptions, and they are listed here exhaustively:

1) **restitution upon annulment of a false verification**, governed by Article 20b of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality: where annulment withdraws POEN records that have already been spent, the uncovered part becomes a negative value and is transferred to the verifier who introduced the account;

2) **annulment of a record update following an exchange report**, governed by Article 16, paragraph 11 of this Rulebook: the ledger is corrected by a counter-entry in the amount of the entire record update, even where the recipient's record thereby becomes negative;

3) **write-off of a contribution from the children's space**, governed by the Rulebook on the Participation of Children: upon the ending of a friendship and upon attaining the age of majority, the contribution recorded under that Rulebook is written off, and the write-off is executed even where the record thereby becomes negative;

4) **write-off upon the conversion of an adult account into a minor's account**, governed by Article 4d of the Rulebook on the Participation of Children: where the Foundation establishes that a minor stands behind an account opened as an adult account, the contribution recorded to that account through the channels under Article 15 of this Rulebook is annulled, as is the contribution recorded to other persons on the occasion of the verifications that fall with that conversion; the write-off is executed even where the record thereby becomes negative, each person returns solely what was recorded to them on that occasion, and the burden is not transferred to another person;

5) **write-off upon the alignment of existing confirmations**, governed by Article 22a of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality: where a contribution recorded under verifications performed before the condition under Article 7 of that Rulebook was introduced is returned to the state of a noted contribution, each affected person has written off what was recorded to them on the occasion of that verification; the write-off is executed even where the record thereby becomes negative, and the burden is not transferred to another person;

6) **write-off upon the termination of a farm**, governed by Article 40c of this Rulebook and by the Rulebook on Farms: where an approved farm terminates — because the Foundation has established that the declaration of accuracy of data given when registering a farm or when joining a farm was false and has annulled the farm's approval, or on another ground of termination established by the Rulebook on Farms — each member of that farm at the moment of termination, and in the case of a false declaration also each person who was a member of the farm during the period to which the false declaration relates, even if they have since left, has written off the contribution recorded to them under that farm; the write-off is executed even where the record thereby becomes negative, each person returns solely what was recorded to them, and the burden is not transferred to another person;

7) **write-off upon non-performance of sponsorship in goods and services**, governed by Article 13j of the Rulebook on Sponsorship and Donations: where the Foundation, upon a complaint by the voucher holder, establishes that the giver did not hand over the good or did not provide the service, and did not remedy this within the period granted, the agreement is terminated and the user who submitted the sponsorship application has written off the contribution recorded to them for each voucher under that agreement that the giver did not perform; the write-off is executed even where the record thereby becomes negative, and the burden is not transferred to another person;

8) **write-off upon establishing a multiple account**, governed by Article 4b of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality: where the Foundation establishes that two accounts are accounts of the same person, the account that ceases is closed, and the account the person retains has written off the net amount of the record updates made from the ceasing account in favour of the retained account, reduced by the record updates in the opposite direction; the write-off is executed even where the record thereby becomes negative, and the records of other persons in whose favour the ledger was updated from the ceasing account are not affected.

The same regime applies to a negative record on each of the eight grounds: it is not a debt and is not collected, POENs that subsequently accrue to the user fill it first, initiating a ledger update in favour of another user is possible only once the record goes above zero, and the exchange of goods and services is not restricted.

No further ground for a negative record may be established — neither by this Rulebook without amending this Article, nor by any other act.

The exchange of goods and services redistributes existing POENs and does not change their total number in the system. The total number of POENs increases through the inscription of new records via the contribution recording channels and through the correction of an annulment effected without grounds within the meaning of Article 14a of this Rulebook, and decreases through the annulment of records on the grounds established by this Rulebook, including the one-time annulment of the existing contribution for supervision, the implementation of which is governed by Article 22b of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality. There is no further ground for increasing the total number of POENs.

### Article 14a

A POEN record is also annulled when a user uses it to participate in a project of the Foundation, under the conditions established by a separate rulebook.

Annulment upon use is not a measure against the user. It does not presuppose any irregularity, is not based on the termination of user status, and differs from the annulment of records under Article 34 of this Rulebook and from annulment under Chapter VIII of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

The annulment is effected by a counter-record in favour of the Protocol. The sum of all records continues to be zero, while the total number of recorded POEN decreases.

Through annulment upon use, a user's record cannot pass into a negative value; only the number of POEN present in the record is annulled. This ground does not introduce a new exception to the prohibition of a negative record under Article 14, paragraph 3.

Where it is established that the condition of use was not met — that the good was not collected, or that the collected good was not usable for the purpose for which it was procured — the annulment is corrected by a counter-entry in favour of the user, in the number of POEN that had been annulled. The correction is not the inscription of a new record via a channel under Article 15 of this Rulebook, but the removal of an annulment effected without grounds.

The correction is neither a refund of consideration nor any form of payment. On account of the correction the user receives no value other than the record that had been annulled, and the Foundation neither pays out nor receives anything on that ground.

The conditions, procedure, amount, and moment of annulment upon use, as well as the deadline and procedure upon a reported defect, are established by a separate rulebook.

### Article 15

The Protocol inscribes new POEN records through the following contribution recording channels:

1) operational contribution;

2) verification of users in the chain of confirmations;

3) financial contribution;

4) sponsorship;

5) growth of collective forms;

6) social programmes;

7) founding contribution;

8) contribution to platform content;

9) contribution of children in the children's space;

10) farm contribution.

The channel under item 8 comprises the contribution for a first listing (Article 40a), and the channel under item 10 the farm contribution (Article 40c).

The channels under items 1 and 6 are subject to a common daily limit established by the Rulebook on Support Programmes and the Rulebook on Operational Contribution. Each of the other channels is limited by its own measure: the channels under items 2, 8, and 10 record a contribution once per person; the channels under items 3 and 4 by the amount of funds actually received, or by the value of the goods under the donation agreement; the channel under item 5 by the closed table of growth thresholds; the channel under item 7 by the upper limit of founding contribution; and the contribution under item 9 is written off upon the child attaining the age of majority. The total number of POENs arising outside the daily limit is thereby tied to the number of real persons, to the funds received, and to pre-determined limits.

The channels under items 1, 3, 4, 7, 8, and 10 are governed by Chapter VI of this Rulebook. The channel under item 2 is governed by the Rulebook on Proof of Reality. The entry of POENs under that channel occurs once the participant makes their first confirmed contribution, under the conditions established by Article 7 of that Rulebook; until entered, the contribution is noted and does not enter the total number of recorded POENs. The channels under items 5, 6, and 9 are governed in Chapter VIII as system modules; the channel under item 9 is further governed by the Rulebook on the Participation of Children. Recording under the channel under item 9 is an automatic act of the Protocol and does not count towards the daily limit of Protocol Programs; the POENs recorded enter the total number of recorded POENs and thereby the accounting framework of the system. The recording parameters, conditions, and limitations for each channel are established by special rulebooks, in particular the Rulebook on Operational Contribution, the Rulebook on Proof of Reality, the Rulebook on Sponsorship and Donations, the Rulebook on Cooperatives, the Rulebook on Farms, the Rulebook on Support Programmes, and the Rulebook on Founding Contribution.

Financial contribution is made in dinars. Sponsorship is made by donating money, goods, or services. Sponsorship in goods and services is received when the donation agreement has been signed and the vouchers issued, under the conditions of the Rulebook on Sponsorship and Donations. Dinar funds enter the Foundation and do not enter the accounting framework of the system. Upon receipt of a donation or a sponsorship contribution, the Protocol records the contribution in POENs in the record of the relevant user, pursuant to the recording parameters established by a special rulebook. The recording of POENs is an automatic act of the Protocol and does not create a contractual relationship between the donor and the Foundation.

Two decisions in that procedure are separate. The fact of receipt of a payment and the person with whose funds it was made are established by the Foundation, by comparing the payer's details with the user's account; the payment intermediary's report of a completed charge does not itself trigger recording. In the case of sponsorship in goods and services, the Foundation establishes that the agreement has been concluded and that the price list has been attached. The number of POENs recorded is determined automatically by the Protocol, pursuant to the parameters of a separate rulebook, and is not subject to the judgement of any actor in the system.

### Article 16

Users within the system exchange goods and services. The Protocol records each exchange by updating the POEN ledger: the record of the user providing the good or service is increased, and the record of the receiving user is decreased by the same amount.

Exchange redistributes existing POENs among users and does not increase their total number in the system. The Protocol updates the ledger when users conduct an exchange.

Exchange is not a payment transaction. The ledger update of POENs is not a transfer of monetary value and does not constitute a payment within the meaning of payment services regulations.

For performance, quality, liability, and risk in exchange, the users who take part in it are responsible, pursuant to the general rules of the law of obligations. The Foundation and the Protocol do not mediate in the exchange and are not responsible for the performance of users' obligations.

All users may exchange goods and services. Initiating a POEN ledger update in favor of another user is available to participants, and to users who are not in the Kolo under the conditions of Article 28.

A ledger update carried out for the purpose of a transaction by which POEN is offered, bought, sold, or brokered for value outside the system is prohibited and entails the measures established by the Terms of Use. Such an update is not an exchange of goods and services within the meaning of this Article.

The Foundation does not broker exchanges of POEN for money, does not organise such exchanges and bears no responsibility for them. Publishing listings in the platform's advertising space that offer or seek an exchange of POEN for money is not permitted. The transfer of POEN between users serves the exchange of products, services and knowledge and other forms of reciprocity within the community.

The preview of listings in the platform's advertising space — the content of an offer or request, the amount in POEN, location, and pseudonym of the advertiser — is public and available to all visitors for the accessibility of exchange.

Posting a listing offering or seeking a good or a service and initiating communication regarding a listing are available to users as well, under the conditions and limitations established by the Terms of Use. Access to the advertiser's contact details is available exclusively to participants. A user may reply within a conversation in which they participate.

A listing posted by a user carries a visible mark indicating that the advertiser is not in the Kolo. The mark is available to everyone to whom the listing itself is available.

A user who initiated a record update in favour of another user, where the exchange on account of which they did so has not been fulfilled, may lodge a complaint with the Foundation, within thirty days of the record update. Only that user may lodge the complaint, from their own profile, and only one complaint per record update. Before deciding, the Foundation notifies the other party and allows it seven days to make a statement; if it makes no statement within that period, the decision is taken without it. The Foundation decides with a statement of reasons communicated to both parties; where it establishes that the exchange has not been fulfilled, the Protocol corrects the ledger by a counter-entry in the amount of the entire record update, in favour of the complainant's record. The correction is executed even where the recipient's record thereby becomes negative (Article 14, paragraph 3, item 2). The correction of the ledger is neither a refund nor a payment: the Foundation pays out nothing and receives nothing on that ground. A user whose record has been decreased by that decision may lodge a complaint against it within thirty days of the annulment. Deciding on a complaint concerns the record in the Protocol's ledger; it does not make the Foundation a party to the exchange, does not establish its liability for the fulfilment of users' obligations, and does not constitute mediation in the exchange.

For non-registered persons, the advertiser's pseudonym in the advertising space is not linked to the contribution ledger, account balance, or user profile, and for users who are not in the Kolo it is linked only to the restricted profile established by the Terms of Use, without the contribution ledger and account balance. The rules of the platform's advertising space are governed by the Terms of Use.

## Chapter IV — ZRNO and the Accounting Framework

### Article 17

ZRNO is the second record the system maintains about the user. While POEN records contribution, ZRNO records position — the measure of more permanent participation in the common good, on the basis of which the Kolo keeper acquires a vote in decisions about system rules. The accounting coefficient is the numerical ratio that connects POEN and ZRNO.

This chapter governs what ZRNO is, how it is inscribed and written off, what states it has, why it cannot be transferred, and how the accounting coefficient is calculated and what the accounting period is.

### Article 18

ZRNO is the internal accounting unit of the system by which a user's position in the common good is recorded in the Protocol's ledger. The position arises from the user's contribution and gives them a vote in decisions about system rules. ZRNO does not represent a share, a stock, a security, a collective investment unit, an investment contract, or digital assets, and carries no dividend, interest, return, or right to liquidation proceeds.

The total number of ZRNOs is 1,000,000 and cannot be increased or decreased.

At any given time, one part of the ZRNOs is inscribed with users and another part is available for inscription in the Protocol; the sum of inscribed and available ZRNOs always equals the total number.

ZRNO is inscribed and written off exclusively by the Protocol, at the user's request and pursuant to the system rules. The governance function — the right to vote in the Upper Kolo — arises from inscribed and activated ZRNO; ZRNO is not in itself a voting right, but a record of position from which that right arises.

### Article 19

ZRNO is inscribed from the contribution recorded in the record of a participant, irrespective of whether that contribution was recorded through a channel under Article 15 or the record was increased by the exchange of goods and services with other users. By inscribing ZRNO a Kolo member becomes a Kolo keeper.

ZRNO is inscribed only by a Kolo member or a member without confirmation; a user does not inscribe ZRNO. By inscribing ZRNO a member without confirmation does not become a Kolo keeper and does not activate the inscribed ZRNO until confirmed (Article 28a). A vote in the Upper Kolo arises not from inscribed but from activated ZRNO of a Kolo keeper whose reality has been confirmed (Article 30 and Article 45). The position of a user to whom ZRNO is inscribed is governed by Article 30.

Inscription of ZRNO requires a minimum of 20,000 recorded POENs in the record of the user inscribing ZRNO. In one accounting period, a user may use at most 1% of their recorded POENs for ZRNO inscription.

An exception to the conditions of this Article at the start of the system is governed by the transitional provision (Article 82).

ZRNO inscription is initiated by the participant inscribing ZRNO. The Protocol verifies the fulfilment of conditions and executes the inscription if conditions are met. Inscription is not a discretionary decision and no system actor approves or rejects it.

Upon ZRNO inscription, the Protocol reduces that user's recorded POENs pursuant to the accounting coefficient applicable for the current accounting period.

### Article 20

Inscribed ZRNO has two states: free and active.

Free ZRNO is inscribed ZRNO without a governance function. A Kolo keeper may activate or write off free ZRNO. A member without confirmation to whom ZRNO is inscribed may write off free ZRNO, but may not activate it (Article 28a).

Active ZRNO is ZRNO that the Kolo keeper has activated for participation in governance; active ZRNO grants the right to vote in the Upper Kolo. Active ZRNO cannot be written off until the Kolo keeper returns it to the free state.

Transitions between ZRNO states and their effects occur at the end of the accounting period.

### Article 21

A Kolo keeper may write off free ZRNO — return it to the Protocol. Free ZRNO may also be written off by a member without confirmation to whom ZRNO is inscribed (Article 20).

Upon write-off, the Protocol records POENs to the user writing off the ZRNO pursuant to the accounting coefficient applicable for the current accounting period.

Active ZRNO cannot be written off. A Kolo keeper who wishes to write off active ZRNO must first return it to the free state.

A Kolo keeper whose ZRNO has been entirely written off becomes a Kolo member.

The difference between the accounting coefficient at the time of inscription and at the time of write-off is not a return. It is an arithmetic consequence of a change in the ledger state of the whole system; no actor pays it or guarantees it. POENs recorded upon write-off have the same status as all other POENs.

### Article 22

ZRNO cannot be transferred to another user.

ZRNO is bound to the user in whose record it is inscribed and is not transferred — neither on its own nor together with the account.

There is no market for ZRNO, there is no price for ZRNO, and there is no possibility of trading it.

### Article 23

The accounting coefficient is the numerical ratio of the total number of recorded POENs to the number of ZRNOs available for inscription in the Protocol. The accounting coefficient indicates how many POENs are needed to inscribe one ZRNO in a given accounting period.

The accounting coefficient is calculated by the Protocol, automatically and without discretion.

The accounting coefficient changes with the total number of recorded POENs: the inscription of new POEN records shifts it upward, and the annulment of recorded POENs shifts it downward. The coefficient is not predetermined to rise.

The inscription and write-off of ZRNO do not shift the accounting coefficient. Upon inscription, alongside the number of ZRNOs available in the Protocol, the total number of recorded POENs is reduced in the same proportion — because the POENs spent on the inscription return to the Protocol; upon write-off, both quantities increase in the same ratio. A Kolo keeper cannot shift the coefficient by their own inscription or their own write-off.

The exchange of goods and services between users does not change the coefficient.

The accounting coefficient is an administrative variable. It is not a price, not an exchange rate, and not a performance index; it does not express the value of ZRNO or POEN outside the system.

### Article 24

The accounting period is the time interval at the end of which the Protocol calculates the accounting coefficient.

At the end of the accounting period, the rules for ZRNO inscription and write-off are applied and transitions between ZRNO states occur.

The accounting period lasts twenty-four hours, from midnight to midnight.

### Article 25

A Kolo keeper holds a position in the accounting system that shifts with the accounting coefficient, as well as the right to participate in system governance through the Upper Kolo.

A change in a Kolo keeper's position arises exclusively as a consequence of activity in the system and a change in the accounting coefficient. That change is not a return, is not guaranteed, and no actor pays it.

The benefit of the position is realized by the Kolo keeper exclusively within the system. ZRNO and POEN have no value outside the system and cannot be converted into money.

A Kolo keeper who does not actively participate in the system retains their recorded position; any change in that position is a consequence of a change in the accounting coefficient, which the Protocol calculates automatically and without discretion (Article 23), not an action of the system or of another person directed at the individual.

The position of a Kolo keeper does not constitute an investment. That assessment rests neither on the name of the unit nor on a declaration that ZRNO is not an investment instrument, but on the absence of the elements that make an investment an investment:

1) ZRNO is not acquired by investing funds — it is inscribed from a contribution already recorded in the record of a participant; the Foundation does not sell ZRNO, does not offer it and does not set a price for it, inscription is not a counter-performance, and no price of ZRNO exists (Article 22);

2) there is no return that could be realized — a change in position is expressed exclusively in POENs, which have no value outside the system and are neither redeemed nor converted, directly or indirectly (Article 71), so the difference in the accounting coefficient cannot leave the Protocol's records;

3) there is no effort of another person directed at creating a benefit for the Kolo keeper — the accounting coefficient is the quotient of two sums from the records, which the Protocol calculates automatically and without discretion (Article 23); no person manages it or promises it, and the Kolo keeper does not shift it by their own inscription or their own write-off;

4) the Kolo keeper is not separated from governance — the vote in the Upper Kolo arises from activated ZRNO (Article 45), so the Kolo keeper takes part in establishing the rules of the system themselves. Whoever wants a vote must activate their ZRNO and thereby forgo write-off until it is returned to free status (Article 46); the governance function and accounting flexibility are mutually exclusive.

A user to whom ZRNO is inscribed but who is no longer in the Kolo holds ZRNO but does not write it off (Article 30). A member without confirmation to whom ZRNO is inscribed does not activate it until confirmed (Article 28a); that restriction arises from proof of reality, not from the position in the accounting system. A member without confirmation may write off free ZRNO, but does not govern through it; points 1–3 of paragraph 5 apply to them as well, and they acquire a vote not through a stake but through confirmation, which is not obtained with funds and does not depend on the number of inscribed ZRNO. From the funds by which that user's contribution was recorded, a larger number of recorded POENs cannot arise.

## Chapter V — User Statuses, Proof of Reality, and Termination of Status

### Article 26

For a user to be able to exchange, to have contributions recorded, or to govern the system, the system must know that the person is a real and unique individual. This chapter governs the statuses a user may hold, the way in which it is confirmed that a user is a real person, and what happens when a user ceases to be part of the system.

The operational elements of proof of reality and the mechanics of status termination are governed by a special rulebook.

### Article 27

An adult user in the KOLO system may hold one of four statuses: user, member without confirmation, Kolo member, and Kolo keeper.

A minor user does not hold a status under this Article; their position is governed by the Rulebook on the Participation of Children.

A user transitions from one status to another under the conditions established by the system rules.

Status determines the scope of a user's rights and capabilities in the system.

### Article 28

The status of user is held by an adult user who is not in the Kolo.

A user has the right to view public system content, including viewing listings in the platform's advertising space. They may exchange goods and services and post a listing in the platform's advertising space offering or seeking a good or a service, under the conditions set out in the Terms of Use.

In POEN ledger updates a user participates as a receiver and may initiate a ledger update in favor of another user. A ledger update moves the record between users' accounts; the total number of POEN does not change (Article 15). POEN records made before verification remain valid and are not annulled.

A user has a contribution recorded through the channel of contribution to platform content (Article 15 item 8 and Article 40a) and through the channels of financial contribution and sponsorship (Article 15 items 3 and 4). Through the other channels no contribution is recorded for them.

A user may not access advertisers' contact details. They initiate communication via the Platform regarding a listing, to the extent established by the Terms of Use, and reply in a conversation in which they participate.

A user who does not know any Kolo member may submit a conversation request, by which they leave Kolo members the possibility of calling them in order to get acquainted (Article 16b of the Terms of Use). A user under Article 32 paragraph 5 is also placed on the call list. In both cases a Kolo member may, after the conversation, introduce them into the Kolo, and only in the chain of confirmations.

### Article 28a

*Member without confirmation*

A member without confirmation is an adult user whom someone has introduced into the Kolo, but who has not yet been confirmed by a Kolo member who knows them personally, in accordance with the Rulebook on Proof of Reality. A member without confirmation belongs to the Kolo (Article 2), and belonging to the Kolo is not membership in the Foundation.

Introduction into the Kolo is made by a Kolo member, after a telephone conversation with a user from the call list, or by a member without confirmation, who introduces a user whom they know personally. Whoever is introduced by a member without confirmation is themselves a member without confirmation.

A member without confirmation is a participant and has the rights that the Foundation's acts attach to a participant: they exchange goods and services, initiate POEN ledger updates, have contributions recorded in POENs, participate in collective forms, social programmes, projects and procurements, and introduce into the Kolo users whom they know personally. They do not have the rights that the Foundation's acts attach to a Kolo member, or to a confirmed user, unless an act expressly provides otherwise. They do not confirm the reality of others, but only introduce them into the Kolo; they do not confirm that the conditions for another user's support programme are met; they do not see the call list, do not give a member recommendation, and do not carry out supervision of verifications. They inscribe ZRNO under the conditions of Article 19, but do not activate it: they have no vote, are not part of the Upper Kolo, do not delegate a vote, and do not become a Kolo keeper by inscribing ZRNO. They may write off free ZRNO (Article 20). By inscribing ZRNO they come onto the call list for confirmation, in first place, and the Foundation is obliged to ensure that a Kolo keeper calls them within three days, in accordance with the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

A member without confirmation becomes a Kolo member when confirmed by a Kolo member, or a founder, who knows them personally, by a Kolo keeper after a telephone conversation, when the member without confirmation has been in the Kolo for at least 30 days and meets the other conditions of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality, or by the Foundation on its own decision. An exchange of goods and services may be the way in which they became acquainted, but it is not a condition of confirmation. The confirmation of a member without confirmation is not a new verification and no POENs are recorded for it; it is subject to supervision in the same way as a verification, in accordance with the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

When a user becomes a Kolo member, they receive a list of linked users: those whom they introduced into the Kolo while they were a member without confirmation and the one who introduced them, if these are still members without confirmation. From that list they personally confirm those whom they know; each such confirmation is a confirmation of a member without confirmation, is subject to supervision, and no POENs are recorded for it. A member without confirmation never becomes a Kolo member automatically. Detailed conditions are established by the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

### Article 29

A Kolo member is a user whose reality has been confirmed in the chain of confirmations by the confirmation of a Kolo member who knows them personally, or of a founder, or who, as a member without confirmation, has been confirmed under Article 28a, in accordance with the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

A Kolo member may exchange goods and services, have contributions recorded in POENs, participate in collective forms and social programmes, confirm the reality of users whom they know personally, introduce into the Kolo, after a conversation, users from the call list, and confirm members without confirmation whom they know personally.

A Kolo member inscribes ZRNO when they satisfy the conditions of Article 19, thereby becoming a Kolo keeper. A member without confirmation to whom ZRNO is inscribed becomes a Kolo keeper when confirmed. The activation of ZRNO and the rights arising from activation are bound to confirmed status — to the confirmation of a Kolo member, or to the confirmation under Article 28a.

### Article 30

A Kolo keeper is a Kolo member to whom ZRNO has been inscribed in the Protocol. The first Kolo keepers — the founders — are established at the start of the system in the manner set out in the transitional provision of this Rulebook (Article 82).

A Kolo keeper has all the rights of a Kolo member. In addition, they hold a position in the accounting system, participate by activating ZRNO in system governance through the Upper Kolo, and carry out supervision of verifications, in accordance with the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

A Kolo keeper whose confirmation of reality falls becomes a user. Their ZRNO remains inscribed and frozen: until confirmed again, a user to whom ZRNO is inscribed does not activate it, does not write it off, does not delegate a vote on its basis and is not part of the Upper Kolo; from inscribed ZRNO there arises neither a vote, nor delegation, nor the right to confirm. When their reality is confirmed again, they are again a Kolo keeper, and if they are only introduced into the Kolo, they hold the position of a member without confirmation to whom ZRNO is inscribed (Article 28a). The same applies to a member without confirmation to whom ZRNO is inscribed and whose verification ceases. A Kolo keeper whose confirmation alone is annulled, while their introduction into the Kolo remains valid, becomes a member without confirmation (Article 28a): their ZRNO remains inscribed, and active ZRNO passes into the free state; they may activate it again when confirmed.

A Kolo keeper whose ZRNO has been entirely written off becomes a Kolo member.

### Article 31

Proof of reality is a model of user verification based on personal acquaintance or on a conversation following a call, in accordance with the Rulebook on Proof of Reality. It confirms that the user is a real person and that they are continuously the same person. Uniqueness — one person, one account — is not confirmed by verification, because the verifier knows the person, not their other accounts: it is a prohibition under the Terms of Use, and a multiple account is established by the Foundation under the Rulebook on Proof of Reality, on the basis of a comparison of data and of contacting the person.

Proof of reality does not require the collection of personal documents or the unique citizen identification number. The contribution ledger is kept under a pseudonym; the user's first and last name, where obtained, is kept in the separate identity register under Article 31a.

Verification of reality is the processing of personal data. The legal basis for that processing is the execution of the contractual relationship between the user and the Foundation.

### Article 31a

*Identity register*

The Foundation maintains an identity register — a register in which the user's first and last name is kept alongside the account's pseudonym. The identity register serves to give effect to the principle of one person, one account under Article 31 and to verify the ground on which a contribution was recorded. It is kept separately from the contribution ledger.

The first and last name is entered in the identity register in the following cases:

1) upon registering a farm and upon joining a farm — the first and last name is entered by the farm member themselves, for their own account;

2) upon establishing the receipt of a public donation — the first and last name of the donor, where it matches the name of the payer from the bank statement;

3) upon acquiring the capacity of a cooperative member — the first and last name that the cooperative submits to the Foundation;

4) upon establishing the receipt of a sponsorship — the first and last name of the user who submitted the sponsorship application on behalf of the legal entity or the entrepreneur, after the Foundation has compared the name, the registration number and the representative with the data of the register in which the sponsor is entered, or the first and last name of the holder of a registered agricultural holding, together with the number of the holding stated by the holder;

5) upon entering the call list — the first and last name is entered by the user themselves, for their own account, in accordance with Article 4a of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

One first and last name is kept per account. A first and last name obtained in a later case under paragraph 2 does not replace the one already entered. If it differs, joining a farm, acquiring the capacity of a cooperative member, a sponsorship application and entering the call list are carried out only once the discrepancy has been resolved, while the donation is recorded and the entered name remains. An inaccurate entry is corrected by a complaint under Article 37a of the Terms of Use.

The unique citizen identification number, the identity card number and data from other identity documents are not entered in the identity register, and copies of identity documents are not kept. Where the Foundation compares the first and last name with an identity document or a public register, only the fact that the comparison was carried out is recorded.

The first and last name from the identity register is not entered in the contribution ledger and is not displayed to other users. It is accessible only to the Foundation's administrator team and to the user themselves, as well as to the Kolo member who calls a user from the call list in order to get acquainted, to the extent and under the conditions established by Article 4a of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality. The first and last name is compared with the names kept alongside other accounts in order to prevent multiple accounts (Articles 4a and 4b of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality); the comparison discloses to another user neither the name nor the pseudonym of the other account. The entry is not a verification, never confirms reality, and does not change the user's status; reality is confirmed only in the chain of confirmations. The entry is not a condition for proof of reality or for access to system functions, except for participation under paragraph 2.

For a minor user, the identity register is not kept.

The first and last name is deleted from the identity register upon the termination of the user's status (Article 34).

### Article 32

A user's reality is confirmed exclusively through the chain of confirmations: Kolo members confirm the reality of users whom they know personally or, after a conversation, introduce them into the Kolo, while members without confirmation introduce into the Kolo users whom they know personally. A user, as a rule, becomes a Kolo member because someone who is already a Kolo member knows them and confirms that they exist.

A user's reality is established by one verifier, through one verification — a confirmation or an introduction into the Kolo. A user is either in the Kolo or not; belonging to the Kolo has no degrees, and the difference between a confirmation and an introduction into the Kolo determines only the actions that the Foundation's acts attach to a Kolo member. Verification determines access to system functions, while confirmed status determines the right to confirm the reality of others. The number of verifications a verifier may perform is not limited; the pace of confirmation is governed by the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

The set of confirmations in the chain of confirmations constitutes the verification graph. The verification graph is a record of personal data; it is maintained on the infrastructure on which the Protocol operates, with data minimization. The graph also covers supervision data — the supervision outcome and, where suspicion is expressed, the subject of suspicion and the reason code (Article 11 of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality). Those data are not public and Article 67 of this Rulebook applies to them.

A user establishes the contact necessary for the conduct of verification through the platform's advertising space (Article 16), by posting a listing by which they present themselves to Kolo members, and through the conversation request established by the Terms of Use. The existence of that path does not change the nature, procedure, or conditions of verification established by this Rulebook and the Rulebook on Proof of Reality. The rules for publication, content, and data processing are established by the Terms of Use and the Privacy Policy.

The payment of a public donation of at least 5,000 dinars, a sponsorship in money, and the application of a farm entered in the Register of Agricultural Holdings do not confirm reality. When the Foundation establishes the receipt of such a payment, or receives such an application, and compares the details of the payer, the submitter or the holder of the farm with the account, the user is placed on the call list: a Kolo member calls them in order to get acquainted and, after the conversation, may introduce them into the Kolo. The recording of a contribution for a donation or sponsorship does not depend on the call. The conditions of the list, the call and the confirmation are established by the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

The rules of confirmation and the measures against abuse of the chain of confirmations are established by a special rulebook.

### Article 33

A user's status terminates by withdrawal, exclusion, or death.

Withdrawal is the voluntary termination of status, on the basis of the user's electronic declaration, without a notice period.

Exclusion is the termination of status by a Foundation decision, due to a serious breach of system rules.

A user's access to system functions may be temporarily restricted by suspension; during suspension the user retains their status but may not use the functions to which the suspension applies.

The grounds, procedure, and mechanics of each mode of status termination and suspension are governed by the Terms of Use.

### Article 34

Upon termination of status, the user's active ZRNO transitions to the free state, after which all free ZRNO is written off and returned to the available ZRNOs in the Protocol. The write-off of ZRNO upon status termination does not trigger the recording of POENs pursuant to the accounting coefficient.

The user's POEN records are annulled, and the Protocol's corresponding counter-entry is reduced by the same amount. The zero-sum invariant remains preserved. A negative POEN record arising under Article 14, paragraph 3 of this Rulebook is not annulled upon termination of status and is not transferred to the Protocol; it remains attached to the pseudonymized numerical history under this Article. Otherwise, leaving the system would erase the restitution and the burden would fall on all remaining users.

The annulment of POEN records and the return of ZRNO to the available pool changes the accounting coefficient on both levels — the numerator (total recorded POENs) decreases and the denominator (available ZRNOs) increases.

No monetary compensation is paid to the user.

Upon status termination, the pseudonymization of user data is initiated: the email address, voluntarily submitted data, and the first and last name from the identity register (Article 31a) are deleted, the user's connections in the verification graph are anonymized, and the numerical history is retained under a pseudonym that does not point to the name. The Foundation retains the internal account identifier, so the retained records still constitute personal data; they are kept because the right to erasure does not apply where processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to retain them and for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims (Article 11 of the Privacy Policy).

POEN and ZRNO records are not inherited as property rights. The handling of ledger records in the event of a user's death is governed in more detail by the Terms of Use.

## Chapter VI — Contribution to the Common Good

### Article 35

The common good does not arise by itself — it arises through contribution. In addition to the exchange of goods and services between users, which redistributes existing POENs, the system records new POENs through the channels established in Article 15 of this Rulebook. This chapter elaborates five of those channels — operational contribution, donations, sponsorship, founding contribution, and contribution to platform content. The verification of users as a form of contribution is governed by the Rulebook on Proof of Reality. The channels for growth of collective forms and social programmes are governed in Chapter VIII as system modules.

Recording parameters, procedures, and limitations are established by special rulebooks.

### Article 36

Operational contribution is an activity outside the platform whose contribution is recorded in POENs after verification of completion.

The Foundation, the Upper Kolo, or Kolo keepers publish a task to be performed for the common good. The user voluntarily applies for the execution of the task. After completion, Kolo keepers verify the completion before recording the contribution. In Phase 1, while the Upper Kolo has not been established, the verification function is performed by members of the Foundation's Management Board or by a person authorized by the Management Board, in accordance with the Rulebook on Operational Contribution. All applications are publicly visible to all system users.

Operational contribution may encompass a wide spectrum of activities — from organizing a local event, through technical work on infrastructure, to promoting the system in the community.

Operational contribution does not establish an employment relationship within the meaning of Article 5 of the Labour Act, nor does it constitute work outside an employment relationship — in particular not a contract for work. A commissioning party, a commissioned performance and remuneration are all absent: the task is not commissioned on behalf of the Foundation but published on account of a need of the common good, the performer decides independently whether to apply and how to carry the task out, and by reason of the completed task the Foundation receives no value whatsoever and acquires no right against the performer. This is regulated in more detail by the Rulebook on Operational Contribution.

The procedure for applying for, executing, and verifying operational contribution is established by a special rulebook.

### Article 37

Founding contribution is the work performed before the platform's launch — system design, protocol development, and legal and organizational preparation — whose contribution is retroactively recorded in POENs.

Founding contribution is in its nature an operational contribution performed before the system existed. The Protocol records that contribution retroactively through a special channel.

The recording of founding contribution is not remuneration for work. A commissioning party, a commissioned performance and remuneration are all absent: the work was not performed on the instructions of the Foundation, on that basis the Foundation receives no value whatsoever and acquires no right against the founder, and POEN recorded through this channel does not express the value of working time and cannot be converted into money.

The recording of founding contribution is gradual, in steps of a fixed amount, tied to the cumulative growth of the total number of POENs.

Founding contribution is limited. When the Protocol has recorded the full amount up to a pre-determined upper limit, the channel closes permanently.

POENs recorded through this channel are entered in the records of the founders within the meaning of the Rulebook on Founding Contribution — the natural persons who performed the work before the platform's launch — and have the same status as all other POENs. Founding contribution is a separate recording channel and does not affect the operational contribution limit.

The upper limit of founding contribution, the amount and schedule of recording steps, and the point at which the channel closes are established by a special rulebook.

### Article 38

Financial contribution is a dinar inflow to the Foundation on the basis of donations by natural persons and sponsorship by legal entities, entrepreneurs and holders of registered agricultural holdings.

Both forms rest on the same principle: a non-refundable donation to the Foundation whose contribution is recorded in POENs as an automatic act of the Protocol. Dinar funds enter the Foundation and do not enter the accounting framework of the system.

When dinar inflows exceed the Foundation's operational costs, the surplus is directed to system programs. The rules for distributing the surplus are determined by the founder of the Foundation and the Foundation in Phase 1, and by the Upper Kolo in Phase 2.

### Article 39

A natural person's donation is a dinar inflow to the Foundation on the basis of a donation agreement.

Receipt of a donation is established by the Foundation, by comparing the payer's details with the user's account. The payment intermediary's report of a completed charge is not an establishment of receipt but a ground for entering the payment into that procedure.

Upon established receipt of a donation, the Protocol records the contribution in the donor's record in POENs, pursuant to the recording parameters established by a separate rulebook. The recording of POENs is an automatic act of the Protocol and does not establish a contractual relationship between the donor and the Foundation with respect to POEN.

The Foundation issues a donation receipt upon the donor's request, in accordance with the law. The tax treatment of the donation depends on the donor's status, the Foundation's registered status, and the applicable tax regulations at the time of the donation.

The recording parameters for donations, including the donation recording coefficient and donation levels, are established by a special rulebook.

### Article 40

A sponsor is a legal entity or entrepreneur that supports the Foundation's operations by donating money, goods, or services, as well as a holder of a registered agricultural holding that supports the Foundation's operations by donating money or through sponsorship in goods and services. A sponsor is not a system user and does not receive POEN or ZRNO.

The sponsorship contribution is recorded in the record of the natural person who is a system user — the owner of the legal entity, or the entrepreneur themselves or the holder of the registered agricultural holding.

The relationship between the Foundation and the sponsor is governed by a donation agreement. Every donation is recorded at the time of receipt. The Foundation maintains a record of the link between the sponsor and the user in whose record the contribution is entered. The procedure for identifying the beneficial owner and the rules in cases of multiple or indirect ownership are established by a special rulebook.

### Article 40a

*Contribution to platform content*

The content users create within the system is part of the common good and is licensed in accordance with Article 7 of this Rulebook. The platform's advertising space without content does not fulfil its purpose; a user who first fills it with their own offer remedies that deficiency and thereby contributes to the common good.

A user has a contribution of 1,000 POENs recorded for the first listing in which they offer a good or a service and which meets the content minimum established by the Terms of Use. This contribution is recorded at most once per user, regardless of the number of listings posted.

The listing is published immediately, while the contribution is noted at the moment of posting and is recorded in the Protocol when the Foundation approves the listing. This applies to every user, regardless of whether they are in the Kolo.

Until it is recorded, the contribution does not constitute a POEN record and does not enter the total number of recorded POENs in the system. The contribution is not recorded without the Foundation's approval; verification of the user in the chain of confirmations and a POEN ledger update in their favor are not grounds for recording.

The Foundation decides on approval within a reasonable time, by checking that the listing meets the content minimum, that it represents a genuine offer of a good or a service, and that it does not offer a prohibited good or service (Article 21 of the Terms of Use). The Foundation does not approve a listing offering a prohibited good or service, but removes it, stating the reason. If the contribution is not approved, the user is notified with a statement of reasons; the listing is not removed thereby, and the contribution is considered again in respect of a supplemented or a new listing by the same user.

Approval serves to ensure that the total number of recorded POENs does not grow before a trace of genuine participation appears in the system. By approving, the Foundation establishes that the condition is met — that the listing meets the content minimum and represents a genuine offer — and does not decide whether a contribution will be recorded for the user. An approved listing is at the same time one of the forms of contribution upon which the POEN recorded for verification is entered (Article 7 of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality), so a single decision enters several records.

If the listing is removed for a breach of the Terms of Use before the contribution has been recorded, the noted contribution is annulled. A contribution already recorded is not annulled by the removal of the listing.

The recording of a contribution to platform content is an automatic act of the Protocol and does not enter the daily limit established by the Rulebook on Operational Contribution and the Rulebook on Support Programmes.

For a user who was in the Kolo on the day this paragraph entered into force and whose contribution has been noted but not recorded, the contribution is recorded once, in the same amount.

### Article 40c

*Farm contribution*

The platform's advertising space fulfils its purpose only when it contains a standing offer of food and agricultural products. A farm that joins the system with such an offer fills that space and thereby contributes to the common good.

For the purposes of this Rulebook, a farm is a household that produces food or agricultural products, regardless of whether it is entered in the Register of Agricultural Holdings. A farm is not a user of the system, is not a collective form, and has no record in the Protocol.

When the Foundation approves a farm, a contribution is noted for the farm's members in the order in which they joined: 5,000 POENs for the first member, and 2,000 POENs each for the second to the sixth member. The contribution is noted for each member separately. A person has a contribution under this Article in respect of at most one farm: whoever leaves a farm that continues to exist retains the contribution noted, or recorded, for them under that farm, unless the farm later terminates because of a false declaration, and does not acquire it again in another farm; where their contribution has been annulled or written off upon the termination of the farm, the person may acquire it again in another farm, unless the farm terminated because of a false declaration.

The noted contribution is recorded in the member's record when both conditions are met: that the member has published a listing in which they offer a good or a service and which meets the content minimum established by the Terms of Use, and that they are in the Kolo. The listing condition is also met by a listing published before joining the farm, and also when it is no longer published; if a contribution for the first listing under Article 40a has already been recorded for the member, the farm contribution is recorded immediately, as soon as the other condition is also met. Until it is recorded, the contribution does not constitute a POEN record and does not enter the total number of recorded POENs in the system.

By approving, the Foundation establishes that the conditions are met — that the registration is complete, that the declaration of accuracy of data has been given, and that the listings accompanying the registration represent a genuine offer — and does not decide whether a contribution will be recorded for the member. The Foundation does not check data in state registers and relies on the applicant's declaration; no extract from the Register of Agricultural Holdings is attached to the registration.

When an approved farm terminates — because the Foundation establishes that the declaration of accuracy of data is false and annuls the farm's approval, or on another ground of termination established by the Rulebook on Farms — the noted contribution is annulled, and the contribution recorded under this Article is written off for all members of the farm at the moment of termination, on the ground under Article 14, paragraph 3, item 6. When a farm terminates because of a false declaration, the annulment and the write-off also affect every person who was a member of the farm during the period to which the false declaration relates, even if they have since left, and exclusion is imposed on the person who made the false declaration or knew of it, in accordance with the Rulebook on Farms; on other grounds of termination, the contribution of a member who left earlier is not affected. Each affected person is notified of the write-off and of the number of POENs written off.

The recording of a farm contribution is an automatic act of the Protocol and does not enter the daily limit established by the Rulebook on Operational Contribution and the Rulebook on Support Programmes.

The conditions of registration, the joining of members, listings on behalf of a farm, the map of local producers, the grounds for termination of a farm, and the procedure in the event of a false declaration are established by the Rulebook on Farms. The number of members and the amounts under this Article are amended under the procedure for amending this Rulebook.

## Chapter VII — System Governance

### Article 41

Every system has rules, and someone must set, change, and ensure their application.

The KOLO system resolves this through progressive decentralization — a structured pathway from centralized to decentralized governance with measurable transition conditions. Governance begins with the founder and the Foundation and is progressively transferred to the Community through the Upper Kolo.

The Upper Kolo is a special body of the Foundation established by this Rulebook, on the basis of the Management Board's power under the Statute to establish, by rulebook, advisory and other bodies that participate in the preparation of decisions, as well as mechanisms for gathering the opinions of users. It gives effect to the Foundation's purpose recorded in the Statute — the development of economic models based on democratic decision-making.

This chapter governs the two governance phases, the conditions for transition from one to the other, the composition and decision-making procedure of the Upper Kolo, the vote delegation mechanism, the Foundation's protective veto, and the limits of governance power. Voting procedures and the operational rules of the Upper Kolo are established by the Rulebook on the Upper Kolo.

### Article 42

System governance proceeds in two phases.

In Phase 1, the founder establishes the Protocol's rules in cooperation with the Foundation. Phase 1 lasts until the total number of POENs recorded in the system reaches 1,000,000.

In Phase 2, the Protocol rules are established on the proposal of the Upper Kolo, which the Management Board adopts by its own act (Article 51). The Foundation retains its service and executive role.

The transition from Phase 1 to Phase 2 occurs automatically upon reaching the threshold and does not require a special decision.

### Article 43

In Phase 1, the founder has discretion to change the system's rules and adjust parameters based on initial experience.

The founder's discretion is not unlimited. The founder may not amend the four principles, may not change the licenses under which the common good has been published, and may not appropriate the common good.

The limitations on the founder's discretion are embedded in this Rulebook as a normative act of the Foundation, and simultaneously in the technical architecture of the system.

The system's rules are established by acts of the Foundation adopted by its Management Board. The founder's discretion is exercised through proposing and participating in the work of the Foundation, in accordance with the Statute.

### Article 44

The threshold for transition from Phase 1 to Phase 2 is 1,000,000 recorded POENs in the system.

Reaching the threshold simultaneously activates the possibility of ZRNO inscription and establishes the Upper Kolo.

The Foundation's Management Board shall without delay adopt and publish an act recording that the conditions for the transition have been met. That act is declaratory — the transition occurs upon reaching the threshold, not upon the adoption of the act.

In the accounting logic, the threshold of one million recorded POENs corresponds to a Protocol record state of −1,000,000.

### Article 45

The Upper Kolo is the body of the community that establishes proposals on Protocol rules; it is composed of the Kolo keepers who have activated ZRNO. By activation, ZRNO turns into voting power; free ZRNO does not give a place in the Upper Kolo.

The composition of the Upper Kolo is not established by appointment, but by the fulfilment of the condition prescribed by this Rulebook: a Kolo keeper enters the composition of the Upper Kolo by activating ZRNO, and leaves it when none of their ZRNO is active any longer, or when their confirmation of reality falls (Article 30). A member without confirmation to whom ZRNO is inscribed is not part of the Upper Kolo until confirmed (Article 28a). Since the number of active ZRNOs and voting power change constantly, the composition of the Upper Kolo and the voting power of each Kolo keeper are determined from the Protocol's ledger separately for each accounting period and are valid only during that period.

The Upper Kolo is established upon reaching the threshold under Article 44 of this Rulebook. From then on it is composed of the Kolo keepers who have activated ZRNO, including the founders.

The Upper Kolo establishes by vote proposals on Protocol rules, on the activation and deactivation of modules, and on all matters affecting the common good, except matters excluded from its competence in accordance with this Rulebook. A proposal so established is a decision of the Upper Kolo within the meaning of this Rulebook; it is addressed to the Foundation's Management Board, which adopts it by its own act in the manner established by Article 51 of this Rulebook.

The Upper Kolo is not a legal entity and is not among the organs of the Foundation established by the Statute — the organs of the Foundation are the Management Board and the Director. The Upper Kolo does not represent the Foundation, does not dispose of its property, and does not assume obligations on its behalf. A decision of the Upper Kolo does not directly create rights or obligations towards third parties. The Upper Kolo does not govern the Foundation.

### Article 46

The Upper Kolo makes decisions by quadratic voting — a mechanism in which voting power equals the integer value of the square root of the Kolo keeper's active ZRNOs, rounded down.

Free ZRNO does not confer voting power. A Kolo keeper who wishes to vote must activate ZRNO, thereby relinquishing the possibility of write-off until they return it to the free state.

ZRNO is not spent by voting. Voting power arises from activated ZRNO, not from the number of POENs, and not from dinar donations.

The types of decisions, thresholds for decision-making, quorum, and voting procedure are established by the Rulebook on the Upper Kolo.

### Article 47

Kolo keepers who do not wish or are unable to participate in voting may delegate their votes to another Kolo keeper. A member without confirmation has no vote, and therefore does not delegate (Article 28a).

Delegation is general — the delegate votes on behalf of the delegator on all matters while the delegation lasts.

Delegated votes are added to the delegate's own votes.

The rules of delegation, including the effects of revocation and delegation limitations, are established by the Rulebook on the Upper Kolo.

### Article 48

The Foundation has the right to refuse the execution of an Upper Kolo decision that would jeopardize the operational and financial sustainability of the Foundation before it attains financial self-sufficiency — in particular decisions on the expenditure of dinar funds (including collective procurements) that would impair the Foundation's ability to cover basic costs and maintain the system's infrastructure.

The protective veto is not discretionary. The Foundation must justify every veto by reference to a specific threat to the Foundation's sustainability. A veto without justification is an abuse subject to liability under this Rulebook.

### Article 49

The protective veto is extinguished permanently and irreversibly when the Foundation's financial resources reach the threshold of financial self-sufficiency established by a special rulebook.

The extinguishment of the veto is irreversible.

The extinguishment of the veto does not abolish the statutory obligations of the Management Board.

### Article 50

The Upper Kolo does not have unlimited power. Three limitations are embedded in the system.

The first limitation consists of the four principles. No Upper Kolo decision may abolish non-convertibility, introduce property rights over records, make donations refundable, or abandon the principle of data minimization.

The second limitation is the Foundation's protective veto while in force, and the statutory obligations of the Management Board after the veto is extinguished.

The third limitation consists of the common-good licenses. The Upper Kolo may not replace AGPL-3.0 and CC BY-SA 4.0 with more restrictive licenses.

### Article 51

In Phase 2, the Foundation retains its service role — it provides and maintains the infrastructure, represents the system in legal transactions, and implements Upper Kolo decisions. The Foundation does not decide on Protocol rules at its own discretion, but acts upon the decision of the Upper Kolo, within the limits of the law and the Statute and with retention of the Management Board's statutory responsibilities.

A decision of the Upper Kolo is adopted and implemented by the Management Board through the adoption of a corresponding act. By this Rulebook the Management Board undertakes to adopt that act without delay and not to assess the expediency of the decision in doing so.

The Management Board may refuse to implement a decision only if the decision is contrary to law, contrary to the Foundation's Statute, directed at a matter excluded from the competence of the Upper Kolo under Article 50 of this Rulebook, or covered by the protective veto under Article 48 while it lasts. No other grounds for refusal exist and none may be established.

A refusal must be reasoned by reference to a specific ground from the preceding paragraph and is published alongside the decision to which it relates. The Upper Kolo may amend the decision so as to remove the established ground and decide upon it again.

In the act by which it adopts or refuses a decision of the Upper Kolo, as well as in the response to a recommendation, the Management Board states which of its members voted in the Upper Kolo on that decision or recommendation.

With regard to the distribution of dinar funds, the Upper Kolo issues recommendations to the Foundation's Management Board. The Management Board considers recommendations and applies them within its statutory powers under the Law on Endowments and Foundations, with an obligation to give a reasoned response to each recommendation.

### Article 51a

The Foundation's dinar funds in excess of operating costs may be allocated to projects for the benefit of the community.

Collective procurement is a project by which the Foundation acquires goods from a third party using dinar funds, and participants collect them in equal shares with the annulment of POEN records under Article 14a of this Rulebook.

The Upper Kolo decides on a project and on all of its parameters. The decision concerns the allocation of dinar funds and therefore has the nature of a recommendation to the Management Board of the Foundation within the meaning of Article 51 of this Rulebook, and is subject to the protective veto under Article 48.

Until the Upper Kolo is constituted in Phase 2, the decision is made by the Management Board of the Foundation, following the same procedure and with the same publication.

In relation to the third party from which the goods are acquired, the Foundation is the buyer and the contracting party. This provision does not affect the rule that the Foundation is not a party to an exchange between users.

The funds available for projects, the proposing and selection of the object of procurement, the collection of offers and the calculation, the application, the order and collection, as well as the amount and moment of annulment of records, are established by a separate rulebook.

### Article 52

In the event of the Foundation's dissolution, the common good does not cease to exist. The software and content remain available under the license terms.

The ledger and infrastructure are transferred to a legal successor that accepts the system's principles and assumes the obligation of safeguarding the common good.

The rules of transfer are established by the Statute and a special Foundation act.

## Chapter VIII — System Modules

### Article 53

The KOLO system separates the core system from modules. The core constitutes the minimal set of elements without which the system does not exist. Modules are extensions that add functionality to the core and are activated according to their own prerequisites.

This chapter governs modules at the framework level — the operational rules of each module are established by special rulebooks.

Among modules, a distinction is drawn between collective forms — Circle and Cooperative — as organizational units connecting users into more permanent structures, and other modules that govern special participation regimes.

### Article 54

A module is an extension that adds functionality to the core system.

The order in which modules are activated depends on the Community's needs and is not predetermined.

New modules may be added by decision of the Foundation in Phase 1, and of the Upper Kolo in Phase 2, provided they do not undermine the system's principles.

A decision of the Upper Kolo under the preceding paragraph is implemented in the manner established by Article 51 of this Rulebook.

If the activation of a module introduces new processing of personal data, the act activating the module is adopted following a prior update of the data protection impact assessment.

### Article 55

A Circle is a collective form based on shared interest or activity.

Circles are formed by the association of users. Existing associations and cooperatives registered under applicable laws may transfer their structure into a Circle that mirrors their composition and organization.

Circles have an incentive function through the growth mechanism — the Protocol records new POENs in accordance with the number of Circle members and the reaching of defined thresholds. POENs arising from this mechanism are recorded in the Circle's record as an organizational unit.

A Circle has no legal personality. An association or cooperative that forms a Circle retains its legal personality independently of the Circle.

The rules for the formation, functioning, and dissolution of Circles are established by a special rulebook.

### Article 56

A Cooperative is a collective form based on the territorial principle, registered under the Law on Cooperatives, with full legal personality.

A Cooperative within the system has two functions. The first is local coordination — the structure through which exchange, communication, and the organization of activities takes place in the territory it covers. The second is the growth mechanism — the reaching of user number thresholds is recorded in POENs in the Cooperative's record, by the same mechanism as for Circles.

The relationship between the Foundation and the Cooperative is governed by a cooperation agreement, with the Cooperative retaining full autonomy as an independent legal entity. The Cooperative does not become the owner of any part of the common good.

A Cooperative, as a legal entity registered under the Law on Cooperatives, has its own legal obligations — maintaining business books, annual reporting, and compliance with cooperative principles.

A Circle whose members register as a Cooperative continues as a Cooperative with the same record; a threshold of the number of members that has been reached is recorded only once.

A POEN ledger update from or to the Cooperative's record is made only on a ground established by a special rulebook; a ledger update without a ground is not permitted.

Upon termination of the Cooperative's participation in the system, the Cooperative's record is closed, and the POEN recorded in it is annulled by a counter-entry in favour of the Protocol. The annulment covers only the number of POEN present in the record, so the Cooperative's record thereby does not pass into a negative value, and the total number of recorded POENs decreases.

The conditions of participation, cooperative members, growth thresholds, grounds for entries in the record, distribution of goods, and cooperative contribution are established by the Rulebook on Cooperatives.

### Article 57

Social programmes are a mechanism for the automatic recording of POENs for groups of users whose structural participation in the common good the Protocol recognizes even though it is not expressed through individual activities.

The automatic recording of new POENs for qualifying groups has a redistributive effect: new records increase the total number of recorded POENs in the system, thereby changing the accounting coefficient for all users. This effect is a deliberate design decision — the system recognizes that participation that is by its nature continuous and diffuse cannot be recorded through individual activities.

The initial qualifying groups are mothers (primary caregivers), elderly users, users in difficult circumstances, and users in education. New groups may be added by decision of the Foundation in Phase 1, and of the Upper Kolo in Phase 2.

A participant who belongs to a qualifying group enters the data that confirms that status; the fulfilment of the conditions is confirmed by a Kolo member, and the application is approved by the Foundation, in accordance with a special rulebook. After approval, the Protocol automatically records new POEN entries for that support recipient without the need for a specific activity.

Automatic recording in social programmes is not remuneration — it pays for no work, service or other performance, nor is anything required of the support recipient in return towards the Foundation. The programs are carried out in pursuit of the objectives of the Foundation laid down in Article 6 of the Statute, in particular social protection and solidarity support for vulnerable social groups and support for the voluntary association of citizens for mutual aid, and they enable the support recipient to participate in the system on a more equal footing.

Social programmes require the verification of special categories of personal data within the meaning of the Law on Personal Data Protection. The legal basis for the processing of those data is the explicit consent of the support recipient. Consent may be withdrawn at any time, with the consequence of ceasing the automatic recording of POENs.

The qualifying groups, the conditions for status verification, and the recording parameters are established by a special rulebook. The activation of this module requires an update of the data protection impact assessment.

### Article 58

This module defines a special regime of rights, limitations, and protections for minor users of the system, with additional restrictions for persons under fifteen years of age in accordance with Article 16 of the Law on Personal Data Protection.

A minor may open an account independently, but until the consent of a parent or legal guardian the account has only a profile and friendships made by scanning a code in person, without the other system functions and without the recording of contributions (Article 4c of the Rulebook on the Participation of Children). Parental consent is a condition of participation, not a condition of opening an account.

A minor user has a limited scope of activities in the system. A minor user may not inscribe ZRNO or participate in governance through the Upper Kolo until the age of eighteen.

The processing of minors' data is subject to enhanced requirements. The consent of the parent or legal guardian is the legal prerequisite for processing.

The parent is liable for the actions of the minor user within the system.

The rules for access, the scope of permitted activities, exchange restrictions, the contribution under Article 15, item 9, and measures for the protection of minor users are established by the **Rulebook on the Participation of Children**. The activation of this module requires an update of the data protection impact assessment.

### Article 59

Internationalization is the infrastructural expansion of the system into new regions. The system expands its infrastructure, ledger, and rules to new territories, retaining a unified Protocol and a unified common-good ledger.

Expansion into new regions requires adaptations in several dimensions: the legal framework of the target jurisdiction, linguistic localization of the platform, establishment of a local chain of confirmations, and the possible formation of local cooperatives.

The prerequisite for activating this module is a stable system with an active Upper Kolo, sufficient experience with the system's operation in the primary region, and a legal analysis for the target jurisdictions. The decision to expand is made by the Upper Kolo.

Expansion into the territory of the European Union requires full compliance with the General Data Protection Regulation. Expansion into other jurisdictions requires analysis of local regulations on personal data protection, digital assets, foundations, and cooperatives.

## Chapter IX — Transparency and Protection of Personal Data

### Article 60

The KOLO system by its nature processes personal data — the verification graph, the contribution ledger, donation data, and, in the context of certain modules, special categories of data. At the same time, the system rests on the principle of data minimization as one of its four principles.

This chapter governs how the system approaches data protection: two design decisions that shape the protection architecture, the legal basis for processing and the Foundation's position as controller, the relationship between the right to erasure and the integrity of the ledger, the Foundation's obligations, and users' rights. The system applies the Law on Personal Data Protection.

The technical and organizational protection measures, the privacy policy, and the procedures for exercising users' rights are established by special acts.

### Article 61

Data protection in the system is based on data protection by design and by default.

The first design decision is pseudonymity of the ledger. Records in the ledger are tied to pseudonyms, not to personal names. The user's first and last name is kept only in the identity register (Article 31a), separately from the contribution ledger, and is not displayed to other users, except in the case under Article 31a paragraph 5. Pseudonymity is not anonymity — pseudonymized data remain personal data because they can, with additional information, be linked to an identified person.

The second design decision is data minimization. The Platform collects only the data necessary for the functioning of the system. A user may voluntarily enter additional data for easier use of the platform, but such entry is not a condition for proof of reality or for access to system functions.

### Article 62

The system processes users' personal data — data on platform users, proof-of-reality data, the identity register, voluntarily entered data, activity data, donation and sponsorship data, and, in the context of certain modules, special categories of data and data of minors.

The categories of data, their scope, purpose, and processing regime are established by the Privacy Policy and the Records of Processing Activities.

### Article 63

The KOLO Foundation is the data controller — it determines the purposes and means of processing. The Protocol is the technical means of processing. The processing of personal data is based on legal bases established by law; the legal basis for each category of data is established by the Privacy Policy and the Records of Processing Activities.

If the Foundation engages third parties for infrastructure maintenance, those parties are data processors. The relationship between the Foundation and processors is governed by a processing agreement in accordance with the law.

### Article 64

The user's right to erasure of personal data and the integrity of the common-good ledger are reconciled by the separation of identification and accounting data. The mechanics of pseudonymization upon status termination are governed by Article 34 of this Rulebook.

### Article 65

The Foundation is obliged to conduct a data protection impact assessment before commencing processing, to designate a data protection officer, and to apply technical and organizational protection measures proportionate to the risk.

The activation of modules that introduce the processing of special categories of data or data of minors requires an updated impact assessment before activation.

When the system infrastructure includes servers outside the Republic of Serbia, the transfer of personal data outside the country is subject to the rules on cross-border transfer in accordance with the law.

### Article 66

System users have all the rights guaranteed to them by law in connection with the protection of personal data. The Foundation provides an accessible mechanism for submitting requests and acts within the timeframes established by law.

The procedures for exercising users' rights are established by the Privacy Policy of the KOLO system.

### Article 67

The contribution ledger is public in pseudonymous form. Every user may verify records in the ledger and the consistency of the accounting framework.

The transparency of the ledger ensures user oversight of the system and reduces the possibility of manipulation.

The transparency of the ledger does not encompass data whose publication would infringe users' privacy — the verification graph, the identity register, voluntarily entered data, and special categories of data. The pseudonymous contribution ledger is accessible to participants. Visitors see general indicators (aggregates), while users additionally see the amounts and timestamps of transactions, without the pseudonyms of the parties and without record balances (Article 28; Article 14 of the Terms of Use). Exceptionally, a user sees the list of donations, in which the donor of a public donation is stated by first and last name, together with the amount and date, without the pseudonym (Article 5a of the Rulebook on Sponsorship and Donations). The viewing of listings in the platform's advertising space is governed separately (Art. 16) and is distinct from the contribution ledger. The scope of public data availability is established by the Privacy Policy.

A user may at all times see their own records and their own noted contribution not yet recorded (Article 40a). A noted contribution is not visible to other users and does not enter public aggregates until it is recorded.

## Chapter X — The Legal Nature of the KOLO System and the Four Principles

### Article 68

The KOLO system is not conceived or designed as a financial service, a payment service, or an investment scheme. This chapter governs what KOLO is and what it is not, and establishes the four principles that protect the legal position of the system.

The principles ensure that the system's accounting units cannot become money or a financial instrument, that the contribution ledger cannot become property that is traded, that donations cannot become the purchase of rights, and that the system does not collect data it does not need.

These principles are not rules that the system can change itself — they are the limits within which the system exists. Their amendment would change the legal nature of the system.

### Article 69

The KOLO system is a participatory common-good system based on the principles of social and solidarity economy and mutuality. The KOLO system is not a legal entity nor a special legal category.

The KOLO system is not a payment institution, an issuer of digital assets, a collective investment scheme, or an employer. The legal characterization of the system arises from its structural properties established by this Rulebook, in particular from the four principles and the rules on POEN, ZRNO, and operational contribution.

The relationship between users and the Foundation is a contractual relationship governed by the terms of use. The recording of POENs is not a counter-service for any obligation of the user towards the Foundation.

### Article 70

The legal position of the KOLO system rests on four principles: non-convertibility, the absence of property rights over records, the non-refundability of donations and automatic recording, and data minimization.

The four principles are structural limits of the system. They cannot be amended or abolished by any governance decision, including a decision of the Upper Kolo.

An amendment to this Rulebook that would amend or abolish any of the four principles is not permitted. A violation of any of the four principles changes the legal nature of the system.

The four principles are elaborated in the provisions that follow in this chapter.

### Article 71

No accounting unit of the KOLO system may be converted into money, currency, or any means with value outside the system, directly or indirectly.

The prohibition on conversion includes indirect conversion, including the exchange of an accounting unit for vouchers, gift cards, or other means with external value — those that can be turned into money, transferred to a third person, or used with a merchant of the holder's choice.

The distribution of vouchers from sponsorship in goods and services is not indirect conversion within the meaning of the preceding paragraph: the voucher is provided by the giver under an agreement for the benefit of third parties concluded with the Foundation, the giver does not receive POEN, the Foundation owns, procures and pays for nothing in the distribution, and the voucher is issued in the name of the holder and for a specified good or service with a single giver, and is neither transferable nor redeemable for money. The annulment of the record upon taking a voucher is an annulment upon use under Article 14a, and not an exchange of the record for a means with external value.

The Foundation does not redeem POENs or ZRNOs and does not provide for their conversion into dinars or any other means of payment.

### Article 72

Users do not have property rights over POEN and ZRNO records in the system's ledger. POEN and ZRNO records are data in the common-good ledger, not assets in the user's property.

POEN and ZRNO records are not transferred or inherited as property rights.

A recorded contribution and a recorded user position do not constitute a claim against the Foundation or a basis for a property claim.

### Article 73

A donation of money, goods, or services to the Foundation is non-refundable. The donor does not acquire, on the basis of the donation, a right to a refund, a governance right in the Foundation, or a stake in the system.

The recording of POENs is an automatic act of the Protocol executed pursuant to system rules. The recording of POENs is not a counter-service for a donation or for any other obligation of the user towards the Foundation.

There is no exchange agreement of dinars for POENs. The donation agreement does not contain provisions on POEN or on its recording.

The donor has no contractual or other claim against the Foundation for the recording of POENs. The recording of POENs is executed exclusively pursuant to system rules, independently of the act of donation.

### Article 74

Only data necessary for the functioning of the system are collected and processed in the system.

The Foundation does not store personal data of platform users in its own databases; such data are stored on the infrastructure on which the Protocol operates.

Data voluntarily submitted by a user for easier use of the platform may be amended or deleted by the user; their entry is not a condition for proof of reality or for access to system functions.

## Chapter XI — Liability, User Risks, and Dispute Resolution

### Article 75

Every system user assumes certain risks and bears certain responsibility. This chapter governs who is responsible for what in the system, what risks the user should understand before participating, and how disputes are resolved.

### Article 76

The Foundation is responsible for providing and maintaining the infrastructure, for the protection of personal data in accordance with the law, and for the application of system rules as established by the Rulebook.

The Foundation is not responsible for the performance of users' obligations in the exchange of goods and services. The Foundation does not mediate in exchange and is not a party to the legal relationship between users who exchange goods and services.

The Foundation does not guarantee that the system will function without interruption, that the infrastructure will not be affected by technical problems, or that the ledger will be protected from every security incident. The Foundation is obliged to take reasonable technical and organizational protection measures.

### Article 77

For performance, quality, and risk in the exchange of goods and services, the users who take part in it are responsible, pursuant to the general rules of the law of obligations.

The user is responsible for the accuracy of the data they submit to the system. A verifier in the chain of confirmations is responsible for the truthfulness of the verification in accordance with the proof-of-reality rules.

A user who abuses the system is subject to measures in accordance with the rules on status termination and suspension.

### Article 78

Users should understand the following risks before participating in the system:

POEN and ZRNO have no value outside the system and cannot be converted into money. A recorded contribution does not constitute a claim against the Foundation.

The accounting coefficient changes with activity in the system. A change in a Kolo keeper's position is not a return and is not guaranteed.

The Foundation may cease to exist. In that case, the obligation of safeguarding the common good and responsibility for infrastructure transfer to a legal successor in accordance with the Statute, but continuity of the system's functioning is not guaranteed.

The system may be affected by technical problems, security incidents, or regulatory changes that affect its functioning.

Donations to the Foundation are non-refundable, regardless of the further development of the system.

The exchange of goods and services within the system may have tax implications for the user. The Foundation does not provide tax advice. The user is responsible for their own tax obligations.

### Article 79

Disputes between users in connection with exchanges are resolved pursuant to the general rules of the law of obligations, before the competent court.

Disputes between users and the Foundation in connection with the application of system rules are resolved by agreement where possible. If agreement is not possible, the dispute is resolved before the competent court in Sombor, unless another local jurisdiction is determined by law.

Disputes in connection with the protection of personal data are resolved in accordance with the law, including the user's right to lodge a complaint with the Commissioner for Information of Public Importance and Personal Data Protection.

Internal dispute resolution mechanisms may be established by a special rulebook or a decision of the Upper Kolo.

### Article 80

This Rulebook is adopted and amended by the Management Board of the KOLO Foundation.

In Phase 1, the Management Board amends the Rulebook on the proposal of the founder or on its own initiative. In Phase 2, the Management Board amends the Rulebook on the basis of an Upper Kolo decision, in accordance with the governance rules established by this Rulebook.

An amendment to this Rulebook that would amend or abolish any of the four principles is not permitted.

Every amendment to the Rulebook is published to system users before it takes effect within a period that allows them to familiarize themselves with the amendments. The minimum publication period before taking effect is established by a special rulebook.

### Article 81

This Rulebook is the overarching operational act. Special rulebooks elaborate the individual areas to which this Rulebook refers and must be consistent with this Rulebook.

Special rulebooks are adopted and amended following the same procedure as this Rulebook, unless otherwise established by this Rulebook.

The Terms of Use govern the rules of platform access, the use of the exchange space, and other matters of technical use, and may not conflict with this Rulebook.

The Privacy Policy governs the procedures for exercising users' rights in connection with personal data protection and must be consistent with this Rulebook and with the law.

The hierarchy of acts, their mutual ranking, and the rules for resolving inconsistencies are governed by the Rulebook on the Hierarchy of Acts.

### Article 82 — Start of the system (transitional provision)

When the system is put into operation, there are no Kolo members who could confirm the reality of the first users, nor recorded POENs from which ZRNO could be inscribed. For this reason, the first Kolo keepers — the founders — are established directly: the founders are the initial Kolo keepers designated under Article 14 of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

The founders are exempt, as a one-time event of the start of the system, from the conditions for ZRNO inscription under Article 19 (the minimum of recorded POENs) and from the rules of the chain of confirmations under Article 32 and the Rulebook on Proof of Reality that presuppose the existence of a prior verifier. They constitute the origin of the chain of confirmations; their confirmation status from the establishment of the account and their exemption from supervision are governed by the Rulebook on Proof of Reality (Chapter VI — Initial Mechanism).

The founders are not a separate status within the meaning of Article 27: they are Kolo keepers with the attributes established by the Rulebook on Proof of Reality. The capacity of founder does not confer the powers of the Management Board nor make a person a member of the Management Board. Once the system is established, all new Kolo keepers acquire their status by the ordinary route (Art. 19, 29, and 30).

The circle of persons whose founding contribution is recorded and the distribution of that contribution are established by the Rulebook on Founding Contribution; they are founders within the meaning of that Rulebook, who are not equated with the founders under this Article.

### Article 83

This Rulebook enters into force on the day of its adoption by the Management Board of the KOLO Foundation.
