# Rulebook on the KOLO System

## I — General Provisions

### Article 1 — Subject matter

(1) This Rulebook governs the concepts, actors, contribution ledger, accounting framework, user statuses, contribution channels, governance and legal nature of the KOLO system.

(2) Individual matters are governed in more detail by the rulebooks to which this Rulebook refers, and access to and use of the platform by the Terms of Use.

### Article 2 — Meaning of terms

(1) Individual terms in this Rulebook and in the other acts of the KOLO system have the following meanings.

(2) The system and who decides in it:

1) **KOLO system**: a participatory common-good system; the functional whole comprising the common good, the actors and instruments of the system and users, organised for the purpose of recording contribution and participation in the common good. The KOLO system is not a legal entity nor a special legal category;

2) **common good**: the collective good of all users and the purpose of the KOLO system; it comprises the system software, the system rules, the contribution and participation ledger and content created in the system;

3) **KOLO Foundation (the Foundation)**: a non-profit private legal entity established under the Law on Endowments and Foundations, with its seat in Sombor; the legal instrument of the system, which safeguards the common good but is not its owner;

4) **Management Board**: the organ of the Foundation whose composition, mandate and competence are governed by the Statute; it adopts the acts of the KOLO system;

5) **Upper Kolo**: the body of the community composed of the Kolo keepers who have activated ZRNO; it decides on proposals on system rules, and the Management Board adopts the decision by its own act (Article 46). The Upper Kolo does not govern the Foundation;

6) **KOLO Protocol (the Protocol)**: the software that maintains the ledger, calculates the accounting coefficient and applies the system rules; it has no legal personality;

7) **KOLO Community (the Community)**: a descriptive term for all users of the system; it is not a legal entity, has no bodies or representatives and does not make decisions;

8) **Kolo**: the circle of users who have been confirmed or introduced into the Kolo in the chain of confirmations: members without confirmation, Kolo members and Kolo keepers. The Kolo is not a legal entity, and belonging to the Kolo is not membership in the Foundation or in any other legal entity.

(3) Users and statuses, in the order in which they are acquired:

1) **user**: any person with an account on the platform, regardless of status; a user of the Foundation's services within the meaning of the Statute. A user is a child, a participant or an adult user who is not in the Kolo; the latter has the status of user. The position of a child is governed by the Rulebook on the Participation of Children;

2) **participant**: a user who has been confirmed or introduced into the Kolo in the chain of confirmations: a member without confirmation, a Kolo member and a Kolo keeper, as well as a founder;

3) **member without confirmation**: an adult user whom someone has introduced into the Kolo, but who has not yet been confirmed by a Kolo member who knows them personally. They have the rights and obligations of a participant; provisions of the Foundation's acts concerning a Kolo member, or a confirmed user, do not apply to them, unless an act expressly provides otherwise;

4) **Kolo member**: a user whose reality has been confirmed in the chain of confirmations by a confirmation, in accordance with the Rulebook on Proof of Reality. Only a Kolo member, including a Kolo keeper and a founder, is a confirmed user;

5) **Kolo keeper**: a Kolo member to whom ZRNO has been inscribed in the Protocol, as well as a founder;

6) **founders**: the initial Kolo keepers (Article 58 of this Rulebook and Article 29 of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality). This term is distinct from the founder of the Foundation and from founders within the meaning of the Rulebook on Founding Contribution;

7) **pseudonym**: the account name under which the user is visible in the system and in the ledger;

8) **place**: a settlement from the register of settlements that the user states in their account; a cooperative operates in the place it has determined.

(4) Proof of reality:

1) **proof of reality**: the manner of establishing the reality and continuity of a user based on personal acquaintance or on a telephone conversation with a user from the call list (Article 30);

2) **verification**: the collective term for confirmation and introduction into the Kolo, by which the reality of a user is established once in the chain of confirmations;

3) **chain of confirmations**: the mechanism of proof of reality in which Kolo members confirm the reality of users whom they know personally, or introduce into the Kolo users from the call list after a telephone conversation, while members without confirmation introduce into the Kolo users whom they know personally;

4) **confirmation**: a verification by which a Kolo member, or a founder, confirms the reality of a user whom they know personally. The act by which a member without confirmation is confirmed under the Rulebook on Proof of Reality is also called a confirmation; it does not add a new step in the chain of confirmations;

5) **introduction into the Kolo**: a verification by which a Kolo member, after a telephone conversation, introduces into the Kolo a user from the call list, or by which a member without confirmation introduces into the Kolo a user whom they know personally. Responsibility for truthfulness and the recording of POEN are the same as for a confirmation.

(5) The ledger:

1) **POEN**: the internal accounting unit of the system; a record in the Protocol's ledger by which contribution and other forms of participation in the common good are recorded;

2) **re-registration of POEN**: a ledger update between users by which the record of one user is decreased and the record of another is increased by the same amount; the total number of POEN does not change thereby;

3) **noted contribution**: a contribution whose ground has arisen, but the condition for entry has not yet been met; it is not a POEN record and does not enter the total number of recorded POEN;

4) **recorded contribution**: a contribution entered in the Protocol's ledger as a POEN record;

5) **zero-sum rule**: the property of the system whereby the sum of all POEN records, including the Protocol's record, equals zero at all times;

6) **automatic recording**: the recording of POEN as an automatic act of the Protocol executed pursuant to the system rules, without a discretionary decision by any actor;

7) **ZRNO**: the internal accounting unit of the system by which a participant's position in the common good is recorded in the Protocol's ledger;

8) **accounting coefficient**: the numerical ratio of the total number of recorded POEN to the number of ZRNO available for inscription in the Protocol;

9) **accounting period**: the period at the end of which the Protocol calculates the accounting coefficient and applies the rules of inscription, write-off and change of state of ZRNO.

(6) Contribution and governance:

1) **founding contribution**: work performed before the platform was opened whose contribution is subsequently recorded in POEN; one-time and time-limited;

2) **financial contribution**: a dinar inflow to the Foundation on the basis of donations from natural persons and sponsorship in money;

3) **operational contribution**: an activity outside the platform whose contribution is recorded in POEN after confirmation of completion;

4) **sponsor**: a legal entity or entrepreneur that supports the Foundation's work by donating money or by an agreement on sponsorship in goods and services for the benefit of users, as well as a holder of a registered agricultural holding who does so; a sponsor is not a user of the system and does not receive POEN or ZRNO;

5) **cooperative**: a cooperative registered under the Law on Cooperatives which, on the basis of a cooperation agreement, participates in the system in the place in which it operates; it is an independent legal entity;

6) **square-root voting**: the manner of decision-making in the Upper Kolo in which voting power equals the integer value of the square root of the number of active ZRNO;

7) **module**: an extension of the system introduced under Article 49.

(7) Terms used in the masculine grammatical gender in the acts of the KOLO system apply equally to persons of male and female gender.

(8) Terms not defined in this Article have the meaning established in the relevant articles of this Rulebook or in separate rulebooks.

## II — The Common Good and the Actors

### Article 3 — Common good

(1) No user, nor the founder of the Foundation, nor the Foundation has ownership rights over the common good or the right to dispose of it.

(2) The common good does not constitute collective ownership within the meaning of applicable property law categories. The user's relationship to the common good is participatory: a right to use and to contribute, not a right to dispose.

### Article 4 — Infrastructure

(1) The infrastructure on which the system operates (servers, databases, network equipment and other technical resources) is not a constituent part of the common good. The ledger as a record and the content belong to the common good; the database as a storage medium belongs to the infrastructure.

(2) The provision and maintenance of the infrastructure is a service obligation of the Foundation. The Foundation selects, changes and upgrades the infrastructure; no such change affects the status or integrity of the common good.

### Article 5 — Licences and brand

(1) The system software is licensed under the GNU Affero General Public License, version 3.0 (AGPL-3.0).

(2) Content created in the system is licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International licence (CC BY-SA 4.0).

(3) The moral rights of authors of software and content remain intact and are not transferred.

(4) The software and content of the common good may not be re-licensed under more restrictive terms; only replacement with a licence of an equivalent copyleft, or share-alike, regime is permitted.

(5) The system brand (the name "KOLO", the logo, the mark and the domains) is not part of the common good and is under the exclusive control of the Foundation.

### Article 6 — Contributions to software and content

(1) Contributions to the software are accepted under the terms of the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO), by which the contributor confirms the right to contribute the code under the common-good licence.

(2) Contributions to content are accepted upon acceptance of the common-good licence.

(3) Contributions belong to the common good.

### Article 7 — Foundation

(1) The Foundation safeguards the common good, but is the owner neither of the common good nor of the system.

(2) The Foundation provides and maintains the infrastructure, receives dinar donations, finances the operating costs of the system and projects for the benefit of the community, and represents the system in legal transactions.

(3) The Foundation does not acquire POEN or ZRNO and does not participate in the accounting framework of the system. The Foundation's relationship with the system is exclusively in dinar funds.

(4) Actions of the Foundation's administrator team in the ledger and in work with users are recorded in an audit log. The work of the administrator team is supervised by the Management Board.

(5) The Foundation publicly discloses its inflows and costs in dinars, including the costs of projects, separately from the POEN ledger.

### Article 8 — Protocol

(1) All Protocol operations are deterministic and algorithmic, without discretion. The Protocol cannot act outside the system rules.

(2) The Protocol does not conclude contracts, does not hold accounts, does not enter into legal relationships with users and is not a controller of personal data.

## III — Legal Nature, Principles and Limits of the Common Good

### Article 9 — Legal nature

(1) The KOLO system is a participatory common-good system based on the principles of social and solidarity economy and mutuality. The KOLO system is not a legal entity nor a special legal category.

(2) The KOLO system is not a payment institution, an issuer of digital assets, a collective investment scheme or an employer. The legal characterisation of the system arises from its structural properties established by this Rulebook, in particular from the principles and limits of the common good (Article 10) and the rules on POEN, ZRNO and operational contribution.

(3) The relationship between the user and the Foundation is a contractual relationship governed by the Terms of Use. The recording of POEN is not a counter-performance for any obligation of the user towards the Foundation.

### Article 10 — Principles and limits of the common good

(1) The legal nature of the KOLO system rests on four principles: non-convertibility (Article 11), the absence of property rights over records (Article 12), the non-refundability of donations (Article 13) and automatic recording (Article 14).

(2) The common good has two limits: data minimisation and the common-good licences (Article 15).

(3) The principles and limits are not rules that the system can change itself, but the framework within which it exists. They cannot be amended or abolished by any governance decision, including a decision of the Upper Kolo. An amendment of any of the principles would mean a system of a different legal nature, and an amendment of any of the limits a different common good.

### Article 11 — Non-convertibility

(1) No accounting unit of the KOLO system may be converted into money, currency or any means with value outside the system, directly or indirectly.

(2) The prohibition of conversion includes indirect conversion, including the exchange of an accounting unit for vouchers, gift cards or other means with external value, that is, means that can be turned into money, transferred to a third person or used with a merchant of the holder's choice.

(3) The distribution of vouchers from sponsorship in goods and services is not indirect conversion, because the voucher lacks the elements of a means with external value referred to in paragraph 2:

1) it cannot be turned into money;

2) it is not transferred, because it is issued in the name of the holder;

3) it is not used with a merchant of the holder's choice, because it is issued for a specified good or service with a single giver;

4) it is provided by the giver under an agreement for the benefit of third parties concluded with the Foundation; the giver does not receive POEN, and in the distribution the Foundation owns nothing, procures nothing and pays for nothing.

(4) The annulment of a record upon taking a voucher is an annulment upon use under Article 18, and not an exchange of the record for a means with value.

(5) The Foundation does not redeem POEN or ZRNO and does not provide for their conversion into dinars or any other means of payment.

### Article 12 — Absence of property rights

(1) Users do not have property rights over POEN and ZRNO records in the system's ledger. POEN and ZRNO records are data in the common-good ledger, not assets in the user's property.

(2) POEN and ZRNO records are not transferred or inherited as property rights.

(3) A recorded contribution and a recorded position do not constitute a claim against the Foundation or a basis for a property claim.

### Article 13 — Non-refundability of donations

(1) A donation of money to the Foundation and sponsorship in goods and services are non-refundable. On the basis of the donation, the donor acquires neither a right to a refund, nor a governance right in the Foundation, nor a stake in the system.

(2) The Foundation spends money received through donations and sponsorship on the objectives set out in the Statute and does not keep it as cover for anyone's record (Article 16 paragraph 4 point 7). A donation is not a deposit: it is not returned, bears no interest, and the Foundation does not hold it for the donor.

### Article 14 — Automatic recording

(1) The recording of POEN is an automatic act of the Protocol executed pursuant to the system rules. The recording of POEN is not a counter-performance for a donation or for any other obligation of the user towards the Foundation and does not establish a contractual relationship between the donor and the Foundation with respect to POEN.

(2) There is no agreement on the exchange of dinars for POEN. The donation agreement does not determine POEN or its recording and contains neither a number of POEN, nor a recording coefficient, nor a tier; it contains only a statement that the contribution is recorded under the system rules, independently of the agreement, that this entry is not a counter-performance and that the donor has no claim to it.

(3) The donor has no contractual or other claim against the Foundation for the recording of POEN. The recording of POEN is executed exclusively pursuant to the system rules, independently of the act of donation.

### Article 15 — Limits of the common good

(1) The first limit is data minimisation: only data necessary for the functioning of the system are collected and processed in the system.

(2) Personal data are stored on the infrastructure provided by the Foundation (Article 4), with processors with whom the Foundation concludes a processing agreement.

(3) Data that a user submits voluntarily for easier use of the platform may be amended or deleted by the user; their entry is not a condition for proof of reality or for access to system functions.

(4) The second limit is the common-good licences (Article 5): the software and content may not be re-licensed under more restrictive terms.

## IV — POEN and the Contribution Ledger

### Article 16 — POEN

(1) POEN is not money and cannot be converted into money.

(2) New POEN records are inscribed exclusively by the Protocol through the contribution recording channels.

(3) POEN has no bearer; it exists solely as a record in the Protocol's ledger. POEN amounts are expressed in whole numbers. POEN does not represent money, currency, electronic money, a means of payment, digital assets, a financial instrument or a security. POEN cannot be converted into a means with value outside the system.

(4) The provision of paragraph 3 is not merely a label but a consequence of the properties of the record. Users re-register POEN to one another on the occasion of the exchange of goods and services; beyond that, POEN does not meet the elements by which digital assets, that is a virtual currency, are determined:

1) it cannot be acquired by purchase from the Foundation: the Foundation does not sell it and does not issue it for consideration, and the entry of POEN is not a counter-performance for any giving whatsoever;

2) alienation for consideration is prohibited: offering, buying, selling and brokering the trade of POEN for value outside the system are prohibited by the Terms of Use and entail the measures under those Terms; if they nevertheless occur, they occur outside the platform, without any protection and without effect in the system;

3) it is neither redeemed nor converted (Article 11), so there is no exit from it in money;

4) the Foundation does not determine its price and does not offer it on a market: it publishes no ratio of POEN to the dinar or to any other currency and applies no such ratio in any of its own procedures. The amount stated in a listing is set by the user themselves (Terms of Use); if in doing so they start from a value expressed in money, that is their own estimate, which binds neither the Foundation nor the other party;

5) it does not exist outside the Protocol's ledger and cannot be transferred to a record that the Protocol does not keep;

6) it does not serve the settlement of monetary obligations and cannot be offered in place of money;

7) there are no monetary funds behind the record: the Foundation holds no funds whatsoever as cover for POEN and does not owe them to the holder of the record; money received through donations and sponsorship is spent on the objectives set out in the Statute, and its spending changes no record in the ledger.

(5) No one is obliged to accept POEN. The Foundation does not accept POEN, expresses none of its own amounts in it and publishes no price list in POEN. The amount in POEN in an exchange is set by the parties themselves and binds no other user. The number of POEN annulled in the distribution of goods in the Foundation's projects, including collective procurement and the distribution of vouchers from sponsorship in goods and services, is determined from the recorded contribution under the rules of that distribution; it is not the price of a good or service, does not express their value and does not constitute a price list.

(6) POEN cannot be acquired for the purpose of performing an exchange. A contribution on the basis of a donation is not recorded at the moment of payment: recording presupposes that the Foundation has first established the fact of receipt of the payment and the person with whose funds it was made (Article 36).

(7) On account of POEN, the Foundation provides no payment services and does not issue POEN as electronic money. The elements by which a payment service and electronic money are determined are missing:

1) a POEN record is not a claim either against the Foundation or against any other person (Article 12);

2) POEN is not issued in exchange for a payment of monetary funds: a contribution on the basis of a donation is recorded under the system rules, independently of the act of donation, and is not a counter-performance for the payment (Article 14);

3) in no ledger update does the Foundation receive, hold or transfer users' monetary funds, and the update concerns not monetary funds but a record in the ledger kept by the Protocol;

4) the Foundation charges nothing for the re-registration of POEN and receives no fee whatsoever for it;

5) there is no payout, redemption or conversion from a POEN record (Article 11);

6) a person who has no record in the Protocol's ledger does not receive POEN, and neither does the giver of a voucher from sponsorship in goods and services receive POEN: the giver performs its own agreement with the Foundation for the benefit of third parties.

(8) The properties set out in the preceding paragraphs also have a consequence with regard to the notion of a user's income. By the entry of POEN the user receives neither money nor goods; nothing passes from the Foundation's assets into the user's assets, and the record so created cannot be turned into money, assigned as a claim or collected. The re-registration of a record to another user on the occasion of an exchange is not an assignment: the record is not a claim; rather, the Protocol updates the ledger. The Foundation makes no payout on any basis by reason of the entry. It does not determine or publish a value of POEN expressed in money and applies no such ratio, so no amount expressed in money is determined on that basis.

### Article 17 — Zero-sum rule and negative record

(1) The sum of all POEN records in the system, including the Protocol's record, equals zero at all times. The Protocol maintains a record that is negative at all times and represents the counter-entry for all POEN recorded with users and cooperatives, so every recorded contribution has an exact counter-entry. The Protocol's record is the bookkeeping counterpart of recorded contribution, and not an obligation: in it there is no debtor or creditor, no term, no collection, no interest and no cover, and it decreases only through the annulment of records.

(2) No user or cooperative may have a negative POEN record. There are eight exceptions, and they are listed here exhaustively. In each of them the write-off, or the correction, is executed even where the record thereby becomes negative. Each person returns solely what was recorded to them on that ground, and the burden is not transferred to another person, except in the case under point 1:

1) restitution upon annulment of a false verification: the uncovered part of the annulled records is transferred to the person who confirmed the account or introduced it into the Kolo (Rulebook on Proof of Reality, Article 36);

2) correction of the ledger upon a complaint concerning an unfulfilled exchange, in the amount of the entire re-registration (Article 20 paragraph 5);

3) write-off of a contribution from the children's space upon the ending of a friendship and upon attaining the age of majority (Rulebook on the Participation of Children);

4) write-off upon the conversion of an adult account into a minor's account, including the contribution that had been recorded to other persons on the occasion of the confirmations and introductions into the Kolo that fall with that conversion (Rulebook on the Participation of Children, Articles 15 and 16);

5) write-off upon the alignment of existing confirmations (Rulebook on Proof of Reality, Article 39);

6) write-off upon the termination of a farm, on any ground of termination, to every member of the farm at the moment of termination, and in the case of a false declaration also to a person who was a member during the period to which the declaration relates (Article 39 and the Rulebook on Farms);

7) write-off upon non-performance of sponsorship in goods and services, to the user who submitted the application, for each voucher that the giver did not perform (Rulebook on Sponsorship and Donations, Article 35);

8) write-off upon establishing a multiple account: from the account that the person retains, the net amount of re-registrations from the account that ceases is written off, while the records of other persons are not affected (Rulebook on Proof of Reality, Article 14).

(3) The same regime applies to a negative record on each of the eight grounds: it is not a debt and is not collected, POEN that subsequently accrue to the user fill it first, re-registration in favour of another user is possible only once the record goes above zero, and the exchange of goods and services is not restricted. A user whose record becomes negative is always notified of this, together with the ground and the amount.

(4) No further ground for a negative record may be established, neither by this Rulebook without amending this Article, nor by any other act.

(5) The exchange of goods and services moves existing POEN records and does not change their total number in the system. The total number of POEN increases through the inscription of new records via the contribution recording channels and through the correction of an annulment effected without grounds (Article 18), and decreases through the annulment of records on the grounds established by this Rulebook. There is no further ground for increasing the total number of POEN.

### Article 18 — Annulment upon use

(1) A POEN record is also annulled when a user uses it to participate in a project of the Foundation, under the conditions of the Rulebook on Projects and Collective Procurement.

(2) Annulment upon use is not a measure against the user. It does not presuppose any irregularity, is not based on the termination of user status, and differs from the annulment of records under Article 33 and from annulment under Chapter IX of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

(3) Annulment upon use is not consideration for a good: POEN returns to the Protocol, not to the Foundation, and the Foundation receives nothing on that ground. The annulment is effected by a counter-entry in favour of the Protocol; the sum of all records continues to equal zero, while the total number of recorded POEN decreases.

(4) Through annulment upon use, a user's record cannot pass into a negative value; only the number of POEN present in the record is annulled. This ground is not an exception to the prohibition of a negative record under Article 17 paragraph 2.

(5) Where it is established that the condition of use was not met (that the good was not collected, that the collected good was not usable for the purpose for which it was procured, or that the voucher was not performed), the annulment is corrected by a counter-entry in favour of the user, in the number of POEN that had been annulled. The correction is not the inscription of a new record via a channel under Article 19, but the removal of an annulment effected without grounds. The correction is not a payout: on account of it the user receives no value other than the record that had been annulled, and the Foundation neither pays out nor receives anything on that ground.

### Article 19 — Contribution recording channels

(1) The Protocol inscribes new POEN records through the following contribution recording channels:

1) operational contribution (Article 34 and the Rulebook on Operational Contribution);

2) confirmation and introduction into the Kolo (Rulebook on Proof of Reality);

3) financial contribution (Article 36 and the Rulebook on Sponsorship and Donations);

4) sponsorship (Article 37 and the Rulebook on Sponsorship and Donations);

5) growth of cooperatives (Article 50 and the Rulebook on Cooperatives);

6) support programmes (Article 51 and the Rulebook on Support Programmes);

7) founding contribution (Article 35 and the Rulebook on Founding Contribution);

8) contribution for a first listing (Article 38);

9) contribution of children in the children's space (Article 52 and the Rulebook on the Participation of Children);

10) farm contribution (Article 39 and the Rulebook on Farms).

(2) The channels under points 1 and 6 are subject to a common daily limit established by the Rulebook on Operational Contribution. The other channels are limited by their own measure: the channels under points 2, 8 and 10 record a contribution once per person; the channels under points 3 and 4 by the amount of funds actually received, or by the value of the goods under the agreement; the channel under point 5 by the closed table of growth thresholds; the channel under point 7 by the upper limit of founding contribution; and the contribution under point 9 is written off upon the child attaining the age of majority. The total number of POEN arising outside the daily limit is thereby tied to the number of real persons, to the funds received and to pre-determined limits.

(3) Under the channel under point 2, the contribution is entered when the participant makes their first confirmed contribution, under the conditions of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality; until entered, the contribution is noted and does not enter the total number of recorded POEN.

(4) Recording under the channels under points 3, 4, 8, 9 and 10 is an automatic act of the Protocol and does not count towards the daily limit referred to in paragraph 2.

### Article 20 — Re-registration and exchange

(1) Users within the system exchange goods and services. An exchange is recorded by the re-registration of POEN: the record of the user providing the good or service is increased, and the record of the receiving user is decreased by the same amount.

(2) Exchange is not a payment transaction. The re-registration of POEN is not a transfer of monetary value and does not constitute a payment within the meaning of payment services regulations. The Foundation and the Protocol do not mediate in the exchange and are not responsible for the performance of users' obligations.

(3) Re-registration is initiated by the user, under the conditions of the Terms of Use and the Rulebook on the Participation of Children.

(4) A re-registration carried out for the purpose of a transaction by which POEN is offered, bought, sold or brokered for value outside the system is prohibited and entails the measures established by the Terms of Use; such a re-registration is not an exchange of goods and services. The Foundation does not broker exchanges of POEN for money, does not organise them and bears no responsibility for them. The re-registration of POEN between users serves the exchange of products, services and knowledge and other forms of reciprocity within the community.

(5) When, upon a complaint by the user who initiated the re-registration, the Foundation establishes that the exchange has not been fulfilled, the Protocol corrects the ledger by a counter-entry in the amount of the entire re-registration, in favour of the complainant, even where the recipient's record thereby becomes negative (Article 17 paragraph 2 point 2). The correction of the ledger is neither a refund nor a payout: the Foundation pays out nothing and receives nothing on that ground, does not become a party to the exchange and does not assume responsibility for the performance of users' obligations. The complaint procedure is governed by the Terms of Use.

(6) The recording of an exchange in POEN does not change the tax or other public-law characterisation of the transaction between users. A user who offers a good or service within the scope of their business activity acts in accordance with the regulations applicable to that activity.

(7) The rules of the platform's advertising space (Pijaca), including the visibility of listings and of advertisers' pseudonyms and access to contact details, are governed by the Terms of Use.

## V — ZRNO and the Accounting Framework

### Article 21 — ZRNO

(1) The position that ZRNO records arises from the participant's contribution and gives them a vote in decisions about system rules. ZRNO does not represent a share, a stock, a security, a collective investment unit, an investment contract, digital assets or a digital token, because it does not represent any property right, and carries no dividend, interest, return or right to liquidation proceeds.

(2) The total number of ZRNO is 1,000,000 and can be neither increased nor decreased. At any given time, one part of the ZRNO is inscribed with participants and another part is available for inscription in the Protocol; the sum of inscribed and available ZRNO always equals the total number.

(3) ZRNO is inscribed and written off exclusively by the Protocol, at the participant's request and pursuant to the system rules. A vote in the Upper Kolo arises not from inscribed but from activated ZRNO of a Kolo keeper whose reality has been confirmed (Article 43); ZRNO is not in itself a voting right, but a record of position from which that right arises.

### Article 22 — Inscription of ZRNO

(1) ZRNO is inscribed from Phase 2 (Article 41), under the same conditions for all participants, including the founders.

(2) ZRNO is inscribed from the recorded contribution in the participant's record, irrespective of whether that contribution was recorded through a channel under Article 19 or the record was increased by the exchange of goods and services. By inscribing ZRNO a Kolo member becomes a Kolo keeper.

(3) ZRNO is inscribed by a participant; a user who is not in the Kolo does not inscribe ZRNO. A member without confirmation does not activate inscribed ZRNO and does not become a Kolo keeper by inscription until confirmed (Article 28 paragraph 3).

(4) ZRNO may be inscribed while the participant's record amounts to at least 20,000 POEN. In one accounting period, a participant may use at most 1% of their recorded POEN for ZRNO inscription.

(5) Inscription is initiated by the participant. The Protocol checks whether the conditions are met and executes the inscription if they are met; inscription is not a discretionary decision and no actor in the system approves or rejects it. Upon inscription, the Protocol reduces that participant's recorded POEN pursuant to the accounting coefficient applicable for the current accounting period.

### Article 23 — States and write-off of ZRNO

(1) Inscribed ZRNO is free or active. Free ZRNO is inscribed ZRNO without a governance function. Active ZRNO is ZRNO that a Kolo keeper has activated for participation in governance, and it grants the right to vote in the Upper Kolo.

(2) A Kolo keeper may activate free ZRNO or write it off, that is, return it to the Protocol. A member without confirmation to whom ZRNO is inscribed may write off free ZRNO, but may not activate it. Active ZRNO cannot be written off until the Kolo keeper returns it to the free state.

(3) Upon write-off, the Protocol records POEN to the participant writing off the ZRNO pursuant to the accounting coefficient applicable for the current accounting period. A Kolo keeper whose ZRNO has been entirely written off becomes a Kolo member.

(4) The difference between the accounting coefficient at the time of inscription and at the time of write-off is not a return. It arises from a formula that the Protocol applies without discretion, it may arise in either direction, and no actor pays it out or guarantees it. POEN recorded upon write-off have the same status as all other POEN.

(5) Transitions between ZRNO states and their effects occur at the end of the accounting period.

### Article 24 — Non-transferability of ZRNO

ZRNO is not transferred to another user, neither on its own nor together with the account. There is no market for ZRNO, there is no price for it, and there is no possibility of trading it.

### Article 25 — Accounting coefficient

(1) The accounting coefficient indicates how many POEN are needed to inscribe one ZRNO in a given accounting period. It is calculated by the Protocol, automatically and without discretion.

(2) The accounting coefficient changes with the total number of recorded POEN: the inscription of new POEN records shifts it upward, and the annulment of recorded POEN shifts it downward. The coefficient is not predetermined to rise.

(3) The inscription and write-off of ZRNO do not shift the accounting coefficient. Upon inscription, alongside the number of ZRNO available in the Protocol, the total number of recorded POEN is reduced in the same proportion, because the POEN used for the inscription return to the Protocol; upon write-off, both quantities increase in the same ratio. A Kolo keeper cannot shift the coefficient by their own inscription or write-off. The exchange of goods and services between users does not change the coefficient.

(4) The accounting coefficient is an administrative variable. It is not a price, not an exchange rate and not a performance index; it does not express the value of ZRNO or POEN outside the system.

### Article 26 — Accounting period

The accounting period lasts 24 hours, from midnight to midnight, Serbian time. At the end of the accounting period, the Protocol calculates the accounting coefficient, applies the rules of ZRNO inscription and write-off and executes the transitions between ZRNO states.

### Article 27 — The position of a Kolo keeper is not an investment

(1) The position of a Kolo keeper does not constitute an investment. That assessment rests neither on the name of the unit nor on a declaration that ZRNO is not an investment instrument, but on the absence in it of the elements that make an investment an investment:

1) ZRNO is not acquired by investing funds: it is inscribed from a contribution already recorded in the participant's record; the Foundation does not sell ZRNO, does not offer it and does not set a price for it, inscription is not a counter-performance, and no price of ZRNO exists (Article 24);

2) there is no return that could be realised: a change in position is expressed exclusively in POEN, which have no value outside the system and are neither redeemed nor converted, directly or indirectly (Article 11), so the difference in the accounting coefficient cannot leave the Protocol's ledger;

3) there is no effort of another person directed at creating a benefit for the Kolo keeper: the accounting coefficient is the quotient of two sums from the ledger, which the Protocol calculates automatically and without discretion (Article 25); no person manages it or promises it, and the Kolo keeper does not shift it either by their own inscription or by their own write-off;

4) the Kolo keeper is not separated from governance: the vote in the Upper Kolo arises from activated ZRNO (Article 43), so the Kolo keeper takes part in establishing the rules of the system themselves. Whoever wants a vote must activate ZRNO and thereby forgo write-off until it is returned to the free state (Article 44); the governance function and accounting flexibility are mutually exclusive.

(2) Points 1–3 of paragraph 1 apply to a member without confirmation to whom ZRNO is inscribed: they may write off free ZRNO, but do not govern through it, and they acquire a vote not through a stake but through confirmation, which is not obtained with funds and does not depend on the number of inscribed ZRNO. A user to whom ZRNO is inscribed but who is no longer in the Kolo holds the ZRNO but does not write it off (Article 29).

## VI — User Statuses, Proof of Reality and Termination of Status

### Article 28 — Statuses

(1) An adult user holds one of four statuses: user, member without confirmation, Kolo member or Kolo keeper. A minor user does not hold a status under this Article; their position is governed by the Rulebook on the Participation of Children.

(2) The rights and restrictions according to status are established by the Terms of Use and the Rulebook on Proof of Reality. For a user who is not in the Kolo, contribution is recorded only through the channels under points 3, 4 and 8 of Article 19.

(3) A member without confirmation becomes a Kolo member when confirmed, in accordance with the Rulebook on Proof of Reality; never automatically. The confirmation of a member without confirmation does not add a new step in the chain of confirmations and no POEN is recorded for it. The activation of ZRNO and the rights arising from activation are bound to confirmed status.

### Article 29 — ZRNO upon change of status

(1) A Kolo keeper whose confirmation is annulled, and who has not remained introduced into the Kolo, becomes a user. Their ZRNO remains inscribed and frozen: until they are again confirmed or introduced into the Kolo, they do not activate it, do not write it off, do not delegate a vote on its basis and are not part of the composition of the Upper Kolo. When their reality is confirmed again, they are again a Kolo keeper, and if they are only introduced into the Kolo, they hold the position of a member without confirmation to whom ZRNO is inscribed. The same applies to a member without confirmation to whom ZRNO is inscribed and whose introduction into the Kolo is annulled.

(2) A Kolo keeper whose confirmation alone is annulled, while their introduction into the Kolo remains valid, becomes a member without confirmation: their ZRNO remains inscribed, and active ZRNO passes into the free state; they may activate it again when confirmed.

### Article 30 — Proof of reality

(1) Proof of reality confirms that the user is a real person and that they are continuously the same person. Uniqueness (one person, one account) is not confirmed, because the person who confirms knows the individual, not their other accounts; a multiple account is established by the Foundation under the Rulebook on Proof of Reality, on the basis of a comparison of data and of contacting the person.

(2) A user's reality is confirmed exclusively through the chain of confirmations. Reality is established by one person, once: by a confirmation or by an introduction into the Kolo. A user is either in the Kolo or not; belonging to the Kolo has no degrees, and the difference between a confirmation and an introduction into the Kolo determines only the actions that the Foundation's acts attach to a Kolo member.

(3) A payment, sponsorship and the registration of a farm do not confirm reality. On their basis a user may come onto the call list, under the conditions of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality. The recording of a contribution for a donation or sponsorship does not depend on the call.

(4) Proof of reality does not require the collection of personal documents or the unique citizen identification number. The contribution ledger is kept under a pseudonym; the first and last name, where obtained, is kept in the identity register (Article 31).

(5) The record of the chain of confirmations is a record of personal data; it also covers supervision data (the supervision outcome and, where suspicion is expressed, the subject of suspicion and the reason code). Those data are not available to other users. The establishment of reality is the processing of personal data whose legal basis is the execution of the contractual relationship between the user and the Foundation.

(6) The rules of confirmation and the measures against abuse of the chain of confirmations are established by the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

### Article 31 — Identity register

(1) The Foundation maintains an identity register, that is, a register in which the user's first and last name is kept alongside the account's pseudonym. The identity register serves to give effect to the principle of one person, one account and to verify the ground on which a contribution was recorded. It is kept separately from the contribution ledger.

(2) The first and last name is entered only in the following cases: upon registering a farm and upon joining a farm, upon establishing the receipt of a public donation, upon acquiring the capacity of a cooperative member, upon establishing the receipt of a sponsorship and upon entering the call list. The manner of entry in each case is governed by the relevant rulebook.

(3) One first and last name is kept per account. A first and last name obtained in a later case under paragraph 2 does not replace the one already entered. If it differs, joining a farm, acquiring the capacity of a cooperative member, a sponsorship application and entering the call list are carried out only once the discrepancy has been resolved, while the donation is recorded and the entered name remains. An inaccurate entry is corrected by a complaint under the Terms of Use.

(4) The unique citizen identification number, the identity card number and data from other identity documents are not entered in the identity register, and copies of identity documents are not kept. Where the Foundation compares the first and last name with an identity document or a public register, only the fact that the comparison was carried out is recorded.

(5) The first and last name is not entered in the contribution ledger and is not displayed to other users. It is accessible only to the Foundation's administrator team, to the user themselves, to the person whom a cooperative proposes for work with the cooperative members of that cooperative (Rulebook on Cooperatives) and to the Kolo member who calls a user from the call list in order to get acquainted, to the extent and under the conditions of the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

(6) The first and last name is compared with the names kept alongside other accounts in order to prevent multiple accounts; the comparison discloses to another user neither the name nor the pseudonym of the other account. The entry is not a confirmation or an introduction into the Kolo, never confirms reality and does not change the user's status; reality is confirmed only in the chain of confirmations. The entry is not a condition for proof of reality or for access to system functions, except for participation under paragraph 2.

(7) For a minor user, the identity register is not kept. The first and last name is deleted from the identity register upon the termination of the user's status (Article 33).

### Article 32 — Termination of status and suspension

(1) A user's status terminates by withdrawal, exclusion or death. Withdrawal is the voluntary termination of status, on the basis of the user's electronic declaration, without a notice period. Exclusion is the termination of status by a decision of the Foundation, due to a serious breach of the system rules.

(2) Access to system functions may be temporarily restricted by suspension; during suspension the user retains their status but may not use the functions to which the suspension applies.

(3) The grounds, procedure and mechanics of termination of status and of suspension are governed by the Terms of Use.

### Article 33 — Consequences of termination of status

(1) Upon termination of status, the user's active ZRNO passes into the free state, after which all free ZRNO is written off and returned to the available ZRNO in the Protocol. The write-off of ZRNO upon termination of status does not trigger the recording of POEN pursuant to the accounting coefficient.

(2) The user's POEN records are annulled, and the Protocol's corresponding counter-entry is reduced by the same amount. A negative record arising under Article 17 paragraph 2 is not annulled upon termination of status and is not transferred to the Protocol, but remains attached to the pseudonymised numerical history; otherwise, leaving the system would erase the restitution and the burden would fall on the other users.

(3) No monetary compensation is paid to the user.

(4) Upon termination of status, the pseudonymisation of the user's data is initiated: the email address, voluntarily submitted data and the first and last name from the identity register are deleted, the user's connections in the chain of confirmations are pseudonymised, and the numerical history is retained under a pseudonym that does not point to the name, until the anonymisation referred to in Article 55 paragraph 3. Confirmations and introductions that the user gave remain. The Foundation retains the internal account identifier, so the retained records still constitute personal data.

(5) The numerical history is kept for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims and for preserving the integrity of the ledger on which other users rely, and the dinar records of donations and sponsorship also for compliance with a legal obligation of retention (Privacy Policy).

(6) The handling of the ledger in the event of a user's death is governed in more detail by the Terms of Use.

## VII — Contribution Channels

### Article 34 — Operational contribution

(1) The Foundation, the Upper Kolo or Kolo keepers publish, on behalf of the community, a task to be performed for the common good. A user voluntarily applies for the execution of the task. After execution, the execution is confirmed by Kolo keepers and by a person authorised by the Management Board, before the contribution is recorded, in accordance with the Rulebook on Operational Contribution.

(2) Operational contribution does not establish an employment relationship within the meaning of Article 5 of the Labour Act, nor does it constitute work outside an employment relationship, in particular not a contract for work. A commissioning party, a commissioned performance and remuneration are absent: the task is not commissioned on behalf of the Foundation but published on account of a need of the common good, the contributor decides independently whether to apply and how to carry out the task, and by reason of the completed task the Foundation receives no value whatsoever and acquires no right against the contributor.

(3) A special form of operational contribution is cooperative contribution: organisational units in a cooperative, which the cooperative allocates within the maximum daily amount established according to the number of cooperative members. The execution is confirmed by Kolo keepers, and the amount counts towards the common daily limit. Paragraph 2 applies to cooperative contribution. Details are established by the Rulebook on Operational Contribution and the Rulebook on Cooperatives.

### Article 35 — Founding contribution

(1) Founding contribution encompasses work performed before the platform was opened (system design, protocol development and legal and organisational preparation), which the Protocol subsequently records through a special channel.

(2) The recording of founding contribution is not remuneration for work. A commissioning party, a commissioned performance and remuneration are absent: the work was not performed on the instructions of the Foundation, on that basis the Foundation receives no value whatsoever and acquires no right against the founder, and POEN recorded through this channel does not express the value of working time and cannot be converted into money.

(3) The recording of founding contribution is gradual, in steps of a fixed amount, tied to the cumulative growth of the total number of POEN. When the Protocol has recorded the full amount up to a pre-determined upper limit, the channel closes permanently.

(4) POEN recorded through this channel are entered in the records of the founders within the meaning of the Rulebook on Founding Contribution (the natural persons who performed the work before the platform was opened) and have the same status as all other POEN. Founding contribution does not affect the operational contribution limit.

(5) The upper limit, the amount and schedule of steps and the point at which the channel closes are established by the Rulebook on Founding Contribution.

### Article 36 — Donation

(1) A natural person's donation is a dinar inflow to the Foundation on the basis of a donation agreement. Dinar funds enter the Foundation and do not enter the accounting framework of the system.

(2) Two decisions in that procedure are separate. The receipt of the donation and the person with whose funds it was made are established by the Foundation, by comparing the payer's details with the user's account; the payment intermediary's report of a completed charge does not itself trigger recording.

(3) When the Foundation establishes the receipt of a donation, the Protocol, under the rules of the channel, records the contribution in POEN in the donor's record. The number of POEN is determined by the Protocol automatically, pursuant to the parameters of the Rulebook on Sponsorship and Donations, and is not subject to the judgement of any actor in the system.

(4) The Foundation issues a donation receipt upon the donor's request, in accordance with the law.

(5) Funds that the Foundation receives from public sources, in calls for proposals and from other legal entities that do not submit a sponsorship application are spent for the purpose for which they were given and are not recorded in anyone's POEN record.

### Article 37 — Sponsorship

(1) Sponsorship is realised by donating money to the Foundation or by an agreement on sponsorship in goods and services for the benefit of users. Sponsorship in goods and services is deemed realised when the agreement has been signed and the vouchers issued; the Foundation does not receive or possess the goods and services. The contribution is recorded upon signature because the signature gave rise to the giver's obligation in favour of the voucher holders; it is not an advance against a future handover. If the giver does not perform the obligation, the ledger is corrected under Article 17 paragraph 2 point 7.

(2) The sponsorship contribution is recorded in the record of the user who submitted the sponsorship application on behalf of the sponsor. The Foundation establishes the receipt of sponsorship in money, or that the agreement on sponsorship in goods and services has been concluded and the price list attached; the number of POEN is determined by the Protocol automatically, pursuant to the parameters of the Rulebook on Sponsorship and Donations.

(3) The relationship between the Foundation and the sponsor is governed by a donation agreement. The Foundation maintains a record of the link between the sponsor and the user to whom the contribution is recorded.

### Article 38 — Contribution for a first listing

(1) A user has a contribution of 1,000 POEN recorded for the first listing in which they offer a good or a service and which meets the content minimum established by the Terms of Use. This contribution is recorded at most once per user, regardless of the number of listings posted, and regardless of whether the user is in the Kolo.

(2) The listing is published immediately; the contribution is noted at the moment of posting and is recorded in the Protocol when the Foundation approves the listing. Without the Foundation's approval the contribution is not recorded; a confirmation or introduction of the user into the Kolo and a re-registration in their favour are not grounds for recording.

(3) By approving, the Foundation establishes that the condition is met (that the listing meets the content minimum and represents a genuine offer) and does not decide whether a contribution will be recorded for the user. An approved listing is at the same time one of the forms of contribution upon which the POEN recorded for a confirmation, or an introduction into the Kolo, is entered (Rulebook on Proof of Reality).

(4) If the listing is removed for a breach of the Terms of Use before the contribution has been recorded, the noted contribution is annulled. A contribution already recorded is not annulled by the removal of the listing.

(5) The approval procedure is governed by the Terms of Use.

### Article 39 — Farm contribution

(1) For the purposes of this Rulebook, a farm is a household that produces food or agricultural products, regardless of whether it is entered in the Register of Agricultural Holdings. A farm is not a user of the system and has no record in the Protocol.

(2) When the Foundation approves a farm, or the joining of a farm, a contribution is noted for the farm's members in the order in which they joined, and it is recorded under the conditions of the Rulebook on Farms. By approving, the Foundation establishes that the conditions are met and does not decide whether a contribution will be recorded for the member.

(3) When an approved farm terminates, on any ground of termination, the noted contribution is annulled, and the recorded contribution is written off on the ground under Article 17 paragraph 2 point 6; each affected person is notified of the write-off and of the number of POEN written off for them.

(4) The amounts of the contribution, the number of members for whom the contribution is noted, the conditions of recording, the grounds for termination of a farm and the procedure in the event of a false declaration are established by the Rulebook on Farms.

## VIII — System Governance

### Article 40 — The Upper Kolo as a body of the Foundation

The Upper Kolo is a special body of the Foundation established by this Rulebook, on the basis of the Management Board's power under the Statute to establish, by rulebook, advisory and other bodies that participate in the preparation of decisions, as well as mechanisms for gathering the opinions of users. It gives effect to the Foundation's objective recorded in the Statute: the development of economic models based on democratic decision-making.

### Article 41 — Governance phases

(1) System governance proceeds in two phases. Phase 1 lasts until the total number of POEN recorded in the system reaches 1,000,000. In Phase 2, the rules of the Protocol are established by a decision of the Upper Kolo, which the Management Board adopts by its own act (Article 46); the Foundation retains its service and executive role.

(2) The transition to Phase 2 occurs automatically, at the end of the accounting period in which the threshold has been reached, and does not require a special decision. ZRNO inscription is open from the next accounting period (Article 22); inscribed ZRNO may be activated from the period following the inscription, and the Upper Kolo is determined for the first time in the period following the first activation (Article 43). The transition is one-way: a later decrease in the total number of POEN does not return the system to Phase 1.

(3) The Management Board without delay adopts and publishes an act recording that the conditions for the transition have been met. That act is declaratory: the transition occurs upon reaching the threshold, not upon the adoption of the act.

### Article 42 — Phase 1

(1) In Phase 1, the system rules are established by the Management Board through acts of the Foundation; an amendment may also be proposed by the founder of the Foundation.

(2) Even in Phase 1, an amendment may neither change the principles and limits of the common good (Article 10) nor enable the appropriation of the common good.

### Article 43 — Composition of the Upper Kolo

(1) The Upper Kolo is composed of the Kolo keepers who have activated ZRNO, including the founders. The composition of the Upper Kolo is not established by appointment: a Kolo keeper enters the Upper Kolo by activating ZRNO, and leaves it when none of their ZRNO is active any longer, or when their confirmation of reality falls (Article 29). A member without confirmation to whom ZRNO is inscribed is not part of the composition of the Upper Kolo until confirmed. The composition of the Upper Kolo and the voting power of each Kolo keeper are determined from the Protocol's ledger separately for each accounting period and apply only during that period.

(2) The Upper Kolo establishes by vote proposals on the rules of the Protocol, on the introduction and abolition of modules and on all matters affecting the common good, except matters excluded from its competence under this Rulebook. A proposal on rules may be submitted by any participant; it is voted on when supported by at least one Kolo keeper with activated ZRNO, under the procedure set out in the Rulebook on the Upper Kolo, and the Foundation does not decide whether the proposal will be voted on. An adopted proposal is a decision of the Upper Kolo within the meaning of this Rulebook; it is addressed to the Management Board, which adopts it by its own act in the manner established by Article 46.

(3) The Upper Kolo is not a legal entity and is not among the organs of the Foundation established by the Statute; the organs of the Foundation are the Management Board and the Director. The Upper Kolo does not represent the Foundation, does not dispose of its property and does not assume obligations on its behalf. A decision of the Upper Kolo does not directly create rights or obligations towards third parties. The Upper Kolo does not govern the Foundation.

### Article 44 — Voting and delegation

(1) The Upper Kolo decides by square-root voting: the voting power of a Kolo keeper equals the integer value of the square root of the number of their active ZRNO, rounded down. Free ZRNO does not confer voting power; a Kolo keeper who wishes to vote must activate ZRNO, thereby relinquishing the possibility of write-off until they return it to the free state. ZRNO is not used up by voting. Voting power arises from activated ZRNO, not from the number of POEN and not from dinar donations.

(2) A Kolo keeper may delegate their voting power to another Kolo keeper; a member without confirmation has no vote, and therefore does not delegate.

(3) The types of decisions, the majority, the procedure of proposing, support and voting and the rules of delegation are established by the Rulebook on the Upper Kolo.

### Article 45 — Limits of the Upper Kolo's decision-making

A decision of the Upper Kolo may neither change the principles and limits of the common good (Article 10) nor circumvent the statutory obligations of the Management Board.

### Article 46 — Implementation of Upper Kolo decisions

(1) In Phase 2, the Foundation retains its service role: it provides and maintains the infrastructure, represents the system in legal transactions and implements the decisions of the Upper Kolo. The Foundation does not decide on the rules of the Protocol at its own discretion, but acts upon the decision of the Upper Kolo, within the limits of the law and the Statute and with retention of the Management Board's statutory responsibilities.

(2) A decision of the Upper Kolo is adopted and implemented by the Management Board through the adoption of a corresponding act. By this Rulebook the Management Board undertakes to adopt that act without delay and not to assess the expediency of the decision in doing so.

(3) The Management Board may refuse to implement a decision only if it is contrary to law, contrary to the Statute of the Foundation, or directed at a matter excluded from the competence of the Upper Kolo under Article 45. No other grounds for refusal exist and none may be established.

(4) A refusal must be reasoned by reference to a specific ground under paragraph 3 and is published alongside the decision to which it relates. The Upper Kolo may amend the decision so as to remove the established ground and decide upon it again.

(5) In the act by which it adopts or refuses a decision of the Upper Kolo, as well as in the response to a recommendation, the Management Board states which of its members voted in the Upper Kolo on that decision or recommendation.

(6) With regard to the allocation of dinar funds, the Upper Kolo issues recommendations to the Management Board. The Management Board considers the recommendations and applies them within its statutory powers under the Law on Endowments and Foundations, with an obligation to give a reasoned response to each recommendation. A decision of the Upper Kolo, to the extent that it requires the spending of the Foundation's dinar funds, has the effect of a recommendation.

### Article 47 — Projects and collective procurement

(1) The Foundation's dinar funds in excess of operational costs may be allocated to projects for the benefit of the community.

(2) Collective procurement is a project by which the Foundation acquires goods from a third party using dinar funds, and participants collect them in equal shares with the annulment of POEN records upon use (Article 18). Only a good that users cannot obtain within the system is acquired through collective procurement.

(3) The Upper Kolo decides on a project and on all of its parameters. The decision concerns the allocation of dinar funds and therefore has the nature of a recommendation to the Management Board within the meaning of Article 46 paragraph 6. Until the Upper Kolo is constituted in Phase 2, the decision is made by the Management Board, following the same procedure and with the same publication.

(4) In relation to the third party from which the goods are acquired, the Foundation is the buyer and the contracting party. This provision does not affect the rule that the Foundation is not a party to an exchange between users.

(5) The funds available for projects, the proposing and selection of the object of procurement, the offers and the calculation, the application, the order and collection, as well as the amount and moment of annulment of records, are established by the Rulebook on Projects and Collective Procurement.

### Article 48 — Dissolution of the Foundation

(1) In the event of the dissolution of the Foundation, the common good does not cease to exist. The software and content remain available under the licence terms.

(2) The Management Board, in accordance with the Statute, transfers the infrastructure and the ledger to the person to whom the remaining assets of the Foundation are allocated, and only to a person that accepts the principles and limits of the common good and assumes the obligation of safeguarding it. Users are notified of the transfer before it is carried out.

## IX — Parts of the System

### Article 49 — Modules

(1) New modules may be introduced by decision of the Foundation in Phase 1, or of the Upper Kolo in Phase 2, provided that they do not undermine the principles and limits of the common good (Article 10); a decision of the Upper Kolo is implemented under Article 46.

(2) If a module introduces new processing of personal data, the act introducing it is adopted following a prior update of the data protection impact assessment.

(3) This Rulebook provides for two modules, which are introduced under paragraph 1: inter-cooperative logistics, which connects cooperatives of different places (exchange and issuing of goods between cooperatives, transport and support for founding a cooperative in a new place), and expansion beyond the Republic of Serbia (Article 53).

### Article 50 — Cooperatives

(1) A cooperative in the system serves to organise exchange, the distribution of goods, joint tasks and the use of shared equipment in the place in which it operates; the reaching of thresholds of the number of cooperative members is recorded in POEN in the cooperative's record.

(2) The relationship between the Foundation and a cooperative is governed by a cooperation agreement; the cooperative retains full autonomy as an independent legal entity and its own obligations under the Law on Cooperatives, and does not become the owner of any part of the common good.

(3) POEN is re-registered from and to the cooperative's record only on a ground established by the Rulebook on Cooperatives; a re-registration without a ground is not permitted. The amount of a re-registration is determined by the cooperative, through its organs; the Foundation does not determine, recommend or approve it.

(4) From the termination of the cooperation agreement, or from the initiation of proceedings for deletion of the cooperative from the register, no re-registration is made from or to the cooperative's record. Upon the termination of the cooperative's participation in the system, the cooperative's record is closed, and the POEN recorded in it is annulled by a counter-entry in favour of the Protocol. The annulment covers only the number of POEN present in the record, so the cooperative's record thereby does not pass into a negative value, and the total number of recorded POEN decreases.

(5) A cooperative assists in the amicable resolution of disputes between users from the place in which it operates, through its organs. In doing so, the cooperative does not make a decision binding on the ledger and does not become a party to the exchange, and the data needed for the dispute are provided to it by the parties themselves; the Foundation does not provide the cooperative with data about users. Where there is no cooperative, assistance in amicable resolution is provided by the Foundation. The right to a court remains. The procedure is governed by the Terms of Use and the Rulebook on Cooperatives.

(6) A cooperative helps users who find the platform difficult to use it; the account remains theirs and no one acts on their behalf.

(7) The conditions of participation, cooperative members, growth thresholds, grounds for entries in the record, distribution of goods and cooperative contribution are established by the Rulebook on Cooperatives.

### Article 51 — Support programmes

(1) Support programmes are a mechanism for the automatic recording of POEN for groups of participants whose structural participation in the common good the Protocol recognises even though it is not expressed through individual activities. New records increase the total number of recorded POEN and thereby change the accounting coefficient for all users.

(2) Automatic recording in support programmes is not remuneration: it pays for no work, service or other performance, nor is anything required of the support recipient in return towards the Foundation. The programmes are carried out in pursuit of the objectives of the Foundation laid down in Article 6 of the Statute, in particular social protection and solidarity support for vulnerable social groups and support for the voluntary association of citizens for mutual aid, and they enable the support recipient to participate in the system on a more equal footing. The recording contains no promise of a future service or other performance, so the record is not a claim either against the Foundation or against another user.

(3) Support programmes require the processing of special categories of personal data. The legal basis for that processing is the explicit consent of the support recipient, which may be withdrawn at any time, with the consequence of ceasing the automatic recording of POEN.

(4) The programmes, the conditions, the confirmation that the conditions are met and the recording parameters are established by the Rulebook on Support Programmes; new programmes are introduced by the Foundation in Phase 1, or by the Upper Kolo in Phase 2.

### Article 52 — Children

(1) A minor user participates in the system under a special regime of rights, restrictions and protection, with additional restrictions for persons under 15 years of age in accordance with Article 16 of the Law on Personal Data Protection. Every rule of the children's space is interpreted and applied in the best interests of the child.

(2) A minor user may not inscribe ZRNO or participate in governance through the Upper Kolo until the age of 18. The child participates in shaping the children's space through proposals, under the Rulebook on the Participation of Children.

(3) The child is notified of every write-off of a contribution from the children's space, together with the ground and the amount.

(4) Access, the scope of activities, the relationship with the parent, the contribution from the children's space and the protection measures are established by the Rulebook on the Participation of Children.

### Article 53 — Expansion beyond the Republic of Serbia

(1) The expansion of the system beyond the Republic of Serbia is a module decided on by the Upper Kolo. Even then, the system has one Protocol and one common-good ledger.

(2) The prerequisites are a stable system with an active Upper Kolo and a legal analysis of the target jurisdiction, including regulations on data protection, digital assets, payment services, foundations, cooperatives and taxes.

## X — Protection of Personal Data

### Article 54 — Pseudonymity

(1) Data protection in the system is based on data protection by design and by default. The controller is the Foundation; processing is governed in more detail by the Privacy Policy, the Records of Processing Activities and the Data Protection Impact Assessment.

(2) Records in the ledger are tied to pseudonyms, not to personal names. The first and last name is kept only in the identity register (Article 31), separately from the contribution ledger, and is not displayed to other users except in the cases under Article 31 paragraph 5. Pseudonymity is not anonymity: pseudonymised data remain personal data because they can, with additional information, be linked to an identified person.

### Article 55 — Deletion and integrity of the ledger

(1) The user's right to erasure of personal data and the integrity of the common-good ledger are reconciled by the separation of identification and accounting data (Article 33).

(2) The amount, parties, time and type of a record are not changed; a correction is made by a counter-entry. A description of a record that contains personal data is deleted when the basis for its processing ceases, upon withdrawal of consent or upon termination of the user's status; the record remains.

(3) The numerical history remains permanently in the ledger. Upon the expiry of 10 years from the termination of the user's status, the pseudonym attached to their records is replaced with a designation that does not point to the person even indirectly, so that from then on the records are not personal data.

### Article 56 — Availability of the contribution ledger

(1) The contribution ledger is available to participants in pseudonymous form. Every participant may verify the records in the ledger and the consistency of the accounting framework.

(2) Visitors see general indicators, while users who are not in the Kolo additionally see the amounts and timestamps of transactions, without the pseudonyms of the parties and without the state of the records. Exceptionally, a user sees the list of donations, in which the donor of a public donation is stated by first and last name, together with the amount and date, without the pseudonym (Rulebook on Sponsorship and Donations).

(3) The availability under paragraphs 1 and 2 does not encompass the record of the chain of confirmations, the identity register, voluntarily entered data or special categories of data. The scope of availability is established in more detail by the Privacy Policy.

(4) A user may at all times see their own records and their own noted contribution. A noted contribution is not visible to other users and does not enter public aggregates until it is recorded.

(5) The Protocol calculates from the ledger and states aggregate indicators of the state of the system and of the concentration of recorded POEN, inscribed ZRNO and voting power, over accounts whose status has not terminated. An indicator is not stated when it covers fewer than five persons, contains no pseudonym and does not concern re-registrations between users. The indicators produce no legal effect. Children are counted separately and are not included in the concentration indicators.

## XI — Final Provisions

### Article 57 — Amendments to the Rulebook

(1) This Rulebook is amended by the Management Board, in Phase 1 under Article 42, and in Phase 2 on the basis of a decision of the Upper Kolo, under Article 46 and the Rulebook on the Hierarchy of Acts.

(2) An amendment is published to users before it enters into force, under the procedure for amending acts established by the Terms of Use.

### Article 58 — Start of the system (transitional provision)

(1) When the system is put into operation, there are no Kolo members who could confirm the reality of the first users. For this reason, the first Kolo keepers, the founders, are established directly: the founders are the initial Kolo keepers designated under the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

(2) The founders are exempt, as a one-time event of the start of the system, from the rules of the chain of confirmations that presuppose that someone confirmed them before them. They inscribe ZRNO under Article 22. They constitute the origin of the chain of confirmations; their confirmed status from the establishment of the account and their exemption from supervision are governed by the Rulebook on Proof of Reality.

(3) The founders are not a separate status within the meaning of Article 28: they are Kolo keepers with the attributes established by the Rulebook on Proof of Reality. The capacity of founder does not confer the powers of the Management Board nor make a person a member of the Management Board. Once the system is established, all new Kolo keepers acquire their status by the ordinary route.

(4) The circle of founders is closed and consists of the persons established at the establishment of the system. Later acquisition of the capacity of a member of an organ of the Foundation does not make a person a founder, and new founders are not designated.

### Article 59 — Language

The language of the system is Serbian.

### Article 60 — Entry into force

This Rulebook enters into force on the date of its adoption.
