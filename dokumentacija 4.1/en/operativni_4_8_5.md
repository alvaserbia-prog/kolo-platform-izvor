# Rulebook on Operational Contribution

*This Rulebook governs the operational mechanics of operational contribution — the process of applying for, performing, and verifying off-platform activities whose contribution is recorded in POEN. It is adopted on the basis of Article 36 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System.*

## I — General Provisions

### Article 1

*Subject matter*

This Rulebook governs: the concept and content of a task for the common good, the persons authorised to propose a task, the procedure for a user to apply for execution, the content of an execution plan, the procedure for receiving an application, the procedure for execution and verification, the recording of POEN and distribution within the daily limit, transparency towards system users, and the termination of a contributor's participation.

Terms not defined in this Rulebook shall have the meaning established in the Rulebook on the KOLO System.

### Article 2

*Relationship with the Rulebook on the KOLO System*

This Rulebook elaborates the provisions of Article 36 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System. In the event of any inconsistency, the provisions of the Rulebook on the KOLO System shall prevail.

### Article 3

*Definitions*

The following terms are used in this Rulebook:

**Task for the common good (task)** — an off-platform activity that contributes to the common good, published for execution through the operational contribution channel.

**Task proposer** — a person or body authorised to publish a task on the platform in accordance with Article 4 of this Rulebook.

**Contributor** — a participant who has applied for the execution of a task and whose application has been received in accordance with Chapter IV of this Rulebook.

**Execution plan** — a description of the manner, timing, and steps by which the contributor intends to perform the task.

**Verifier** — a person or body authorised to confirm the execution of a task and, in tasks with approval, to approve the execution plan.

**Unit** — a smaller portion of the work on a task, defined by what is accomplished through it rather than by time, as a rule such that it can be completed within one accounting period; the execution of a unit is subject to individual verification.

**Proposed POEN** — the number of POEN stated by the proposer alongside the task as a weighting coefficient for the distribution of the daily limit in accordance with Chapter VI of this Rulebook.

**Daily limit** — the maximum total POEN that may be recorded on account of operational contribution within one accounting period.

## II — Task for the Common Good

### Article 4

*Publication of a task and the position of the Foundation*

A task is published on account of a need of the common good, for the purpose of contributing to that good, and not for the purpose of performing work for the Foundation or for any other person.

Tasks may be published by:

- a Kolo keeper, independently, following the activation of the Upper Kolo;

- Gornje Kolo (Upper Kolo), by a decision adopted in accordance with the Rulebook on the KOLO System, upon its activation.

In Phase 1, while the Upper Kolo has not yet been established, the function referred to in paragraph 2 of this Article is temporarily exercised by the Foundation, through the Management Board (UO) or a person authorised by it. The Foundation exercises that function on behalf of the community and under the same rules that apply to Kolo keepers, and not in its own name.

The Foundation does not commission a task for itself. It is not the party ordering a work, it is not the beneficiary of the contributor's performance, and it receives no value whatsoever on account of a performed task.

### Article 5

*Content of a task*

A task shall contain at least the following elements:

- the name and description of the task;

- the objective of the task and its contribution to the common good;

- execution criteria on the basis of which it is established that the task has been completed;

- an estimate of the scope of work;

- the division of the task into units: a description of one unit and the number of units;

- the proposed POEN for one unit and, derived from it, the proposed POEN for full execution;

- the application deadline and the execution deadline;

- the number of contributors to be accepted for the task;

- a designation of the task as a task "with approval" if Article 14 of this Rulebook applies;

- a qualification filter if Art. 9 paragraph 3 of this Rulebook applies.

### Article 6

*Proposed POEN*

The task is divided into units. A unit is a smaller portion of the work, defined by what is accomplished through it, as a rule such that it can be completed in one day. The description of a unit states a portion of the work, not a period of time.

The proposed POEN is determined for one unit, according to the estimated scope of work and the nature of the task, and is greater than zero. The proposed POEN for full execution equals the proposed POEN for one unit multiplied by the number of units. The task proposer provides a statement of reasons alongside the proposed amount.

The proposed POEN is not a guaranteed amount that the contributor will receive. The actual ledger entry depends on the distribution of the daily limit in accordance with Chapter VI of this Rulebook.

The proposed POEN is not expressed as a value per unit of working time, nor is it determined according to predetermined values by type of work. The fact that a unit is, as a rule, completed in one day does not make the proposed POEN for a unit the value of a day's work.

### Article 7

*Publication and status of a task*

Tasks are published on the platform.

A task has one of the following statuses: open, in execution, completed, not completed, or withdrawn.

The task content described in Article 5 of this Rulebook and the current status of the task are available to all system users, including those who are not in the Kolo.

### Article 8

*Amendment and withdrawal of a task*

The proposer may amend, supplement, or withdraw a task before the first application has been received, without providing reasons.

After the first application has been received, the task may be amended only with the consent of all contributors who are at that time accepted for the task. Withdrawal of a task in execution is possible only for justified reasons, which shall be stated publicly; units of contributors already confirmed at the time of withdrawal remain recorded in accordance with Chapter VI.

## III — Application for Execution

### Article 9

*Persons eligible to apply*

Any participant — a Kolo member or a member without confirmation — may apply for the execution of a task.

Users may view tasks but may not submit an application for execution.

The task proposer may establish previously completed tasks of a specified type, or other qualification requirements in the task if the nature of the work so requires. The qualification filter must be explained and must be proportionate to the nature of the task.

### Article 10

*Content of an application*

An application is submitted electronically on the platform and contains:

- the applicant's pseudonym;

- the execution plan referred to in Article 11 of this Rulebook;

- a declaration that the applicant is aware of the legal nature of operational contribution as set out in Article 27 of this Rulebook and of the weighted nature of the proposed POEN as set out in Article 22 of this Rulebook.

### Article 11

*Execution plan*

The execution plan contains:

- a description of how the applicant intends to perform the task;

- a timeline for execution, broken down by accounting periods or logical steps;

- the method of proving execution (photograph, report, link, other attachment);

- the indicative order in which the units will be executed, if the task has more than one unit.

The applicant proposes the execution plan independently. The task proposer and the verifier do not interfere with the choice of working method; they express their view solely on whether the proposed plan, or the execution, satisfies the task criteria.

### Article 12

*Availability of applications*

Applications, execution plans, and the pseudonym of the contributor are available to all participants.

This ensures transparency towards the community and the possibility of filing a complaint in accordance with Article 21 of this Rulebook.

## IV — Receipt of Application

### Article 13

*Automatic receipt*

Receipt of an application is an automatic act of the Protocol. A participant who meets the requirements of Article 9 of this Rulebook and any qualification filter set out in the task becomes a contributor upon submitting an application. The task status changes to in execution.

For tasks with multiple intended contributors, the Protocol receives applications in the order of submission until the number specified in the task is reached. Subsequent applications are rejected automatically, with notification to the user.

### Article 14

*Tasks with approval*

The proposer may designate a task as a task "with approval" at the time of publication if there is a justified reason for doing so, in particular where:

- the work is specialised and requires verification of the contributor's qualifications in the specific case;

- the task carries an increased risk to the reputation of the Foundation or the community;

- the task's proposed POEN exceeds a threshold established by a decision of the Management Board (UO) in Phase 1, or by the Upper Kolo in Phase 2.

The Management Board (UO) in Phase 1, or the Upper Kolo in Phase 2, may by its decision transfer an individual task to the approval regime after its publication, with reasons, if one of the grounds referred to in the preceding paragraph exists.

For tasks with approval, an application is not received until the verifier expressly approves the execution plan. The verifier shall express a view within seven days of the submission of the application. Rejection of an application must be reasoned; the user may submit an amended application for the same task.

The verifier approves an application if the execution plan corresponds to the nature and objective of the task, contains all elements listed in Article 11, and does not constitute an obvious abuse of the operational contribution channel.

### Article 15

*Multiple applications for the same task*

Where no qualification filter has been established in the task to distinguish between applicants, places on the task are filled in the order of submission.

Where a qualification filter has been established, the first application that satisfies it is accepted; for tasks with approval, the application that best corresponds to the qualification filter is accepted, with written reasons from the verifier.

### Article 16

*Verifier*

The execution plan in tasks with approval is approved, and execution in all tasks is verified, by a Kolo keeper, in accordance with the operating rules of the Upper Kolo.

In Phase 1, while the Upper Kolo has not been established, verification is carried out by Kolo keepers and a person authorised by the Management Board (UO), on behalf of the community and under the same rules.

Verification establishes solely whether the task has been performed in accordance with the criteria published with the task. The verifier does not accept performance on behalf of the Foundation and acquires no right for the Foundation thereby.

The verifier may not be a contributor to the same task. This restriction admits of no exception.

Nor may the verifier be the person who published the task. In Phase 1, while tasks are published and verification carried out by the Foundation, this restriction does not apply to a person expressly authorised for that purpose by the Management Board.

## V — Execution and Verification

### Article 17

*Execution*

The contributor performs the task in accordance with the execution plan from their application. The contributor determines the method of execution independently; the proposer and verifier do not impose working instructions.

The contributor may amend the execution plan during execution, with prior notice to the verifier. A material amendment to the plan requires re-approval only in tasks with approval.

### Article 18

*Execution of units and verification*

At the end of the accounting period in which they completed one or more units, the contributor submits proof of execution on the platform and states the number of units completed. The contributor does not determine the amount of the proposed POEN: it equals the number of units completed multiplied by the proposed POEN for one unit set out in the task. The total of reported units may not exceed the number of units in the task.

The verifier shall express a view on the execution of units within three accounting periods of the submission of proof. The verifier shall:

- confirm the execution of the units, whereby the proposed POEN for those units is introduced into the distribution for the accounting period in which the confirmation is issued, in accordance with Chapter VI of this Rulebook;

- reject it, with reasons, whereby no POEN is recorded for those units;

- conditionally confirm it, requesting supplementary proof, whereby the decision is deferred pending the supplement.

### Article 19

*Final verification*

Upon notification of the completion of a task, the verifier shall express a view on the entire execution within seven days.

If the task has been executed in accordance with the criteria set out in the task, the verifier confirms execution and the proposed POEN for the units not confirmed until then is introduced into the distribution for the accounting period in which the final confirmation is issued. If the task has not been fully executed, the verifier may confirm partial execution with proportionate distribution, or reject confirmation with reasons.

### Article 20

*Withdrawal by the contributor*

The contributor may withdraw from the task at any time by an electronic declaration on the platform, without reasons and without any consequences other than the absence of a ledger entry for the uncompleted portion of the task.

Verified units up to the time of withdrawal remain in the distribution for the accounting periods in which they were confirmed. The task reverts to open status, unless the task proposer decides to withdraw it in accordance with Article 8 of this Rulebook.

### Article 21

*Complaint*

Any participant may, during the course of a task, submit a reasoned complaint to an application, execution plan, verification of the execution of units, or final verification. The complaint is available to all participants.

The verifier shall express a view on the complaint within seven days. A complaint does not in itself halt execution, but the Management Board (UO) in Phase 1, or the Upper Kolo in Phase 2, may by decision temporarily suspend further verification and distribution under that task until the complaint is resolved.

## VI — Recording of POEN and Distribution

### Article 22

*Weighted nature of the proposed POEN*

The proposed POEN is not a guaranteed amount that the contributor receives upon verification of execution. The proposed POEN is a weighting coefficient on the basis of which the contributor is allocated a proportionate share of the daily limit of the accounting period in which the verification is issued.

All confirmed verifications within an accounting period enter into the distribution for that period. No confirmed verification is deferred to the next period, and none creates an obligation of the system towards the contributor.

### Article 23

*Daily limit*

The maximum total POEN that may be recorded on account of operational contribution within one accounting period equals 10% of the total POEN recorded in the system at the start of that accounting period.

The daily limit is hard and may not be exceeded. The distribution in accordance with Article 24 of this Rulebook ensures that the total of all entries on account of operational contribution in one accounting period does not exceed the daily limit.

### Article 24

*Distribution formula*

At the end of each accounting period the Protocol performs the distribution calculation as a single act for all verifications confirmed in that period.

Let P denote the sum of proposed POEN of all verifications confirmed in the current accounting period, and L the daily limit referred to in Article 23 of this Rulebook. The amount of POEN recorded for an individual confirmed verification is calculated as:

*recorded POEN = proposed POEN × min(1, L/P)*

If P is less than or equal to L, each confirmed verification is credited its full proposed POEN. If P is greater than L, each confirmed verification is credited a proportionate share corresponding to its weight in the total sum of proposed POEN.

Any amount not recorded due to the daily limit being exceeded is not carried over to the next accounting period and does not create an obligation of the system towards the contributor.

### Article 25

*Moment of recording*

The recording of POEN on account of operational contribution is an automatic act of the Protocol executed at the end of the accounting period, simultaneously with the calculation of the accounting coefficient.

POEN are entered in the contributor's record and have the same status as all other POEN in the system — they are non-convertible, have no external value, and confer no property right on the user.

### Article 26

*Variability of limits*

The percentage referred to in Article 23 of this Rulebook is an operational parameter of the system.

Its value is changed by the Management Board (UO) of the Foundation in Phase 1, or by the Upper Kolo in Phase 2. A change takes effect from the first accounting period following the adoption of the decision and is communicated to users in accordance with the Rulebook on the KOLO System.

## VII — Legal Qualification

### Article 27

*Legal nature of operational contribution*

Operational contribution is a contribution to the common good. It does not perform work commissioned by the Foundation or by any other person, and nothing is paid out on account of it.

In the relationship between the person who published the task, the verifier, and the contributor:

- there is no commissioning party — a task is published on account of a need of the common good, and not upon the order of a person who would receive the performance for itself; the community of users of the KOLO system is not a legal person, has no bodies, and cannot be a party to a contract;

- there is no remuneration — no amount is paid out and no good is given to the contributor; POEN recorded following verified execution are entries in the Protocol's ledger of a contribution made, with no external property value;

- there is no subordination — the contributor independently decides whether to apply, independently proposes the method of execution, independently determines the pace of work, and may withdraw at any time without consequences;

- there is no personal obligation to work — accepting a task is voluntary and does not create a legal obligation.

The Foundation is not the party ordering a work and is not the beneficiary of the contributor's performance. On account of a performed task it receives no value whatsoever and acquires no right against the contributor.

The result of an operational contribution enters the common good under the licences set out in Chapter II of the Rulebook on the KOLO System — software under the AGPL-3.0 licence, content under the CC BY-SA 4.0 licence. The Foundation acquires no exclusive right to the result on that basis; the result is available to all persons on the same terms, and the Foundation uses it on the same basis as any other person.

By virtue of the characteristics set out in paragraphs 2 and 3 of this Article, operational contribution does not establish an employment relationship within the meaning of Article 5 of the Labour Act, nor does it constitute work outside an employment relationship — in particular not a contract for work (ugovor o delu) — because a commissioning party, a commissioned performance, and remuneration are all absent.

POEN recorded on account of operational contribution do not constitute remuneration within the meaning of labour regulations or of payment services regulations. By the recording the performer receives neither money nor goods: nothing passes from the Foundation's assets into their assets, the record so created cannot be turned into money, assigned or collected, and on that basis the Foundation makes no payment and determines no amount expressed in money (Article 13 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System).

The proposed POEN is not agreed-upon consideration for work, either for the whole task or for a unit — it is a weighting coefficient in the distribution of the shared daily limit, whose actual realisation depends on the state of the system and total demand in the accounting period, in accordance with Chapter VI of this Rulebook. The number of POEN is not a price of work and need not stand in proportion to the value that the same work would have on the market.

## VIII — Transparency

### Article 28

*Data available to all users*

The following data on operational contribution are available to all system users, including those who are not in the Kolo:

- published tasks with all elements listed in Article 5 of this Rulebook;

- the current status of each task.

### Article 29

*Data available to participants*

The following data are available exclusively to participants:

- applications and execution plans;

- the pseudonym of the contributor and the verifier;

- the verifier's decisions on receipt of an application in tasks with approval, on the execution of units, and on final execution, with reasons;

- POEN recorded per task and per contributor;

- complaints and their outcomes;

- the current usage of the daily limit and the distribution coefficient for the current accounting period, as well as historical distribution data for previous accounting periods.

### Article 30

*Pseudonymity*

Data on the contributor and the verifier are published under their pseudonyms in the system. The personal data of the contributor and the verifier are not published through the operational contribution channel.

## IX — Final Provisions

### Article 31

*Amendments to the Rulebook*

This Rulebook is adopted and amended by the Management Board (UO) of the KOLO Foundation, under the procedure established in the Rulebook on the KOLO System.

### Article 32

*Entry into force*

This Rulebook enters into force on the date of its adoption by the Management Board (UO) of the KOLO Foundation.

A task published before this version of the Rulebook entered into force, without division into units, is executed until its closure under the rules in force at the time of its publication.
