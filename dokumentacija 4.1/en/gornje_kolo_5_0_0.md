# Rulebook on the Upper Kolo

## I — General Provisions

### Article 1 — Subject matter

(1) This Rulebook governs, in accordance with Article 44 paragraph 3 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System, the determination of the composition and voting power of the Upper Kolo, the types of decisions and the manner of decision-making, the procedure of proposing, support and voting, the delegation of voting power, the implementation of decisions and the decision register.

(2) Terms from the Rulebook on the KOLO System have the same meaning in this Rulebook.

### Article 2 — Position of the Upper Kolo

The Upper Kolo is a body of the Foundation established by Article 40 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System, on the basis of the Management Board's power under the Statute. It is not an organ of the Foundation, and its decision produces effect only in the manner referred to in Article 17 (Article 43 paragraph 3 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System).

### Article 3 — Application and transition to Phase 2

(1) The transition to Phase 2 and the order of opening ZRNO inscription, activating ZRNO and the first determination of the Upper Kolo are established by Article 41 paragraph 2 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System.

(2) The provisions on decision-making, procedure and delegation (Chapters III–V) apply from the first determination of the Upper Kolo.

(3) The rules of the Protocol existing at the establishment of the Upper Kolo remain in force until they are amended under this Rulebook.

## II — Composition and Voting Power

### Article 4 — Determination of the composition

(1) The composition of the Upper Kolo (Article 43 paragraph 1 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System) and the voting power of each of its members are determined by the Protocol from the ledger, separately for each accounting period (Article 26 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System). The determined composition and voting power apply only during that period.

(2) The composition is not established by appointment; there is no term of office and no removal of a member of the Upper Kolo takes place.

(3) A member without confirmation and a user who has ZRNO inscribed are not part of the composition of the Upper Kolo, have no voting power and do not delegate it.

(4) From the determined composition, the Protocol states aggregate indicators of the concentration of voting power and of delegation (Article 56 paragraph 5 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System).

### Article 5 — Voting power

(1) The voting power of a Kolo keeper referred to in Article 44 paragraph 1 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System is calculated as ⌊√a⌋, where a is the number of their active ZRNO in the accounting period.

(2) Voting power and delegation do not change during an accounting period. The activation of ZRNO, its return to the free state, inscription, write-off, delegation and revocation of delegation take effect from the next accounting period (Article 23 paragraph 5 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System and Article 16).

## III — Decision-Making

### Article 6 — Types of decisions

(1) The Upper Kolo determines by vote:

1) a decision on the rules of the Protocol and on other matters referred to in Article 43 paragraph 2 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System;

2) a recommendation to the Management Board on the allocation of the Foundation's dinar funds (Article 46 paragraph 6 and Article 47 paragraph 3 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System);

3) a choice among several offered options, where so prescribed by a separate rulebook (Article 8).

(2) The type of decision is determined according to the subject of the decision, not according to how the proposer has designated it. A proposal on the allocation of dinar funds is always a recommendation.

(3) A proposal that would change the principles and limits of the common good (Article 10 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System) or that is directed at a matter excluded from the competence of the Upper Kolo (Article 45 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System) is inadmissible and is not put to a vote.

### Article 7 — Majority

(1) A decision or recommendation is adopted when the sum of the voting power of votes cast "for" is greater than the sum of the voting power of votes cast "against". No quorum is required.

(2) When the sums are equal, the proposal is not adopted and the existing state of affairs remains in force.

### Article 8 — Selective vote

(1) Where a decision is made by choosing among several offered options, the vote is selective: a Kolo keeper casts a vote for one option, and the option with the highest sum of the voting power of the votes cast is selected. The separate rulebook under which the selective vote is conducted establishes the manner in which the list of options is compiled.

(2) A tied result is resolved by the criteria from that separate rulebook. If there are none, no selection is made and the vote is repeated.

## IV — Procedure

### Article 9 — Proposal

(1) A proposal may be submitted by any participant (Article 43 paragraph 2 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System). A proposal contains a title and a statement of reasons sufficiently specific to enable decision-making.

(2) A participant may have at most one proposal on which the procedure is still ongoing.

(3) A proposal is submitted during an accounting period and is available to users from then on. The proposer is stated by pseudonym, which only participants see.

### Article 10 — Support of Kolo keepers

(1) During the accounting period following the submission of a proposal, Kolo keepers who are part of the composition of the Upper Kolo may support the proposal. A proposal goes to a vote when at least one Kolo keeper has supported it by the end of that period. A proposal submitted by a Kolo keeper who is part of the composition of the Upper Kolo is deemed supported by its submission.

(2) Support is not a vote and does not oblige the Kolo keeper to vote "for". Support is not delegated.

(3) The Foundation does not decide whether a proposal will be voted on. An inadmissible proposal (Article 6 paragraph 3) is not put to a vote even when it is supported, and the reason is entered in the decision register.

(4) A proposal that no one supports by the end of the period referred to in paragraph 1 is closed and entered in the decision register as a proposal on which no vote was held.

(5) The number of supports is available to users, and the pseudonyms of the Kolo keepers who supported the proposal only to participants.

### Article 11 — Duration of voting

(1) A supported proposal is voted on throughout the entire accounting period following the support period. The proposer does not set a deadline.

(2) Voting closes automatically at the end of that period; after closure, a vote can be neither cast nor changed.

### Article 12 — Vote

(1) A vote is cast by a Kolo keeper who is part of the composition of the Upper Kolo, by declaring "for" or "against", or by choosing one option (Article 8).

(2) A vote carries the voting power of the accounting period in which it is cast. While voting is open, a Kolo keeper may change their vote.

(3) A Kolo keeper who has delegated their voting power does not vote personally while the delegation is in force.

### Article 13 — Re-proposing

A proposal that was not adopted, as well as a proposal on which no vote was held because no one supported it, of the same or substantially identical content may not be submitted again before the expiry of 30 days from the close of the preceding procedure.

## V — Delegation

### Article 14 — Delegation

(1) A Kolo keeper may delegate their voting power to another Kolo keeper. Delegation is general: the delegate votes on behalf of the delegator on all matters while the delegation is in force.

(2) Delegated voting power is added to the delegate's own voting power.

(3) Delegation produces no effect while the delegator or the delegate is not a Kolo keeper.

### Article 15 — Chain and circle

(1) When the delegate also delegates, the delegated voting power passes along the chain to the Kolo keeper who votes personally.

(2) A delegation that would form a circle, in which no one votes personally, produces no effect, and the voting power within the circle is not counted for anyone.

### Article 16 — Effect and revocation

A delegation may be revoked at any time. A delegation, a revocation and a new delegation produce effect from the beginning of the next accounting period.

## VI — Implementation of Decisions

### Article 17 — Implementation of a decision

(1) An adopted decision of the Upper Kolo is addressed to the Management Board. The Management Board adopts the act by which the decision is adopted and implemented without delay and in doing so does not assess the expediency of the decision; it may refuse it only on the grounds referred to in Article 46 paragraph 3 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System, with a statement of reasons.

(2) The designation of the act by which the decision was implemented, or the ground and statement of reasons for the refusal, are entered in the decision register (Article 19).

### Article 18 — Response to a recommendation

To an adopted recommendation, the Management Board gives a reasoned response by which it accepts or does not accept it (Article 46 paragraph 6 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System). The response is entered in the decision register.

## VII — Decision Register

### Article 19 — Decision register

(1) The Protocol maintains a decision register. For each closed proposal, the register contains the title and statement of reasons, the proposer, the number of supports, the type of decision, the time of voting, the outcome with the voting-power totals, the number of Kolo keepers who voted and the smallest number of Kolo keepers whose voting power makes up the majority of the votes cast, and the data referred to in Articles 17 and 18. For a proposal on which no vote was held, the register contains the title, the proposer and the reason.

(2) Proposals, supports, voting-power totals during voting and the decision register are available to users. The pseudonyms of the proposer and of the Kolo keepers who supported the proposal are available only to participants (Article 56 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System). An individual vote is not published, except in the case referred to in Article 46 paragraph 5 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System.

(3) Entries in the register are not subsequently altered.

## VIII — Final Provisions

### Article 20 — Amendment of the Rulebook

In Phase 2, the content of an amendment to this Rulebook is determined by a decision of the Upper Kolo, and the amendment is adopted by the Foundation's Management Board in the manner referred to in Article 17 (Article 12 paragraph 4 of the Rulebook on the Hierarchy of Acts). An amendment may not be contrary to the Rulebook on the KOLO System nor change the principles and limits of the common good (Article 10 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System).

### Article 21 — Entry into force

This Rulebook enters into force on the date of its adoption.
