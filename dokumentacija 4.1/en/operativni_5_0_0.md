# Rulebook on Operational Contribution

## I — General provisions

### Article 1 — Subject matter

(1) This Rulebook governs, in accordance with Article 34 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System, the task for the common good, the application and acceptance of a contributor, execution and confirmation of completion, the recording of POEN within the daily limit, cooperative contribution, the legal nature of operational contribution, and the availability of data on it.

(2) Terms from the Rulebook on the KOLO System have the same meaning in this Rulebook.

### Article 2 — Definitions

The following terms are used in this Rulebook:

1) **task**: an off-platform activity that contributes to the common good, published for execution through the operational contribution channel;

2) **proposer**: the person or body that published the task (Article 4);

3) **contributor**: a participant whose application for the execution of a task has been accepted (Articles 11 and 12), or to whom a cooperative has assigned an organisational unit (Article 23);

4) **execution plan**: a description of the manner, timing, and steps by which the contributor intends to perform the task;

5) **confirming person**: a Kolo keeper, or a person authorised by the Management Board, who confirms the completion of a task and, in a task with approval, approves the execution plan (Article 13);

6) **unit**: a smaller portion of the work on a task, defined by what is accomplished through it rather than by time, as a rule such that it can be completed within one accounting period; the execution of a unit is subject to a separate confirmation;

7) **proposed POEN**: the number of POEN that the proposer establishes alongside the task as a weighting coefficient for the distribution of the daily limit (Articles 6 and 19);

8) **daily limit**: the common daily limit of operational contribution and support programmes under Article 20.

### Article 3 — Decisions of the Management Board and of the Upper Kolo

Decisions that this Rulebook assigns to the Management Board in Phase 1 are adopted in Phase 2 by the Upper Kolo, in the manner set out in Article 46 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System.

## II — Task

### Article 4 — Purpose of a task and who publishes it

(1) A task is published on account of a need of the common good, for the purpose of contributing to that good, and not for the purpose of performing work for the Foundation or for any other person.

(2) In Phase 2 a task is published by a Kolo keeper independently or by the Upper Kolo by a decision adopted in accordance with the Rulebook on the KOLO System.

(3) In Phase 1 a task is published by the Foundation, through the Management Board or a person authorised by it. The Foundation publishes a task on behalf of the community and under the same rules that apply to Kolo keepers, and not in its own name.

### Article 5 — Content of a task

A task contains at least:

1) the name and description of the task;

2) the objective of the task and its contribution to the common good;

3) execution criteria on the basis of which it is established that the task has been completed;

4) an estimate of the scope of work;

5) the division of the task into units: a description of one unit and the number of units;

6) the proposed POEN for one unit and, derived from it, the proposed POEN for full execution, with a statement of reasons;

7) the application deadline and the execution deadline;

8) the number of contributors to be accepted for the task;

9) a designation of the task as a task "with approval", if Article 12 applies;

10) a qualification filter, if Article 9 paragraph 3 applies.

### Article 6 — Units and proposed POEN

(1) The task is divided into units. The description of a unit states a portion of the work, not a period of time.

(2) The proposed POEN is determined for one unit, according to the estimated scope of work and the nature of the task, and is greater than zero. The proposed POEN for full execution equals the proposed POEN for one unit multiplied by the number of units.

(3) The proposed POEN is not expressed as a value per unit of working time, nor is it determined according to predetermined values by type of work. The fact that a unit is, as a rule, completed within one accounting period does not make the proposed POEN for a unit the value of a day's work.

### Article 7 — Publication and status of a task

(1) A task is published on the platform.

(2) A task has one of the following statuses: open, in execution, completed, not completed, or withdrawn.

### Article 8 — Amendment and withdrawal of a task

(1) The proposer may amend, supplement, or withdraw a task before the first application has been accepted, without providing reasons.

(2) After the first application has been accepted, the task may be amended only with the consent of all contributors accepted at that time. A task in execution may be withdrawn only for justified reasons, which are stated publicly; units confirmed up to the withdrawal remain in the distribution for the accounting periods in which they were confirmed.

## III — Application and acceptance

### Article 9 — Who may apply

(1) Any participant may apply for the execution of a task.

(2) A user who is not in the Kolo may view tasks but may not submit an application.

(3) The proposer may set as a condition in the task previously completed tasks of a specified type or another qualification requirement, if the nature of the work so requires. The qualification filter must be reasoned and proportionate to the nature of the task.

### Article 10 — Application and execution plan

(1) An application is submitted electronically on the platform and contains:

1) the applicant's pseudonym;

2) the execution plan;

3) a declaration that the applicant is aware of the legal nature of operational contribution (Article 24) and of the weighted nature of the proposed POEN (Article 19).

(2) The execution plan contains:

1) a description of how the applicant intends to perform the task;

2) a timeline for execution, broken down by accounting periods or steps;

3) the method of proving execution (photograph, report, link, or other attachment);

4) the indicative order in which the units will be executed, if the task has more than one unit.

(3) The applicant proposes the execution plan independently. The proposer and the confirming person do not interfere with the choice of working method; they express their view solely on whether the plan, or the execution, satisfies the criteria of the task.

### Article 11 — Acceptance of an application

(1) Acceptance of an application is an automatic act of the Protocol. A participant who meets the requirements of Article 9 becomes a contributor upon submitting an application, and the task changes to the status "in execution".

(2) Applications are accepted in the order of submission, up to the number of contributors specified in the task; where a qualification filter has been established, the first application that satisfies it is accepted. Later applications are rejected automatically, with notification to the applicant.

### Article 12 — Task with approval

(1) The proposer may, at the time of publication, designate a task as a task "with approval" if there is a justified reason for doing so, in particular where it concerns:

1) specialised work that requires verification of the contributor's qualifications in the specific case;

2) a task that carries an increased risk to the reputation of the Foundation or the community;

3) a task whose proposed POEN exceeds a threshold established by a decision of the Management Board.

(2) The Management Board may by decision, with reasons, transfer a task to the approval regime also after its publication, if one of the grounds under paragraph 1 exists.

(3) In a task with approval, an application is accepted only when the confirming person expressly approves the execution plan. The confirming person expresses a view within 7 days of the submission of the application.

(4) The confirming person approves the application if the execution plan corresponds to the nature and objective of the task, contains all elements under Article 10 paragraph 2, and does not constitute an obvious abuse of the operational contribution channel. A refusal must be reasoned; the applicant may submit an amended application for the same task.

(5) Where a qualification filter has been established, in a task with approval the application that best corresponds to it is accepted, with written reasons from the confirming person.

### Article 13 — Confirming person and conflict of interest

(1) Completion is confirmed by Kolo keepers and by a person authorised by the Management Board, on behalf of the community; they also approve the execution plan in a task with approval.

(2) The confirmation of completion establishes solely whether the task has been performed in accordance with the criteria published with the task. The confirming person does not accept performance on behalf of the Foundation and acquires no right for the Foundation thereby.

(3) The confirming person may not be a contributor to the same task. This restriction admits of no exception.

(4) Nor may the confirming person be the person who published the task. While tasks are published by the Foundation, this restriction does not apply to a person expressly authorised for that purpose by the Management Board.

## IV — Execution and confirmation of completion

### Article 14 — Execution

(1) The contributor performs the task in accordance with the execution plan from their application. The contributor determines the method of execution independently; the proposer and the confirming person do not impose working instructions on them.

(2) The contributor may amend the execution plan during execution, with prior notice to the confirming person. A material amendment to the plan requires re-approval only in a task with approval.

### Article 15 — Execution of units

(1) For an accounting period in which they completed one or more units, the contributor submits on the platform, once and no later than 3 days after that period, proof of execution and states the number of units completed. The contributor does not determine the amount of the proposed POEN: it equals the number of units completed multiplied by the proposed POEN for one unit. The total of reported units may not exceed the number of units in the task.

(2) The confirming person expresses a view on the execution of units within 3 days of the submission of proof, in that they:

1) confirm the execution, whereby the proposed POEN for those units enters the distribution for the accounting period in which the confirmation is given (Article 21);

2) reject it, with reasons, whereby no POEN is recorded for those units;

3) conditionally confirm it, requesting supplementary proof, whereby the view is deferred pending the supplement.

### Article 16 — Final confirmation

(1) Upon notification of the completion of a task, the confirming person expresses a view on the entire execution within 7 days.

(2) If the task has been executed in accordance with the criteria set out in the task, the confirming person gives the confirmation of completion, and the proposed POEN for the units not confirmed until then enters the distribution for the accounting period in which the final confirmation is given. If the task has not been executed in full, the confirming person may confirm partial execution with proportional distribution, or refuse confirmation with reasons.

### Article 17 — Withdrawal by the contributor

(1) The contributor may withdraw from the task at any time, by an electronic declaration on the platform, without reasons and without any consequences other than the absence of a ledger entry for the uncompleted portion of the task.

(2) Units confirmed up to the withdrawal remain in the distribution for the accounting periods in which they were confirmed. The task reverts to the status "open", unless the proposer withdraws it under Article 8.

### Article 18 — Objection by a participant

(1) Any participant may, while the task is ongoing, submit a reasoned objection to an application, an execution plan, a confirmation of the execution of units, or a final confirmation. The objection is available to all participants.

(2) The confirming person expresses a view on the objection within 7 days. An objection does not in itself halt execution, but the Management Board may by decision temporarily suspend further confirmation and distribution under that task until the objection is decided.

## V — Recording of POEN and distribution

### Article 19 — Weighted nature of the proposed POEN

(1) The proposed POEN is not a guaranteed amount that the contributor receives upon confirmation of completion. It is a weighting coefficient on the basis of which a proportionate share of the daily limit of the accounting period in which the completion was confirmed is recorded for the contributor.

(2) All executions confirmed within an accounting period enter the distribution for that period. No confirmed execution is deferred to the next period, and none creates an obligation of the system towards the contributor.

### Article 20 — Daily limit

(1) The maximum total of POEN that may be recorded on account of operational contribution and all active support programmes together within one accounting period equals 10% of the total number of POEN recorded in the system at the start of that accounting period.

(2) The daily limit is hard and may not be exceeded.

(3) The percentage under paragraph 1 is an operational parameter of the system and is changed by the Management Board by decision. A change takes effect from the first accounting period following the adoption of the decision and is published to users.

### Article 21 — Distribution

(1) At the end of each accounting period the Protocol, by a single calculation, distributes the daily limit across all executions confirmed in that period and the daily amounts of all active support programmes for that period.

(2) Where L is the daily limit, and P the sum of the proposed POEN of all executions confirmed in that period and the daily amounts of all active support programmes for that period, the following is recorded for each confirmed execution:

*recorded POEN = proposed POEN × min(1, L/P)*

The daily amount of each support programme is reduced by the same coefficient.

(3) If P is not greater than L, the full proposed POEN is recorded; if P is greater than L, a proportionate share corresponding to the execution's share in the sum P is recorded. The recorded POEN is rounded down to a whole number.

(4) Any amount not recorded because of the daily limit is not carried over to the next accounting period and does not create an obligation of the system towards the contributor.

### Article 22 — Moment of recording

(1) The recording of POEN on account of operational contribution is an automatic act of the Protocol executed at the end of the accounting period, together with the calculation under Article 26 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System.

(2) POEN are entered in the contributor's record and have the same status as all other POEN in the system.

### Article 23 — Cooperative contribution

(1) Cooperative contribution is a form of operational contribution in which the tasks are organisational units in a cooperative (Article 34 paragraph 3 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System and Article 17 of the Rulebook on Cooperatives).

(2) The organisational units and the contributors to whom they are assigned are determined by the cooperative. A unit is published by the Foundation on behalf of the community, and in Phase 2 by a Kolo keeper, on the proposal of the cooperative. Articles 9, 11, and 12 do not apply to cooperative contribution, because the contributor is determined by the cooperative; the other provisions of this Rulebook apply mutatis mutandis.

(3) The description of a unit states the work done (an organised distribution, a joint action led, and the like), not time (Article 6). The proposed POEN for a unit is determined by the cooperative. The sum of the proposed POEN of the confirmed units of one cooperative in an accounting period may not exceed the maximum amount under Article 17 paragraph 3 of the Rulebook on Cooperatives; the excess does not enter the distribution.

(4) The amount of the unit under Article 17 paragraph 3 of the Rulebook on Cooperatives is established by the Management Board by decision, in POEN, as an operational parameter of the system; a change takes effect from the first following accounting period and is published to users.

(5) The execution of a unit is confirmed only by a Kolo keeper. It is not confirmed by the contributor, nor by the person who assigned the unit or acts on behalf of the cooperative (Article 15 of the Rulebook on Cooperatives).

(6) Confirmed units enter the distribution of the common daily limit under Article 21. Cooperative contribution is not a daily allowance and is not paid by time; Article 24 applies to it.

## VI — Legal nature

### Article 24 — Legal nature of operational contribution

(1) Operational contribution is a contribution to the common good. It does not perform work commissioned by the Foundation or by any other person, and nothing is paid out on account of it.

(2) In the relationship between the person who published the task, the confirming person, and the contributor:

1) there is no commissioning party: a task is published on account of a need of the common good, and not upon the order of a person who would receive the performance for itself; the community of users of the KOLO system is not a legal person, has no organs, and cannot be a party to a contract;

2) there is no remuneration: no amount is paid out and no good is given to the contributor; POEN recorded following confirmed execution are records in the Protocol's ledger of a contribution made, with no external property value;

3) there is no subordination: the contributor independently decides whether to apply, independently proposes the method of execution, independently determines the pace of work, and may withdraw at any time without consequences;

4) there is no personal obligation to work: taking on a task is voluntary and does not create an obligation in the legal sense.

(3) The Foundation does not commission a task for itself. The Foundation is not the party ordering a work and is not the beneficiary of the contributor's performance. On account of a performed task it receives no value whatsoever and acquires no right against the contributor.

(4) The result of an operational contribution enters the common good under the licences set out in Article 5 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System: software under the AGPL-3.0 licence, content under the CC BY-SA 4.0 licence. The Foundation acquires no exclusive right to the result on that basis; the result is available to all persons on the same terms, and the Foundation uses it on the same basis as any other person.

(5) By virtue of the characteristics under paragraphs 2 and 3, operational contribution does not establish an employment relationship within the meaning of Article 5 of the Labour Act, nor does it constitute work outside an employment relationship, in particular not a contract for work (ugovor o delu), because a commissioning party, a commissioned performance, and remuneration are absent.

(6) POEN recorded on account of operational contribution do not constitute remuneration within the meaning of labour regulations or of payment services regulations. By the recording the contributor receives neither money nor goods: nothing passes from the Foundation's assets into their assets, the record so created cannot be turned into money, assigned as a claim, or collected, and on that basis the Foundation makes no payment and determines no amount expressed in money (Article 16 paragraph 8 of the Rulebook on the KOLO System).

(7) The proposed POEN is not agreed consideration for work, either for the whole task or for a unit: it is a weighting coefficient in the distribution of the common daily limit, whose actual realisation depends on the state of the system and total demand in the accounting period (Article 21). The number of POEN is not a price of work and need not stand in proportion to the value that the same work would have on the market.

## VII — Availability of data

### Article 25 — Availability of data

(1) Published tasks with the elements under Article 5 and the current status of each task are available to all users, including those who are not in the Kolo.

(2) The following are available exclusively to participants:

1) applications and execution plans;

2) the pseudonym of the contributor and of the confirming person;

3) the decisions of the confirming person on the acceptance of an application in a task with approval, on the execution of units, and on final execution, with reasons;

4) recorded POEN per task and per contributor;

5) objections and the decisions on them;

6) the current usage of the daily limit and the distribution coefficient for the current accounting period, as well as distribution data for previous accounting periods.

(3) The contributor and the confirming person are displayed only under a pseudonym. Their personal data are not published through the operational contribution channel.

## VIII — Final provisions

### Article 26 — Existing tasks (transitional provision)

A task published before 24 October 2026, without division into units, is executed until its closure under the rules in force at the time of its publication.

### Article 27 — Entry into force

This Rulebook enters into force on the date of its adoption.
